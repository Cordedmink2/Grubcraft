# 26 · Fortifications and Bases

Walls, gates, towers, field works, sieges you can see, and bases that grow into towns, for all four factions. This is the plan Mario approved on 2026-10-22. Mario restarted the work on 2026-10-24 (doc 29, wave 1). Stage 1, towers fire, is built; the rest is the plan. The concept page with sketches is [Grubcraft Fortifications](https://claude.ai/artifact/Vk448ydPoRbbMx3bykGhFa), and its sources are in the project files under `design/fortifications/`.

[21 · Buildings](21-buildings.md) holds the building stats this plan builds on, and [05 · Combat](05-combat.md#structures-in-combat) holds the damage rules. Numbers here are first-pass and move in balance passes, like everywhere else in these docs.

## The aim

Bases should feel like places worth holding. You draw a wall the way you draw an army's line, and it snaps to towers and cliffs. Gates let your own troops through and keep the enemy out. Archers stand on the walls, and towers shoot. When a building falls you watch it come down. Every faction gets all of this in its own way, with one rule each that makes its walls different. Mario's brief: succinct, not overwhelming, but it must work, and it must be easy to build big fortifications.

## Where the game stands (2026-10-22, main at `6a476a5`)

- **Walls:** only the Ember Clans have walls: the Palisade (1×2) and the Gate (1×3), laid one click at a time. Concord, Choir and Brood have no wall buildings.
- **Gates:** a gate is a plain blocking box. `Nav::set_built` blocks a footprint for every side, and the walking grid has no idea of a side, so a gate shuts out its owner's troops too.
- **Towers:** the Watchtower, Tollgate, Bell Tower and Spine Mound carry a `TowerAttack` (range, damage, reload, shots) in `gc_sim::structures`, but no code fires it. Towers do not shoot.
- **Placing:** no rotation (a footprint is anchored at its north-west tile and has no facing), no dragging a line of wall, no snapping.
- **Destruction:** a building brought to nothing is removed at once and its ground freed. Since 2026-10-24 a first pass of "Sieges you can see" draws it: blows on buildings (`Event::Struck`) shake them and knock chips off, shots fly to them, damaged buildings smoke and burn, and a fallen one sinks in dust and leaves rubble that is only a picture ([21 · Buildings](21-buildings.md), "Sieges on screen"). It is drawn with boxes at first, then (2026-10-24, stage 7 part 1) with the shared particle system.
- **Attacking buildings:** regiments ordered to attack a building close in and deal damage (`Command::AttackStructure`). Since 2026-10-24 their soldiers step up to the wall and swing at it, rams draw back and strike, and archers face it and shoot; the blows show on the building (see Destruction).
- **Ground:** heights are fixed after the map loads. Building footprints are the only thing that changes the walking grid in a match (`nav_reblock` updates it in place, at two cells a tile).

## Drawn walls

- **Click and drag** to draw a wall at any angle, and click again to bend it. The length and price show as you draw.
- **Snapping:** a wall end snaps to wall ends, towers, gates and cliff edges within a tile and a half. Shift locks the angle to 15° steps. Otherwise the angle is free.
- **Ring tool:** click a few points round a base and it closes the loop itself, leans on cliffs, and offers a tower every so often and a gate where the workers' path crosses.
- **Blueprints:** a whole plan goes down as a ghost and is paid piece by piece as workers reach it, so a city wall can be planned early and rise over time.
- **Turning:** every other building turns in quarter turns with `R` or the scroll wheel while it is placed.

**How it works.** A wall is a chain of posts at half-tile precision, split into pieces of up to 6 tiles. Each piece is its own structure with its own hit points, so a breach is local. Each piece is stamped into the walking grid as a line one tile thick, so a diagonal wall is smooth on screen and a fine staircase in the grid. Buildings get a facing (0 to 3) that turns the footprint and the model and sets which side units come out of.

**How it works (as built, stage 3 part 1).** It is simpler than planned: a wall is drawn tile by tile, not in half-tile posts. A wall building flagged `Line` is drawn as a straight line from one tile to another, at most 32 tiles in one order (`Command::BuildWall`). The line runs centre to centre and takes the nearest tile at each step along its longer side, so a diagonal is a staircase of tiles meeting at their corners. Each tile is a building of its own, 1×1, with the building's price, hit points and build time, so a breach is one tile and a wall is paid for by the tile. Corner-to-corner tiles still seal the way, because the walking grid never lets a step cut across a corner whose two sides are blocked. On screen each tile is drawn as its share of a slab half a tile thick along the drawn line, so the wall looks straight at any angle. Tiles where the player's own walls or gates already stand are left out, so walls join and cross. If any other tile can't be built (another building, a regiment, ground no building can stand on, or woods and brush, which must be cleared first) the whole line is refused and nothing is paid. The worker that draws it goes to the end nearer it and works along; each time it finishes a tile it goes on to the nearest unfinished tile of its side's walls within 2 tiles (the same line first), and failing that the nearest within 32, so one worker puts up a whole bent wall; if the tile it is heading for is called off or knocked down first, it goes on to the next. A wall can't be laid as a single building, only drawn, and a worker used up on what it builds (the Brood's) can't draw one. A line is refused when the match has no room left for that many buildings. The tool: pick the wall in the build menu, click a start, and each click after that orders the line so far and carries on from its end; right-click or Esc finishes. Shift locks the angle to 15° steps, and ends snap to your own wall ends, gates and towers within a tile and a half (cliff edges aren't snapped to yet). The ghost shows each tile, amber where the ground must be cleared and red where it can't go, with the tile count and price. Only the Ember Palisade is a drawn wall so far (10 Grub, 250 hit points and 4 seconds a tile); the other factions' walls, gates on drawn walls, the ring tool and blueprints come in the next parts.

**How it works (as built, stage 3 part 2).** Every faction now draws a level 1 wall the same way: the Concord's **Bone Fence** (12 Grub, 300 hit points, 4 s a tile), the Choir's **Light Curtain** (10 Grub, 180, 3 s) and the Brood's **Resin Ridge** (8 Grub, 200, 5 s, Living). The Light Curtain (flag `Pass`) is a door in the walking grid like a gate's, but with no setting: once finished it is open to its own side and its allies and shut to everyone else, so Choir troops walk straight through and route through it, and the Choir builds no gates. It needs power to be laid, and a finished curtain whose power is gone drops: its door opens to everyone until power returns. The Resin Ridge (flag `Grown`) takes no worker: the order names no regiment, every tile must lie where the player's linked root-veins reach (or the whole line is refused), and every tile grows at once on its own at one builder's speed. Like the Brood's other Living buildings it regrows on the veins; a tile the veins no longer reach (a relay or the Hive lost) withers 10 hit points a second until it dies. The Choir's pylons with curtains forming between them, and the Hive picking which veins to grow along, are left as they were planned: the curtain is drawn like the other walls, and the ridge is drawn over the veins.

**How it works (as built, stage 3 part 2b, gates on walls).** A gate laid by a worker may go on its owner's own drawn wall tiles: they count as free ground for it, and as it is laid the tiles under it come down (nothing is paid back for them) and the gate's foundation goes up in their place, so the wall never stands open while the gate is built. Only the owner's walls are replaced; a gate can't go on another side's wall, and a building put up finished (the sandbox) replaces nothing. Where the rest of the gate's ground is overgrown it is refused (`Refusal::Overgrown`): the wall would stand open while the ground was cleared, so clear it first. The gate is placed like any building: `R` turns it to run along the wall. The Concord's **Iron Gate** (70 Grub, 1,200 hit points, 22 s) and the Brood's **Maw Gate** (55 Grub, 900, 20 s, Living, grown by a Gnawer that is used up as for any Brood building) work like the Ember Gate; the Choir builds no gates. Their models are in `assets/buildings/fortifications.pak`.

**How it works (as built, stage 3 part 3, blueprints in the simulation).** A blueprint is a wall planned ahead and paid for tile by tile as workers reach it, so a long wall can be laid out early and rise as Grub comes in (`gc_sim::blueprints`). `Command::PlanWall` names a drawn wall and two end tiles, and no worker: each tile of the line becomes a planned tile (a `Plan`), which is only a mark. It holds no ground, costs nothing and stops nobody, and other sides never see it. The line is refused as a whole, as a drawn wall would be, if any tile couldn't go up now; the purse is not checked, tiles already planned or already your wall are left out, a line with nothing new is refused, a grown wall (the Brood's Resin Ridge) can't be planned, and a side holds at most 256 planned tiles at once (`MAX_PLANS`); a side that leaves the field loses its plans. `Command::WorkPlan` sets a worker of the player's to work their plans (refused for a unit that isn't a worker, a Brood worker used up on what it builds, or a side with nothing planned); any other order, falling back included, takes it off them. Each tick, after land clearing, every worker on the plans with nothing else to do (and not fighting or routing) takes the nearest planned tile of its side's (the earliest planned on a tie), pays for it, lays it and goes to build it, and when that tile is up it takes the next. While its side can't pay, or has lost the tier for that wall for now, it waits by the plans. Once it can pay, a tile someone stands by is passed over for the next nearest (up to four a tick) and kept for later, and a tile whose ground has been taken meanwhile (a building, overgrowth, lost power or veins) is dropped. A worker whose side has nothing left planned, or whose match has no room left for buildings, leaves the plans. Workers on the plans count as busy, so the idle-worker button skips them. `Command::DropPlan` drops a line the player planned, named by its two ends (either way round), or one planned tile when both ends are that tile; tiles planned in other lines (a corner shared with the next side of a ring) stay. Drawing a real wall over planned tiles drops them too. The plans and the workers on them are part of the state hash.

**How it works (as built, stage 3 part 3, the client: blueprints, the ring and the guides).** The wall tool (`gc_game::wall_tool`) has two modes, switched with keys while a wall is armed. **`B` plans a blueprint:** a wall can be picked from the build menu even while you can't pay for a tile, so you can plan ahead; each click sends `Command::PlanWall` instead of `BuildWall`, with no worker named and nothing paid. The ghost comes from `World::blueprint` and is pale blue (amber where the ground must be cleared, red where it can't go), and the hint says "paid as workers build them" instead of a price. A grown wall (the Brood's) has no blueprint mode (`B` says so). Carry-on, Shift's 15° steps and snapping work as for a drawn wall, and ends also snap to the ends of your own planned lines. Your own planned tiles are always drawn as faint blue slabs along the lines they were planned in (never another side's plans). **Working the plans:** with workers selected, a **Work plans** button on the worker's card (`A`, shown while your side has plans) and a right-click on a planned tile both send `Command::WorkPlan` for each selected worker; a hint line says how many of the selected workers are working the plans, and any other order stops them. **`Ctrl`/`Cmd` + right-click** on a planned tile sends `Command::DropPlan` along that tile's whole planned line. **`O` switches to the ring tool** (`gc_game::ring_tool` is its plain logic): each click adds a corner, the ghost shows the whole ring closed from the last corner back to the first, and clicking the first corner again or pressing `Enter` orders it, one command per piece: `BuildWall` (every piece names the same worker, the selected one nearest the first piece) or, in blueprint mode, `PlanWall`. The pieces after the first are left to the worker's carry-on along the wall, so a piece cut off past a gap wider than a line can take stays unbuilt until a worker is sent to it. Each side is split into pieces of at most `MAX_WALL_TILES` tiles that share their meeting tile. **Leaning on cliffs:** where the check of a piece finds tiles refused only as `Refusal::Rough`, the side is split around them and those tiles are left open, so the ring uses the cliff as wall; any other refusal shows red and that piece is not sent (the hint counts the red pieces). The price of a drawn ring is checked once for the whole ring against the purse before anything is sent. **Gate suggestions:** the ring draws a pale-gold gate ghost (turned to run along the wall; none for the Choir, which has no gates) where a straight run from a main building of yours to one of its four nearest seeps or veins (within 45 tiles) crosses the ring on a wall tile; suggestions under 4 tiles apart are one. It is only drawn: the player places the gate with the build menu onto the wall as usual. Not done: suggestions for the workers' actual work sites (only seeps and veins near a main building), a tower every so often, and snapping to cliff edges.

## Gates

- A gate dropped onto any wall replaces that stretch. It is at least 3 tiles wide, so a regiment folds into a column to pass (`Regiments::squeeze` already does this in tight ground).
- **Auto** (the default) opens as the owner's or an ally's troops arrive and shuts behind them. **Locked** is shut to everyone. **Open** is held open for a sally.
- An Auto gate won't shut on its own soldiers, so a rout back through the gate can carry the pursuers in with it.
- Enemy routes treat a shut gate as wall.
- At wall level 2 a gate becomes a gatehouse with room for archers above.

**How it works (as built).** Gate cells are *doors* in the walking grid: a shut door is open ground to its owner's side and wall to everyone else, and an open door is open ground for all. Rather than a check on door cells in every search, each side walks a grid of its own once it has a gate that lets it through where it stops others: a copy of the shared grid, made when that side's first gate goes up and kept up to date alongside it (`Nav` keeps one grid per such side and maps each party to one; a side is a party and its allies). Route search on each grid runs exactly as on the one, so it costs nothing extra, and matches with no gates use only the shared grid. The price is memory, one grid per walled side (about 20 MB on a 768-tile map), and bringing each grid up to date when the ground changes: a building going up changes every grid, and a gate opening or shutting changes the grids of the sides whose way it changes, each as a building going up does (a few milliseconds). An Auto gate stays open 2 seconds after the last regiment that opened it, so it doesn't swing with every step.

**How it works (as built, stage 4a, wall levels).** Every faction's drawn wall has a level 2 and a level 3, each a building type whose `upgrades_from` is the level below (the data allows a wall's levels to upgrade from a drawn wall of the same faction and kind, one next level each, never in a loop). The first pass of numbers: level 2 costs 15 Grub a tile and has 2.5 times level 1's hit points, and level 3 costs 20 Grub and 5 Marrow a tile, has 4 times level 1's, and needs tier 2. `Command::RaiseWall` names one tile of the player's wall and, optionally, a worker. It raises the *stretch* that tile belongs to: the tiles of the same wall type touching it edge or corner, and those touching them, that are finished and not already rising, up to 128 (`walls::MAX_RAISE_TILES`). The whole stretch is paid for at once, and refused if the side can't pay for all of it or lacks the tier (`Conquest::wall_level`, also `World::wall_level` for the client). Each tile then rises in place and keeps standing while it does. A drawn wall's tiles rise only while builders work them, at the same pace as building (`Structures::by_hand`). The named worker goes to the tile and works along the wall, carrying on to the next rising or unfinished tile as when building, and any worker sent to build or repair a rising tile raises it. A grown wall's tiles (the Brood's) grow up on their own while their veins reach them, and the order names no worker. `Command::Upgrade` is refused for a wall tile, and the build menu leaves the levels out. The faction twists of level 3 come with stage 4c.

The Choir's walls use the same door cells as gates, held open for the Choir's team at all times. Mario can still overrule the Choir pass-through rule (see [Calls for Mario](#calls-for-mario)).

## Manning the walls

- A regiment right-clicked onto a level 2 or 3 wall climbs at the nearest tower, gatehouse or stair and spreads along the walkway, facing out. The wall is its battle line, so one regiment holds about 15 to 25 tiles.
- **Height:** shooters on a wall reach 20% further. **Cover:** arrows at them do half damage. Melee can't reach them from the ground.
- **Ladders:** infantry attacking a manned wall can climb, slowly, and fight at a disadvantage on top. It is the cheap way in without siege. Spearmen and swordsmen on the wall are what stops climbers.
- When the stretch they stand on breaks, they come down with it, taking damage and disorder.

**How it works.** A manned regiment leaves the walking grid and is held by its wall pieces, with its soldiers in slots along them. Route search never has to know about wall tops.

## Towers

Three kinds, all of which fire:

- **Arrow towers** are the four towers in the game today, firing at last and each keeping its quirk. The Tollgate earns Grub, the Bell Tower silences abilities, the Spine Mound hits fliers, and the Ember Watchtower gets fire arrows that burn wood at level 2.
- **Bolt towers** fire one heavy shot that runs down a file of soldiers: the answer to monsters, cavalry and siege engines.
- **Blast towers** lob splash damage into packed blocks and at enemy siege, but can't hit anything close to them.

Towers snap into walls as joints and are where troops climb up. Each tower has two levels. Towers fire first at siege engines and anything hitting the owner's buildings, then at the nearest enemy, and a right-click focuses one target. Shots use the same volleys and arrows as regiments.

## Field works

- **Stakes:** a dragged line of sharpened stakes. Cavalry and monsters charging into it lose the charge and take hits. Infantry picks through slowly.
- **Plant stakes** is a regiment order: spearmen and archers hammer a stake line in front of themselves in 10 seconds, so fortification is a Field Battle tool too.
- **Barricades:** waist-high carts and logs, built in seconds. They stop walking but not arrows, and troops behind them take less from arrows and fight better across them.
- **One hidden trap per faction**, unseen by the enemy until sprung: the Ember spike pit, Concord grave-hands that hold a regiment in place, a Choir shard mine that stuns, and a Brood burrow nest that bursts out Skitterlings.

## Sieges you can see

- **Attackers spread along the building's face**, each soldier at a spot of their own, hacking, chopping or throwing torches, instead of a block standing beside it. Arrows stick in timber. A ram crew swings on a beat and the building shudders with each blow.
- **Every hit shows**, by material: splinters from wood, chips and dust from stone, shards from bone, sparks and a ring from crystal, ichor from chitin.
- **Damage looks:** cracks and smoke below two thirds of its hit points, fire and holes below one third.
- **Collapse:** each building's model is cut into chunks when it is baked. When it falls it shakes, sags and breaks apart in a cloud of dust. Ember timbers burn and fall in, Concord bones scatter and ghost-lights escape, Choir crystal shatters with a falling chord, Brood shells crack and deflate.
- **Rubble stays** as slow ground for a few minutes. A fallen wall piece leaves a breach, and a smashed gate becomes an open gap.

**Built so far** (2026-10-24): attackers step up to the walls and swing, rams draw back and slam, volleys, stones and fire shots fly to the building, each hit shakes it and knocks off chips, with four new sounds (first pass, PR #200). Stage 7 part 1 moved the effects onto the particle system ([30](30-cinematic-lighting.md#built-stage-3)): hits throw material-specific splinters, chips, sparks, shards or ichor with dust, arrows stay in timber, flesh and bone, damaged buildings smoke and burn with real smoke, flames and embers (only where the camera looks), a fire-pot trails smoke and glow, and a collapse is faction-specific (Ember timbers burn, stone sparks, Concord bones scatter and ghost-lights escape, Choir crystal shatters, Brood shells burst in ichor) with dust rolling along a wall piece's full length. Still to come: pre-cut collapse chunks and a toppling wall (needs a tilt in the model shader, shared with the animation engine), cracks and holes, and rubble as slow ground (a simulation rule).

**How it works.** Almost all of this is drawing only (`gc_game` and `gc_render`, reading `Event::Fallen` and building hit points), so it can't upset the lockstep. It needs a small particle system and pre-cut models. Rubble is the one rule in the simulation: a slow-ground stamp on the walking grid that fades.

## Towns

- When walls and cliffs fully close round a player's main building, the base becomes a **Walled Town**. Buildings inside take a quarter less damage from ordinary regiments, and the owner's regiments fighting inside stand firmer and reinforce faster.
- The game checks enclosure only when a wall goes up or comes down, by flooding out from the main building across the walking grid with shut gates counted as wall.
- Towns look lived in, all as drawing only: paths worn by workers' real traffic, lanterns, chimney smoke, carts by the stores.
- The computer players learn all of this. They wall their bases with the ring tool, put towers on chokepoints, man walls when attacked, plant stakes against cavalry, and bring rams and ladders against gates and the weakest stretch ([18 · AI](18-ai.md)).

## Build plan

Eight stages, one pull request each. Each stage is playable on its own and lands with its docs, golden replays and computer-player support.

1. **Towers fire.** The four existing towers shoot, with their quirks, through the existing volleys. Target priorities and focus fire. The computer players respect tower range. **Built (2026-10-24, stage 1, towers fire):** the four towers shoot. A finished tower looses its shots every reload at an enemy regiment its side can see within range (further downhill), through the same volleys as regiments' arrows; every shot lands, though a shield wall's front and trees still catch some. It picks the regiment its owner told it to shoot (right-click an enemy with the tower selected, `Command::FocusTower`), then siege engines and artillery, then regiments attacking its side's buildings, then the nearest. A Choir tower needs power to shoot, a hasted one reloads faster, and a tower that shoots is known to its target's side from then on. The Tollgate earns 1 Grub for every 4 hit points its volleys take, less upkeep as for gathered Grub. The Bell Tower tolls when an enemy regiment comes within 10 tiles of its walls, at most once in 20 seconds, and for 5 seconds after its enemies can't use a faction power on anything within 10 tiles of it. The Spine Mound and the others all hit fliers already (every tower has `AntiAir`; the simulation has no fliers yet), and the Watchtower's fire arrows wait for tower levels in stage 5. The computer players count enemy towers at a target when weighing an attack, and their raiders keep out of each tower's real range plus 4 tiles. Code: `gc_sim::towers`; `SIM_VERSION` 24 (23 was taken by soldier duels), replay format 25, net protocol 22 (21 was taken by grown land the same day).
2. **Gates that open, buildings that turn.** Door cells in the walking grid with Auto, Locked and Open, measured against the route-search budget. Quarter turns for every building. **Built (2026-10-24, stage 2 part 1, buildings turn):** every building can be put down in any of four quarter turns: `R` (Shift+`R` back) or the mouse wheel turns it while you place it. A quarter turn swaps a footprint's sides, turns the model, and turns the side trained regiments come out of (the front is the south side as drawn; a quarter turn clockwise puts it west). `Command::Build` and `Command::SpawnStructure` carry the turn (0 to 3; more is refused), and `StructureView` gives it with the turned width and depth. The computer players still put everything down as drawn. Code: `BuildingStats::size` and `footprint` in `gc_sim::structures`; `SIM_VERSION` 26, replay format 26 (turned placements have tags of their own, so older replays read as before), net protocol 23. **Built (2026-10-24, stage 2 part 2, gates):** a gate is a wall its owner's side walks through. Select it to set it **Auto** (the default: it opens as your side's regiments march up within 4 tiles of it or stand in the gateway, and shuts 2 seconds after the last has gone; open, it lets anyone through, so a rout can carry its pursuers in, and troops standing in the gateway hold it open, so lock a gate you mean to hold), **Locked** (shut to everyone, your side too) or **Open** (held open to everyone). Allies walk through each other's gates. A gate going up is a wall to everyone until it is finished, and a gate pulled down leaves a gap. The Ember Gate is the only gate until drawn walls bring the others in stage 3. Code: `gc_sim::gates`, `Nav::set_door`; `Command::SetGate` (replay tag 28, wire tag 26); building flag `Gate` (2048); `SIM_VERSION` 28, replay format 28, net protocol 25 (27 and 24 were taken by land clearing the same day). How it is built differs a little from the plan above: see *How it works* under Gates.
3. **Drawn walls for all four factions.** Drag, snap, any angle, ring tool and blueprints. Level 1 walls and gates for the Concord, Choir and Brood, Choir pass-through and Brood walls grown along veins. **Built (2026-10-24, stage 3 part 1, drawn walls):** the Ember Palisade is drawn as a line at any angle, a building a tile, paid and built tile by tile, with joins, crossings, bends, Shift for 15° steps and snapping to your own wall ends, gates and towers (see *How it works* under Drawn walls). The scripted Conquest golden has a worker draw one. Code: `gc_sim::walls`, `gc_game::wall_tool`; `Command::BuildWall` (replay tag 29, wire tag 27); building flag `Line` (4096); the structure pool is 2,048 (`ConquestSetup::STRUCTURES`, was 1,024), since a wall is a building a tile; `SIM_VERSION` 30 (29 was taken by ploughing charges the same day), replay format 29, net protocol 26. **Built (2026-10-24, stage 3 part 2, walls for every faction):** the Concord's Bone Fence, the Choir's Light Curtain (its own side walks through it, and it drops without power) and the Brood's Resin Ridge (grown along root-veins with no worker, withering off them).
