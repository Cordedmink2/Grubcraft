# 08 · UI and UX

The UI goal: **comprehensive, never burdensome**. Think of the best iPad strategy games: you understand what you're looking at in seconds, and the depth is in the decisions, not the interface.

## UI principles

1. **The world is the UI.** Economy, threats and objectives are shown on the map (seep pips, glowing veins, chord triangles, caravan routes, hamlet banners), not in panels.
2. **Glance, don't read.** Every important state has a shape and colour you can recognise in peripheral vision. Text is for tooltips.
3. **One grid, everywhere.** The command card is always a 4 × 3 grid with the same hotkeys (`Q W E R / A S D F / Z X C V`). Common orders always sit in the same slots across every unit and faction.
4. **No modal screens during play.** No pop-ups that pause you, no nested menus. Research and production are queued from the command card.
5. **Smart defaults, optional control.** Workers go to work, groups move in formation, ranged units skirmish. You can override everything, but you never *have* to.
6. **Numbers on demand.** Hover (or hold Alt) on anything to see exact numbers. Nothing important is hidden.

| | | | |
|---|---|---|---|
| `Q` Move | `W` Attack | `E` Hold | `R` Formation |

## Visual style

The in-game UI is stitched like the Bayeux tapestry: linen cloth, ink outlines, and the four wool dyes of the embroidery. The tokens live in `gc_ui::ui::theme` (`LINEN`, `LINEN_DK`, `INK`, `MADDER`, `WOAD`, `WELD`, `WODE`) and every screen draws from them.

- **Cloth and ink.** Panels are linen with a 2-pixel ink outline. The big ones (the Marshal's Scroll, the hero panel, the tooltip's cousin the hint line) also carry a **stitched border**: a strip of dashes in madder, woad, weld and wode wool in turn (`DrawList::stitched_border`, `cloth`, and `stitch_row` for the long strips along the bars). Buttons and cards are `LINEN_DK` with an ink outline; the light panels set into the bar (tray, deployment box) are a paler linen.
- **Ink on linen.** Text is ink; secondary text is ink faded towards the linen. Hotkeys and headings are **madder**, sub-headings and the armed-order hint **woad**, and **weld** marks the thing that is active or waiting for your click (an armed order, the Help button, the minimap frame, the selected card's rim). Good and bad are **wode** green and **madder** red (health, supply at the cap); morale is woad, amber-weld while wavering, madder while routing. A disabled button is paler linen with faded ink, never a different layout.
- **Pictures.** There are no textures in the UI pass, so icons are 12 by 12 pixel-art bit patterns drawn as runs of solid rectangles and scaled in whole pixels (`gc_ui::icons::icon`): Move, Attack, Hold, Formation, Skill, Ultimate, Brace, Charge, Run, Fall back for the command grid; Sword, Spear and Bow on a regiment's banner; Grub, Marrow and Supply in the top bar; and Help, Pause, Play, Hammer, Heart, Flag.
- **Side colour** shows only on banners, swatches, the portrait squares and the minimap, so the cloth stays one colour.

## Marshal's Scroll and tooltips

- **Status strip (top bar):** always shown in a battle or the sandbox. From the right: the **`? Help`** button, the match clock, the game speed (a pause mark and "paused" while paused), and a chip with a swatch of the side you command and the mode ("Sandbox: red", "Field Battle", "Conquest", "Replay"). In Conquest the economy runs from the left (Grub, Marrow and Supply as pictures with numbers, then Larvae, Resonance, Honor, Upkeep); an item that would collide with the chips is left out.
- **Marshal's Scroll:** hidden by default. `H`, `F1` or the Help button opens it; the same key or button, or `Esc`, closes it. It is a linen scroll with a stitched border at the right of the battlefield, listing the keys in three groups (**Orders**, **View**, **Sandbox**, from `gc_ui::hud::HELP_GROUPS`) and, under them, a "Now" section with live lines: the mode and side commanded, what each computer player is doing and expects, what `I` would do next, what is being placed, the selection's formation. It takes the mouse from the battlefield while open. In Conquest `F1` still selects the next idle worker while one stands idle (the button reads "F1 Idle 3"); `H` and the Help button always open the scroll.
- **Tooltips:** rest the mouse on a command button (or the Help button) for about a third of a second and a linen tooltip appears above it with the **name**, the **key** in brackets, one line saying what it does, and, in small ink, the price or why it is unavailable ("Not available now"). It is clamped to the screen and goes away when the mouse moves to another button.
- **Overlay text:** the big text box of earlier builds is gone. The overlay now carries only what needs saying at the moment: the network status, a battle's result, a replay's progress, the stats tuner (`T`) and the last message.

## Camera

- **Bird's-eye view** at a fixed pitch (about 55°), with rotation limited to ±45° (reset with middle-click). Fixed pitch keeps the battlefield readable and the renderer cheap.
- **Zoom** from close (individual soldiers fighting) to far. Past a threshold the view smoothly switches to **Commander View**: soldiers disappear and each regiment is drawn as a banner block with its facing and state, like Total War's tactical map. It is easier to command a big battle in, and much cheaper to render.
- **Built so far (M1 step 8):** zooming out past a camera distance of 175 switches to Commander View (and back in below 160, so it doesn't flicker at the threshold). Each regiment becomes a block the size of its formation in its team colour, with a pale strip along its front and a flagpole whose flag grows with the zoom so it stays readable. `C` opens the credits screen (the authors and licences of the art in the game, read from `CREDITS.md`); `C` or `Esc` closes it.
- **Ride with the banner** (`Y`): a second camera mode, adapted from Mount & Blade, that follows one regiment from just above head height so you feel you are in the line. It is a view, not a mode of play: selecting, orders and control groups all work as normal, and the bird's-eye camera with its fixed pitch stays the normal view. The eye hangs 2.5 tiles above the ground, a few tiles behind the block's rear rank, looking along its facing and a little downward, with a wider lens. The view eases towards the regiment (position and heading are damped, so a block that turns or gives ground swings the view instead of jerking it) and never goes under the terrain. `Y` or `Esc` again, or any pan, zoom, rotate, minimap or tray jump, goes back to the bird's-eye view over that regiment. If the regiment shatters or is destroyed the view returns by itself; a routing regiment is still followed, because watching your line break from inside it is the point. Sound follows the riding eye, so the fight is loud and close. Commander View never switches on while riding. It also works while you watch a replay. `Y` is not in the command grid (`Q W E R / A S D F / Z X C V`) and is none of the other reserved keys (`B`, `Space`, `F1`, the number keys, `G`, `H`, `P`), so it stays free when the order hotkeys arrive.
  - **Built so far (ride with the banner):** `Y` rides with the first selected regiment (`FollowCamera` in `gc_game/src/camera.rs`; the bird's-eye `RtsCamera` is untouched). The eye is 2.5 tiles up and (half the block's depth + 2.5) tiles behind its centre, 12° down, with a 55° lens and a far plane of 420 tiles so the horizon shows; position eases at 4 per second, heading at 2.5 and ground height at 6, and the eye never goes below the true ground plus 0.6. Picking works from the low camera too (a click on a soldier lands on ground far behind it, so while riding the mouse ray is also tested against each block's body); clicking a building to pick it and right-clicking an enemy building to besiege it use the same riding ray; the sound listener moves to the eye; a hint line above the bottom bar says who you are riding with; the minimap outline and the alert badges follow the riding view; the selection plate under the ridden regiment is hidden. The shadow map shrinks to 100 tiles round the eye while riding, so the soldiers nearby get sharper shadows; soldier level of detail and culling use the riding eye, and the scenery (trees, grass) is drawn round a point 40 tiles ahead. Going in and out of a ride is a cut, not a swoop.
- Edge scroll, `WASD`-free arrow scroll (WASD is not used for camera, because the grid uses those keys), middle-drag pan.
- **Mac trackpad:** two-finger pan, pinch zoom, two-finger rotate. Many friends will play on MacBooks without a mouse; this must feel native.

## Selection and control

- Left-click / drag-box to select. Double-click selects all of that type on screen.
- **Control groups** `1` to `0` (`Ctrl+number` to set); double-tap to centre.
- **Group formation:** when several regiments are ordered to move with a right-drag, they arrange themselves along the dragged line in a sensible order (melee front, ranged behind, cavalry on the flanks). This is the single most important "feels like a commander" feature.
- `Tab` cycles sub-groups within a mixed selection (for abilities).
- **Smart cast:** abilities fire on key-press at the cursor (option for click-to-confirm).

**Built so far (M1 step 6):** click and drag-box select (a thin green outline over a faint fill shows the box while you drag; Shift adds or removes), right-click to move, right-drag to set a line's position, width and facing, right-click an enemy to attack, a dotted line along each selected regiment's route with a plate where it will stop, `G` to show the ground no regiment can cross (a dev view for now), `L` for the AI lens (what each computer player is planning, see [18 · AI](18-ai.md#testing-tuning-and-explaining)), and control groups (`Ctrl`/`Cmd` + number to set, the number to recall, `Shift` + number to add, double-tap to centre). `R` cycles the selection's formation (Line, Tight, Loose, and Shield wall when any selected regiment has shields; `F` does the same until the Ultimate slot has an ability, and the sandbox's unit-data reload moved to `Shift`+`R`, see [05](05-combat.md#weapons-and-formations)). Not yet: double-click to select a type, arranging melee in front and ranged behind on a group order (today regiments keep their left-to-right order, and a big group forms several lines with whoever is furthest forward in front), and `Tab` sub-groups.

## Readability on the battlefield

- **Regiment banner** above every regiment: faction colour, unit icon, small model-count bar, morale shown by banner state (upright, tilting when wavering, flat and white-flagged when routing).
- **Team colour** is on banners, cloth and a ground ring when selected. Colour-blind palettes available.
- **Silhouettes first:** every unit type is identifiable by outline at the furthest zoom before Commander View.
- **Ground decals** show formation previews while dragging, attack ranges on hover, chord triangles, vein networks, ability areas.
- **Combat text is off by default.** Damage numbers clutter battles. You read the battle through models falling and banners tilting.
- **Woods and tall grass hide regiments** ([20 · Ambushes](20-ambushes.md#reading-it-on-screen)). Enemies your side can't see aren't drawn and can't be clicked; your own hidden regiments (and your allies') carry a moss-green flag above the treetops, so you always know which of yours the enemy can't see. When an ambush is sprung, by you or on you, an **Ambush!** message names who caught whom, with a war drum and a horn.
- **Built so far (ambushes, 2026-10-15):** trees and tall-grass tufts on the generated maps (the Commander View leaves the grass out), unseen enemies not drawn or picked, the moss-green flag (scaled up in the Commander View), the Ambush! message and sound, and `Shift`+`V` in the sandbox to show every regiment, hidden or not (sandbox only, never in a Field Battle or a network match, and starting one switches it off). Not yet: last-seen ghosts and the hidden tooltip ([20](20-ambushes.md#build-plan), step B).
- **Fog of war on the ground (built 2026-10-17):** in a match played in fog, ground your side can see right now looks as usual, ground it has seen before but cannot see now is a little darker and greyer, and ground it has never seen is dark. The edges are soft, about a tile wide, and move as your regiments and buildings do (the picture is refreshed four times a second). What you have explored is remembered for the match only, for each side (taking command of another side shows what that side has explored). Buildings and other markers are not darkened, so the building ghost stays readable in the dark. Field Battles, the sandbox, replays and the sandbox's reveal toggle (Shift+V) show no fog.

## Onboarding

- **First launch:** name, colour, a 30-second "how to read the screen" overlay, then straight into the **First Harvest** tutorial (skippable).
- **Contextual hints** in the first 3 matches only: one line at a time, near the thing they describe ("Your Hands are idle: right-click a seep"). Off forever after, or turned off in settings.
- **Codex:** every unit, structure and mechanic with numbers, a 3-second looping clip, and "good against / weak against".
- **Faction picker** shows one sentence per faction plus a difficulty rating (Clans: easy; Brood: medium; Choir: hard; Concord: medium-hard).

## Menus and lobby

- Main menu: **Play** (Host / Join), **Learn** (tutorial, primers, sandbox), **Replays**, **Unit viewer**, **Settings**, **Quit**.
- **Host:** choose mode and map, then you're in the lobby. Friends on the same network see the lobby in their Join list automatically; others join by IP or join code.
- **Lobby:** player rows (name, faction, colour, team, ready). Host sets map, mode, Tempo, what happens when a player drops (Wait or Play on), victory conditions. A version check shows clearly if someone's build doesn't match ("Alex is on 0.4.1, host is on 0.4.2").
- From double-clicking the app to being in a friend's lobby should take **under 15 seconds**.

**Built so far (menus and lobby):** the main menu has Play, Sandbox, Replays, Unit viewer, Settings and Exit (Resume and New sandbox appear when you press Esc during a match). Play is one screen: **Host a game** (map, mode, tempo, victory, Host) beside **Join a game** (list of games found on the network, an address or join code box, Join). Hosting opens the lobby: six player rows (colour swatch in one of Red, Violet, Cyan, Bone, Amber or Jade, name, faction, team, ready or AI), the host's Add AI and Remove buttons, Start, and a line saying why the match can't start yet (alone, someone not ready, a version mismatch spelled out as "Alex is on 0.4.1, host is on 0.4.2", Conquest not playable yet). Settings has name, colour, faction, graphics preset, UI scale (75% to 150%), V-sync, frame cap, five audio sliders and the accessibility toggles; they save when you go Back. Working today: name, colour, faction, UI scale (Larger text adds 25% to the scale) and the Master and Music sliders (they change the sound and music at once, and are applied when the game starts; M and N still mute everything and the music on top of them). Saved but not acted on yet: graphics preset, V-sync, frame cap, the Effects, Unit voices and Interface sliders, colour-blind palette, high-contrast banners, reduced shake, hints. Joining works over the local network since M2 step 1: Join's list shows games hosted on the network, or type the host's IP address ([11 · Networking](11-networking.md)). Since step 3, the Host card takes an optional password and the Join card a password box. The Join list marks locked games, and the host's lobby shows a join code with its address, which the Join box accepts. Games appearing or expiring on the network while you type an address never move your keyboard focus, and the game you picked stays picked by its address while the list changes. Since step 5a, a Field Battle lobby has a **Points** stepper for the host (others see "Points: 4,000"), the hint line says what your army is ("Army: picked for you" or "Army: 10 regiments, 4,000 of 4,000 points"), and an **Army** button opens the army builder: a row per regiment of your faction with its points and − count + buttons (+ greys out when the regiment would go over the budget), the total spent, an "Over budget" warning when the host lowers the budget under your army (the lobby line turns red too, and the regiments lowest on the list stay at home), **Pick for me** and **Done** (or Esc). Since Conquest step 5, a Conquest lobby has a **Victory** stepper (Both, Annihilation or Titan's Heart; others see "Victory: Both"), in the lobby's header beside the title, and the Host card has one beside Mode and Tempo, with the password under them. The host's map button in the lobby shows just the map's name (the column is too narrow for "Map: " too), and switching the mode starts on that mode's first map. The lobby's footer has, after Leave and Army, the host's **Champions before AI battles** toggle (on by default; it only matters when a computer player takes part, and other players see it as text); in the battle, the panel announces the champions' challenge and its result for eight seconds ([05 · Combat](05-combat.md#champions-before-battle)). Since M2 step 6a, **Replays** lists every saved battle, newest first, six to a page with Newer and Older, each with its sides, when it was played, how long it lasted and a **Watch** button (greyed, with the reason in red, for one this build can't show); watching is described in [11 · Networking](11-networking.md#save-load-and-replays). Not yet: the Learn entry, key rebinding, the live memory readout, text chat. Enter presses the highlighted (orange) button and Esc goes back one screen.

**Style (menus, tapestry look):** every menu screen is a piece of linen cloth on the dimmed battlefield: a dark ink outline, a faint weave, and a stitched border of madder, woad, weld and wode dashes just inside the edge. The screen's title hangs from the top edge on a madder banner with a swallow-tail (`Ui::banner`, used through `Ui::header`), and a one-line hint in the muted ink sits beside it saying what to do ("Host a game for friends, or join one on your network."). Text is ink on linen (never light on light); errors are a dark red, good news a dark green. Buttons are folded cloth with an ink outline and a soft shadow: a lit edge on top, a weld rim on hover, pressed in one pixel when held, flat and pale with greyed text when disabled. The one primary action per screen (Host, Start, Done, and Settings' Back) is madder with a weld stitch, and Enter presses it. Back (Leave in the lobby) is always the bottom-left button, 140 by 44 pixels, so it never moves between screens. A greyed button that blocks you says why in a short line beside it (Start: "Add at least one more player"; Join: "Pick a game above, or type its address."). Check boxes get a madder cross-stitch, sliders a woad fill with a weld knob, steppers a muted label before the bold value and arrows that light on hover, text boxes sink into the cloth and turn woad when focused, and list rows get a woad bar and weld rim when picked. The dye tokens are in `gc_ui::ui::theme` (`LINEN`, `INK`, `MADDER`, `WOAD`, `WELD`, `WODE`, plus the `CLOTH*` and `INK_MUTED` shades for widgets). No textures: it is solid quads and the 8x16 bitmap font. Not yet: the credits screen (still the plain text overlay in the game, so it keeps the old look) and a display face for the banners (the bitmap font stands in for Cinzel).

## Unit viewer

A place to look at every unit and hero of the four factions as a 3D model, from the main menu's **Unit viewer** button (also from the Esc menu in a match; Back returns to the menu and **Resume** to the battle).

- **Left panel:** the four factions as tabs, and below them that faction's regiments, workers, summoned units and heroes, each with a tag (T1 to T3, worker, summon, hero). A unit with no model yet is dimmed. Up and Down move through the list, Left and Right change faction, and the wheel over the list scrolls it.
- **Middle:** the selected unit alone on a stone plinth on the flattest ground near the middle of the map, with a pale strip along the plinth's front so the way it faces shows from any side. The camera is an orbit camera: **drag** with the left button to turn and tilt (grab the model and turn it), **right-drag** (or Shift and left-drag, or the middle button) to move the view, **wheel** or pinch to zoom, **Home** or **R** to put the view back. The default view fits the whole body, with extra headroom for fliers so the War Kite and the like fit while they hover. A unit with no model says so in the middle of the window instead.
- **Bottom:** a row of animation buttons, **Cycle** (idle, walk, attack, fall, four seconds each) and one button for each clip the model has (Idle, Walk, Run, Attack, Attack 2, Shoot, Cheer, Hit, Block, Fall). Picking a unit goes back to Cycle.
- **Right panel:** the faction, the unit's name and what it is ("Spear regiment, tier 1", "Hero, duelist"), and two tabs: **Stats** (cost, supply, where it is trained and how long, soldiers, hit points, speed, melee numbers, charge bonus, ranged numbers, morale, traits) and **Abilities** (each with how it works and what it does). The wheel over the panel scrolls long text.

**Built so far (unit viewer):** all of the above (`gc_ui::viewer` for the panels, `gc_game/src/viewer.rs` for the camera and the model). The words come from the rosters at build time (`gc_bake::viewer`), so the viewer always matches `data/rosters/`. Models are found by the unit's name, so all four rosters (Ember Clans, Carrion Concord, Shard Choir and Brood) can be seen today; a unit without a model would show "No 3D model for this unit yet". `--viewer NAME|start` opens it at launch, on a named unit or the first one with a model.

## Communication

- **Ping wheel** (`G` + mouse direction): "Attack here", "Defend here", "Danger", "On my way", "Need help", "Good game". Pings show on the minimap and play a sound.
- Text chat (`Enter`), team or all.
- No in-game voice: friends already use Discord or FaceTime.
- **AI allies use the same channels:** they treat your pings as requests, ping their own plans ("Attacking here", "Danger"), and send short chat lines in their personality's voice ([18 · AI](18-ai.md#ai-allies)).

## Settings

- Graphics presets (Battery, Balanced, High) plus individual options; a live memory/frame readout in the settings screen.
- Resolution scale, UI scale (75% to 150%), frame cap, V-sync.
- Full key rebinding; alternate grid layouts (e.g. AZERTY).
- Audio sliders: master, music, effects, unit voices, UI.
- Accessibility: colour-blind team palettes, high-contrast banners, larger text, reduced screen shake, subtitles for unit barks.

## Pause, speed, and fairness

- Multiplayer pause: any player can pause up to 3 times per match; unpausing has a 3-second countdown.
- Game speed is fixed in multiplayer (lobby choice of Normal or Fast). Solo modes allow slow-motion and fast-forward.
- **Built so far (M1 step 7):** `P` pauses, `-` and `=` step through 0.1×, 0.25×, 0.5×, 1×, 2× and 4×. The sandbox panel in the top-left corner shows the speed, the side you command, the type you are placing and the keys; `T` opens a stats panel for that type (Up and Down pick a stat, Left and Right change it, `Shift` for ten steps) and `H` hides the panel. It is drawn with the dev overlay's text for now and moves to the `gc_ui` widgets once the menus land.

## Post-game

- Summary: winner, graphs (income, army value, supply over time), key battles list with "Watch" and "Restage this battle" buttons, MVP regiment (most kills), funniest stat ("Most Grub lost to raids").
- Save replay (automatic).

## AI players in the lobby

- The host can fill any slot with an AI player, as an opponent or a teammate. The row shows a **difficulty** (Recruit, Veteran, General, Warlord) and a **personality** (each faction's two, or Random, the default), both chosen with one click.
- Every slot, human or AI, has a **handicap** setting (income from 50% to 150%), shown on the row whenever it isn't 100%, so nobody is surprised.
- Details in [18 · AI](18-ai.md#difficulty).
- **Built so far (AI-1 step 1, Warlord with AI-2):** an AI row has a level button (Recruit, Veteran, General or Warlord; click to step through them, Veteran to start) beside Remove; other players see the level as text. The personality is picked at random from the faction's two each match and named in the sandbox panel once the battle starts. Not yet: choosing the personality, handicap.
- **In the battle,** the sandbox panel lists each computer player's personality, level, battle plan, posture and expected result, and `I` hands the side you command to the computer (Recruit, then Veteran, then General, then Warlord, then back to you).

## Parley (planned, M4)

Dealing with hamlets, mercenary companies and, later, other peoples on the map uses one small panel, never a pop-up that pauses the game:

- Select one of their units or buildings and the panel opens beside the command card. It shows their **stance**, and **liking** and **trust** as one bar each, with the top reasons underneath ("You traded with us", "You burned Millbrook").
- At most four actions for that party (Trade, Hire, Rumours, Gift; later Request passage, Demand tribute), on the usual grid keys.
- Every action shows its likely answer *before* you commit ("Likely to accept: they like you, you're asking a lot"), because the answer is a visible rule, not a dice roll. See [18 · AI](18-ai.md#deals-and-contracts).

## Guide

<a id="guide"></a>An in-game reference for every building and troop of every faction, so a player never has to leave the game to learn what a Dojo trains or what counters a Rocket Cart.

- **Layout.** A full-window panel. Top row: the **Buildings** and **Troops** tabs, then a button for each faction (Ember Clans, Brood, Shard Choir, Carrion Concord). Left: a scrollable list of the faction's entries, each with its tier (`T2`, `hero`, `summon`). Right: the entry's card.
- **The card** has two sub-tabs. **Overview** has a short summary (what it does, when to use it, what beats it; at most 350 characters), then the real numbers (cost, build time, hit points, models, melee and ranged stats, morale, traits) and links (what a building trains and unlocks, what a troop is trained at, evolves into, is strong and weak against). **Upgrades** has a button for each upgrade and shows the chosen one's cost, what it does for you and its numbers: for a main building its tier upgrade and what that unlocks; for a production building the units it trains; for the Brood's Chrysalis Chamber the evolutions; for a troop its evolutions and abilities (a hero's ranked abilities and its level-6 ultimate); for a hero building the heroes it hires. Research (Granary, Forge, Harmonics Lab) is shown as "not designed yet" because it is not (see [21 · Buildings](21-buildings.md)).
- **Keys and mouse.** Up and Down walk the list, Left and Right change faction, the wheel scrolls the list or the card under the mouse, Escape closes. Each tab and faction remembers its own selected row.
- **Honesty.** Every number comes from the faction rosters (`data/rosters/*.ron`); only the prose is written by hand, in `data/guide/<faction>.ron` ([format](17-unit-rosters.md#guide-data)). The class counters are the design table of [17 · Unit Rosters](17-unit-rosters.md#classes-and-counters), which battles do not all apply yet.
- **Built so far:** the screen (`gc_ui::guide`, headless and unit tested like the other screens) and its data (`gc_game::guide_data`, with tests that every roster building and unit has an entry, that text fits and is plain ASCII, and that numbers match the rosters). **Not wired in yet:** the main-menu Guide button, the battle top-bar button and the `G` hotkey (`Guide::new(guide_data::load())`, `Guide::frame` while open, `GuideEvent::Close` to leave, `Guide::show` to jump to a selected building or regiment, `Guide::wheel` and `guide::over_panel` for the mouse).
