# 08 · UI and UX

The UI goal: **comprehensive, never burdensome**. Think of the best iPad strategy games: you understand what you're looking at in seconds, and the depth is in the decisions, not the interface.

## UI principles

1. **The world is the UI.** Economy, threats and objectives are shown on the map (seep pips, glowing veins, chord triangles, caravan routes, hamlet banners), not in panels.
2. **Glance, don't read.** Every important state has a shape and colour you can recognise in peripheral vision. Text is for tooltips.
3. **One grid, everywhere.** The command card is always a 4 × 3 grid with the same hotkeys (`Q W E R / A S D F / Z X C V`). Common orders always sit in the same slots across every unit and faction.
4. **No modal screens during play.** No pop-ups that pause you, no nested menus. Research and production are queued from the command card.
5. **Smart defaults, optional control.** Workers go to work, groups move in formation, ranged units skirmish. You can override everything, but you never *have* to.
6. **Numbers on demand.** Hover (or hold Alt) on anything to see exact numbers. Nothing important is hidden.

- **Top bar:** the four economic numbers, match clock, menu, and the status strip with the Help button (see [Marshal's Scroll and tooltips](#marshals-scroll-and-tooltips)). Nothing else.
- **Minimap (bottom-left):** terrain, fog, units as dots, seeps as glowing dots, pings. Click to jump, right-click to order. On a Field Battle map with sites (the Wilds, [07](07-world-and-maps.md#field-battle-maps-and-recipes)), each site is a mark in its own colour: villages amber, outposts and lairs dark red, standing stones pale blue (*built 2026-10-17*).
- **Army tray (bottom-centre):** the Total War unit-card row. Each selected regiment (or, with nothing selected, your current control group) has a card showing a portrait, model count bar, morale bar, veterancy chevrons and ability-ready dots. Clicking a card selects that regiment; double-click centres the camera on it. When a structure is selected, the tray shows its production queue and research instead.
- **Command card (bottom-right):** 12 slots. Common layout:

| | | | |
|---|---|---|---|
| `Q` Move | `W` Attack | `E` Hold | `R` Formation |
| `A` Ability 1 | `S` Ability 2 | `D` Ability 3 | `F` Ultimate / special |
| `Z` Brace (Skirmish shares it later) | `X` Charge | `C` Run | `V` Back (Fall back) |

Structures use the same grid for production. Workers' build menu is one sub-page (opened with `B`), the only nested menu in the game. A selected gate of yours shows its mode and whether it is open on its card, and `Q` / `W` / `E` set it to Auto, Locked or Open (`Command::SetGate`; the current mode is lit); on the field an open gate shows its door leaves swung back along the jambs and a shut one shows them meeting across the doorway.

- **Built so far (Conquest UI, piece 1):** the bottom bar is drawn in battles and the sandbox (not behind the menus). The minimap shows the ground (water, woods, grass, rising hide), a dot per regiment your side can see in its team colour, your selection bigger with a white rim, and the camera's view as a white outline; click or drag on it to move the camera, right-click to send the selection there. The army tray shows a card for each selected regiment (or, with nothing selected, the control group you last recalled): a team-coloured portrait with the unit's initial until there is art, a red rim when it is fighting, the name, a model-count bar, a morale bar (blue steady, yellow wavering, red routing) and the model count. Click a card to select that regiment alone (Shift toggles it); double-click centres the camera on it. Too many cards shrink, then show a "+N" count. Clicks on the bar never reach the battlefield. Its buttons (Ready, the command grid, regiment cards, alert badges) fire when you let go over the button you pressed, like the menu's, so moving off before releasing cancels; the minimap, like a slider, follows the press. The bar fills the bottom edge, so scrolling by pushing the mouse against the bottom of the screen is off during a match (the arrow keys still scroll). During a Field Battle's deployment phase the command-grid corner shows DEPLOYMENT, the time left, a hint and an orange **Ready** button ("Waiting for the others" once pressed), and your deployment zone is a dashed amber ring on the ground ([06 · Game modes](06-game-modes.md#field-battle-extras)). Not yet: veterancy chevrons and ability dots on cards (no unit has them yet), the command grid, edge notifications, idle button, the top bar and structure queues (later pieces and Conquest itself).

A dead hero shows "DEAD" and the time until it can be revived. The abilities themselves are used from the command grid: while a hero is shown, its slots `A`, `S`, `D` and `F` carry the abilities' short names and their state (ready, "12s" cooling, "auto" for passives, "locked" before they are learned).

The sandbox panel moves down below it. The menu button is not on it yet (Honor is, see stage 5a below). The **minimap** also shows your buildings and your allies' as their footprints in their owner's colour (the selected one with a white rim; enemy buildings join once buildings have sight, in step 5) and each gathering spot as a small point, gold for a Grub seep and bone white for a Marrow vein, dim once only dregs are left. On the field each gathering spot is a low glowing mound in the same colours that shrinks as it runs down. **Click one of your buildings** to select it (a regiment standing right where you click wins): it gets a white rim on the ground, and the tray shows its card (name, hit points, "Building 40%" or "Upgrading to ..." while that goes on) and its training queue of five slots, the front one with a progress bar. The **idle worker button** now shows in Conquest (not in its replays) with the number of your workers standing idle (not walking, gathering or building), and clicking it or pressing `F1` selects the next one and centres the camera on it. 
- **Built so far (Conquest step 4c, 2026-10-16):** Conquest's orders work from the command grid, which is now wide enough for seven characters a slot (a name's first word and its price, "300/100" for Grub and Marrow). **A selected building** fills the whole grid with the regiments it trains, in its own grid keys (`Q` first), each with its price; one you can't afford, whose tier you haven't reached or whose queue is full is dimmed, and pressing or clicking it says why. The card asks the simulation what it would allow, so it never offers what an order would refuse. `V` is the main building's next tier ("Tier 2", with its price), or "Cancel" (75% back) for a building still going up or upgrading; an upgrading building still trains. Clicking the queue takes its last regiment off, for all it cost. **Right-click the ground** with a building that trains selected to set its rally point, shown as a flag in your colour. **With workers selected**, `D` lays a new main building to expand, and `F` (or `B`) opens the build menu: the grid shows every other building of your faction, with prices; choose one and a ghost follows the cursor (red where it can't go), `R` or the mouse wheel turns the ghost a quarter turn clockwise (`Shift`+`R` back; the wheel does not zoom while a building is being placed), and a click has the selected worker nearest the spot lay it (`Shift` keeps it chosen, `Esc` puts it down, `Esc` again closes the menu). Right-click a seep or vein to send the selected workers to gather there, or one of your buildings still going up (or damaged) to have them build or mend it; a selection that isn't all workers moves there instead, and an enemy there is attacked. While a building is selected or the build menu is open, the grid's twelve keys belong to it. In Conquest the sandbox's editing tools (placing regiments and buildings, deleting, tuning) are off. Hover tooltips on the grid are built (see [Marshal's Scroll and tooltips](#marshals-scroll-and-tooltips)). Not yet: a second worker page, research.
- **Drawn walls (fortifications):** choosing a wall (the Palisade) from a worker's build menu arms the wall tool instead of the one-building ghost. The first click sets the start (a hint line says so); the ghost then follows the mouse as a straight line of tiles, drawn as slabs along the line so a diagonal looks smooth, in the side's colour where every tile can go up, amber on overgrown ground (clear it first: the whole line is refused while any of it is overgrown) and red where it can't, with the tile count and price in the hint ("12 tiles, 120 Grub") or the first reason it can't go. A click sends the order for the nearest selected worker, and the next piece starts from that end, so clicking again bends the wall; right-click or `Esc` ends the tool. `Shift` locks the angle to 15-degree steps from the start. The start and end snap to the ends of your own walls, your towers and your gates when the mouse is within a tile and a half. A line is at most 32 tiles; the end stops short of the mouse beyond that. A finished tile is a slab along the line it was drawn in, about half a tile thick, dimming with its hit points like other buildings.

Hasted buildings and regiments pulse with a cyan outline.

While it is aimed a pale-gold ring of that radius follows the mouse over the ground. `Esc`, a right click or the button again puts an aimed one away. Regiments on a march or under the banner are outlined like hasted ones, amber and pale gold in turn (each a little further out than the last, so all three show at once). A click on something that isn't a post for you (an enemy building, an unfinished Waystation) does nothing special, and the caravans move there as any right-click would. The Conquest **minimap** marks each hamlet (the map's Villages, Towns and the Bazaar) in the same amber as a Field Battle's villages. ## Visual style

The in-game UI is stitched like the Bayeux tapestry: linen cloth, ink outlines, and the four wool dyes of the embroidery. The tokens live in `gc_ui::ui::theme` (`LINEN`, `LINEN_DK`, `INK`, `MADDER`, `WOAD`, `WELD`, `WODE`) and every screen draws from them.

- **Cloth and ink.** Panels are linen with a 2-pixel ink outline. The big ones (the Marshal's Scroll, the hero panel, the tooltip's cousin the hint line) also carry a **stitched border**: a strip of dashes in madder, woad, weld and wode wool in turn (`DrawList::stitched_border`, `cloth`, and `stitch_row` for the long strips along the bars). Buttons and cards are `LINEN_DK` with an ink outline; the light panels set into the bar (tray, deployment box) are a paler linen.
- **Ink on linen.** Text is ink; secondary text is ink faded towards the linen. Hotkeys and headings are **madder**, sub-headings and the armed-order hint **woad**, and **weld** marks the thing that is active or waiting for your click (an armed order, the Help button, the minimap frame, the selected card's rim). Good and bad are **wode** green and **madder** red (health, supply at the cap); morale is woad, amber-weld while wavering, madder while routing. A disabled button is paler linen with faded ink, never a different layout.
- **Pictures.** There are no textures in the UI pass, so icons are 12 by 12 pixel-art bit patterns drawn as runs of solid rectangles and scaled in whole pixels (`gc_ui::icons::icon`): Move, Attack, Hold, Formation, Skill, Ultimate, Brace, Charge, Run, Fall back for the command grid; Sword, Spear and Bow on a regiment's banner; Grub, Marrow and Supply in the top bar; and Help, Pause, Play, Hammer, Heart, Flag.
- **Side colour** shows only on banners, swatches, the portrait squares and the minimap, so the cloth stays one colour.

## The Place panel

Sandbox only (and offline Field Battles, wherever `B` places regiments; off in Conquest, network matches and replays). The top bar's **Place** button, or `B`, opens a linen cloth with a stitched border at the left of the battlefield (`gc_ui::placer`):

- **Tabs:** **Field types** and **All factions**, in the sandbox only (an offline Field Battle has the Field types alone). The tabs only change what the list shows; nothing restarts until you pick a troop.
- **Types:** on the Field types tab, one row for each of the five Field Battle types, with a picture by kind (spear, sword or bow), the name, soldiers, speed and points. On the All factions tab, four faction chips (Ember, Brood, Choir, Carrion, in their dyes) pick whose troops are listed, in roster order, each with its soldiers, speed and training cost (Grub and Marrow, or "summoned" for the troops a power calls). The chosen type is weld yellow and says "chosen", or "placing" while placing is on. Clicking a row chooses the type **and** turns placing on; the field then takes left-clicks as before. `[` and `]` still step through the types.
- **Side:** four colour swatches (the sandbox's sides); click one to place for, and command, that side (the same as `Tab`).
- **Two unit tables, one at a time.** The Field types are `data/units.ron`; the roster troops are the Conquest table (`GameData::conquest_units`), which numbers its units differently, so one field cannot hold both. Picking a troop from the table the sandbox is not running on **starts the sandbox again** on that table: the field is cleared (a line says so), the side you command is kept, and the sandbox is still never recorded. On the roster table any troop can be placed for any of the four sides, and the buildings tool (`K`), the stats panel (`T`) and the other sandbox keys work as before; there is no Conquest economy, worker, build menu or top-bar resources (the game's `conquest_match()` is false there, though its unit table is the roster's). `T` tunes the chosen troop on whichever table is running, and units tuned keep their changes on their own table across restarts. `Shift`+`R` reloads `data/units.ron` (a `--features dev` build run from the repository), which is the Field types: on the roster table it says to choose a Field type first, because the roster is baked into the game's data and is not read from disk.
- **Left out:** the roster's workers (Hands, Gnawer, Tuner, Ledger Clerk), which gather and build, and the Carrion Concord's Caravan, which hauls Grub, are not regiments to fight with. They are greyed under the faction's chip with a count and a line of why, when the screen has room. The table holds no buildings (use `K`) and no heroes yet. Summoned troops (Spectral Samurai, Fire Spirit, Spawnlings, Bound Dead, Grubfolk Militia) are placeable; their powers and the roster's abilities are Conquest's and do nothing here, and flying troops walk the ground as the simulation has no air yet.
- **Closing:** the close box, or `B` while placing (which stops placing too); `Esc` closes it and stops placing. The panel takes the mouse from the battlefield; with it closed, placing carries on.
- **Small screens:** the panel sheds detail rather than leave the screen: first the troop names go from two lines a faction to one, then to counts only, then the greyed section goes (the rows keep their second line, a little tighter), and last the rows shrink to one packed line each. A faction of eleven troops under the tabs needs about 530 pixels of field for the two-line rows, so at 1280x720 and below it uses the tighter rows. Under about 240 pixels of field it is not drawn (the keys still work).
- **Hint:** while placing, a line over the bar says "Placing Yari Ashigaru for red".

## Marshal's Scroll and tooltips

- **Status strip (top bar):** always shown in a battle or the sandbox. From the right: the **`? Help`** button, the match clock, the game speed (a pause mark and "paused" while paused), and a chip with a swatch of the side you command and the mode ("Sandbox: red", "Field Battle", "Conquest", "Replay"). In Conquest the economy runs from the left (Grub, Marrow and Supply as pictures with numbers, then Larvae, Resonance, Honor, Upkeep); an item that would collide with the chips is left out.
- **Marshal's Scroll:** hidden by default. `H`, `F1` or the Help button opens it; the same key or button, or `Esc`, closes it. It is a linen scroll with a stitched border at the right of the battlefield, listing the keys in three groups (**Orders**, **View**, **Sandbox**, from `gc_ui::hud::HELP_GROUPS`) and, under them, a "Now" section with live lines: the mode and side commanded, what each computer player is doing and expects, what `I` would do next, what is being placed, the selection's formation. It takes the mouse from the battlefield while open. In Conquest `F1` still selects the next idle worker while one stands idle (the button reads "F1 Idle 3"); `H` and the Help button always open the scroll.
- **Tooltips:** rest the mouse on a command button (or the Help button) for about a third of a second and a linen tooltip appears above it with the **name**, the **key** in brackets, one line saying what it does, and, in small ink, the price or why it is unavailable ("Not available now"). It is clamped to the screen and goes away when the mouse moves to another button.
- **Overlay text:** the big text box of earlier builds is gone. The overlay now carries only what needs saying at the moment: the network status, a battle's result, a replay's progress, the stats tuner (`T`) and the last message.

## Camera

- **Bird's-eye view** at about 55° from the default zoom out to the widest, easing to 30° at the closest zoom (since 2026-10-23, so zooming in brings the scene toward eye level and the sky begins to show), with rotation limited to ±45° (reset with middle-click). The pitch follows the zoom and nothing else, which keeps the battlefield readable and the renderer cheap. `F9` steps through the prepared looks of the light (Noon, Golden hour, Overcast, Storm, Dusk; see [30](30-cinematic-lighting.md)), and each land tints the air round the camera. `F8` turns the director camera on or off (it frames the fight; any move of the view takes it back; replays start with it on, see [30](30-cinematic-lighting.md#built-stage-4-the-director-camera)). `F10` opens the light tuning panel (a developer's tool for tuning the looks by eye; it keeps the arrow keys while open).
- **Zoom** from close (individual soldiers fighting) to far. Past a threshold the view smoothly switches to **Commander View**: soldiers disappear and each regiment is drawn as a banner block with its facing and state, like Total War's tactical map. It is easier to command a big battle in, and much cheaper to render.
- **Built so far (M1 step 8):** zooming out past a camera distance of 175 switches to Commander View (and back in below 160, so it doesn't flicker at the threshold). Each regiment becomes a block the size of its formation in its team colour, with a pale strip along its front and a flagpole whose flag grows with the zoom so it stays readable. `C` opens the credits screen (the authors and licences of the art in the game, read from `CREDITS.md`); `C` or `Esc` closes it.
- **Ride with the banner** (`Y`): a second camera mode, adapted from Mount & Blade, that follows one regiment from just above head height so you feel you are in the line. It is a view, not a mode of play: selecting, orders and control groups all work as normal, and the bird's-eye camera with its fixed pitch stays the normal view. The eye hangs 2.5 tiles above the ground, a few tiles behind the block's rear rank, looking along its facing and a little downward, with a wider lens. The view eases towards the regiment (position and heading are damped, so a block that turns or gives ground swings the view instead of jerking it) and never goes under the terrain. `Y` or `Esc` again, or any pan, zoom, rotate, minimap or tray jump, goes back to the bird's-eye view over that regiment. If the regiment shatters or is destroyed the view returns by itself; a routing regiment is still followed, because watching your line break from inside it is the point. Sound follows the riding eye, so the fight is loud and close. Commander View never switches on while riding. It also works while you watch a replay. `Y` is not in the command grid (`Q W E R / A S D F / Z X C V`) and is none of the other reserved keys (`B`, `Space`, `F1`, the number keys, `G`, `H`, `O`, `P`), so it stays free when the order hotkeys arrive.
  - **Built so far (ride with the banner):** `Y` rides with the first selected regiment (`FollowCamera` in `gc_game/src/camera.rs`; the bird's-eye `RtsCamera` is untouched). The eye is 2.5 tiles up and (half the block's depth + 2.5) tiles behind its centre, 12° down, with a 55° lens and a far plane of 420 tiles so the horizon shows; position eases at 4 per second, heading at 2.5 and ground height at 6, and the eye never goes below the true ground plus 0.6. Picking works from the low camera too (a click on a soldier lands on ground far behind it, so while riding the mouse ray is also tested against each block's body); clicking a building to pick it and right-clicking an enemy building to besiege it use the same riding ray; the sound listener moves to the eye; a hint line above the bottom bar says who you are riding with; the minimap outline and the alert badges follow the riding view; the selection plate under the ridden regiment is hidden. The shadow map shrinks to 100 tiles round the eye while riding, so the soldiers nearby get sharper shadows; soldier level of detail and culling use the riding eye, and the scenery (trees, grass) is drawn round a point 40 tiles ahead. Going in and out of a ride is a cut, not a swoop.
- Edge scroll, `WASD`-free arrow scroll (WASD is not used for camera, because the grid uses those keys), middle-drag pan.
- **Mac trackpad:** two-finger pan, pinch zoom, two-finger rotate. Many friends will play on MacBooks without a mouse; this must feel native.

## Selection and control

- Left-click / drag-box to select. Double-click selects all of that type on screen.
- **Control groups** `1` to `0` (`Ctrl+number` to set); double-tap to centre.
- **Group formation:** when several regiments are ordered to move with a right-drag (or a right-click on the ground), they arrange themselves by role along the dragged line: melee in the front line, ranged behind it with support in the middle, cavalry on the wings, engines and workers at the back. The shape is chosen with `O` or the army-shape chips in the panel (Line, Deep, Hammer, Square, Plain, or one of your own; see below). This is the single most important "feels like a commander" feature.
- `Tab` cycles sub-groups within a mixed selection (for abilities).
- **Smart cast:** abilities fire on key-press at the cursor (option for click-to-confirm).

**Built so far (M1 step 6):** click and drag-box select (a thin green outline over a faint fill shows the box while you drag; Shift adds or removes), right-click to move, right-drag to set a line's position, width and facing, right-click an enemy to attack, a dotted line along each selected regiment's route with a plate where it will stop, `G` to show the ground no regiment can cross (a dev view for now), `L` for the AI lens (what each computer player is planning, see [18 · AI](18-ai.md#testing-tuning-and-explaining)), and control groups (`Ctrl`/`Cmd` + number to set, the number to recall, `Shift` + number to add, double-tap to centre). `R` cycles the selection's formation (Line, Tight, Loose, and Shield wall when any selected regiment has shields; `F` does the same until the Ultimate slot has an ability, and the sandbox's unit-data reload moved to `Shift`+`R`, see [05](05-combat.md#weapons-and-formations)). Not yet: double-click to select a type, and `Tab` sub-groups.

**Built so far (army shapes, 2026-10-22):** a group order's regiments are arranged by role, and the player chooses the shape (`gc_sim::arrange`, see [10](10-architecture.md#the-deterministic-simulation)). A regiment's role comes from its unit's class: Light, Spear, Heavy and Monster are melee; Ranged is ranged; Cavalry and Air are flankers; Support is support; Worker, Artillery and Siege are the rear. The Field Battle types have no class, so their role is guessed from stats: 5 or more tiles a second is a flanker, range 10 or more is ranged, anything else melee. The presets:
- **Line** (the default): melee in one line, ranged behind with the support regiments among them, cavalry split between the wings, engines and workers behind.
- **Deep:** melee, then ranged, then reserve cavalry, then the rest, one line behind another, no wider than the drag (24 tiles without one).
- **Hammer:** Line, with every horse on the wing it stands on.
- **Square:** melee facing out on four sides, the rest inside, with the target at the centre.
- **Plain:** side by side as they stand, the old behaviour.

With a dragged width, Line and Hammer stretch every frontage by the same factor so the front line, wings included, spans the drag. Square re-forms each regiment to its raised frontage and takes only the drag's place and facing.

`O` cycles the presets and then your saved shapes. `Shift`+`O` saves the selection's current shape (two or more regiments) into the first empty one of "Mine 1" to "Mine 3" and chooses it; when all three are full it replaces the chosen saved shape, or Mine 1 if a preset is chosen. A row of chips along the top of the army tray lists the shapes, the chosen one lit (a click chooses; each has a tooltip). It shows when two or more regiments are selected. Saved shapes and the choice are kept in `army_shapes.ron` beside the settings. A saved shape fits any army: each regiment takes a slot of its own role, spread across them, then any free slot, then a line behind. Only the ordering player's machine does the arranging. The simulation still gets one move per regiment, so the sim, replays and the network protocol are unchanged. The group marches at its slowest member's pace.

## Readability on the battlefield

- **Regiment banner** above every regiment: faction colour, unit icon, small model-count bar, morale shown by banner state (upright, tilting when wavering, flat and white-flagged when routing).
- **Team colour** is on banners, cloth and a ground ring when selected. Colour-blind palettes available.
- **Silhouettes first:** every unit type is identifiable by outline at the furthest zoom before Commander View.
- **Ground decals** show formation previews while dragging, attack ranges on hover, chord triangles, vein networks, ability areas.
- **Combat text is off by default.** Damage numbers clutter battles. You read the battle through models falling and banners tilting.
- **Woods and tall grass hide regiments** ([20 · Ambushes](20-ambushes.md#reading-it-on-screen)). Enemies your side can't see aren't drawn and can't be clicked; your own hidden regiments (and your allies') carry a moss-green flag above the treetops, so you always know which of yours the enemy can't see. When an ambush is sprung, by you or on you, an **Ambush!** message names who caught whom, with a war drum and a horn.
- **Built so far (ambushes, 2026-10-15):** trees and tall-grass tufts on the generated maps (the Commander View leaves the grass out), unseen enemies not drawn or picked, the moss-green flag (scaled up in the Commander View), the Ambush! message and sound, and `Shift`+`V` in the sandbox to show every regiment, hidden or not (sandbox only, never in a Field Battle or a network match, and starting one switches it off). Not yet: last-seen ghosts and the hidden tooltip ([20](20-ambushes.md#build-plan), step B).
- **Fog of war on the ground (built 2026-10-17):** in a match played in fog, ground your side can see right now looks as usual, ground it has seen before but cannot see now is a little darker and greyer, and ground it has never seen is dark. The edges are soft, about a tile wide, and move as your regiments and buildings do (the picture is refreshed four times a second). What you have explored is remembered for the match only, for each side (taking command of another side shows what that side has explored). Buildings and other markers are not darkened, so the building ghost stays readable in the dark. Field Battles, the sandbox, replays and the sandbox's reveal toggle (Shift+V) show no fog.

## Unit viewer

A place to look at every unit and hero of the four factions as a 3D model, from the main menu's **Unit viewer** button (also from the Esc menu in a match; Back returns to the menu and **Resume** to the battle).

- **Left panel:** the four factions as tabs, and below them that faction's regiments, workers, summoned units and heroes, each with a tag (T1 to T3, worker, summon, hero). A unit with no model yet is dimmed. Up and Down move through the list, Left and Right change faction, and the wheel over the list scrolls it.
- **Middle:** the selected unit alone on a stone plinth on the flattest ground near the middle of the map, with a pale strip along the plinth's front so the way it faces shows from any side. The camera is an orbit camera: **drag** with the left button to turn and tilt (grab the model and turn it), **right-drag** (or Shift and left-drag, or the middle button) to move the view, **wheel** or pinch to zoom, **Home** or **R** to put the view back. The default view fits the whole body, with extra headroom for fliers so the War Kite and the like fit while they hover. A unit with no model says so in the middle of the window instead.
- **Bottom:** a row of animation buttons, **Cycle** (idle, walk, attack, fall, four seconds each) and one button for each clip the model has (Idle, Walk, Run, Attack, Attack 2, Shoot, Cheer, Hit, Block, Fall). Picking a unit goes back to Cycle.
- **Right panel:** the faction, the unit's name and what it is ("Spear regiment, tier 1", "Hero, duelist"), and two tabs: **Stats** (cost, supply, where it is trained and how long, soldiers, hit points, speed, melee numbers, charge bonus, ranged numbers, morale, traits) and **Abilities** (each with how it works and what it does). The wheel over the panel scrolls long text.

**Built so far (unit viewer):** all of the above (`gc_ui::viewer` for the panels, `gc_game/src/viewer.rs` for the camera and the model). The words come from the rosters at build time (`gc_bake::viewer`), so the viewer always matches `data/rosters/`. Models are found by the unit's name, so all four rosters (Ember Clans, Carrion Concord, Shard Choir and Brood) can be seen today; a unit without a model would show "No 3D model for this unit yet". `--viewer NAME|start` opens it at launch, on a named unit or the first one with a model.

## Guide

<a id="guide"></a>An in-game reference for every building and troop of every faction, so a player never has to leave the game to learn what a Dojo trains or what counters a Rocket Cart.

- **Layout.** A full-window panel. Top row: the **Quick Start**, **Buildings** and **Troops** tabs, then a button for each faction (Ember Clans, Brood, Shard Choir, Carrion Concord). Left: a scrollable list of the faction's entries, each with its tier (`T2`, `hero`, `summon`). Right: the entry's card.
- **The card** has two sub-tabs. **Overview** has a short summary (what it does, when to use it, what beats it; at most 350 characters), then the real numbers (cost, build time, hit points, models, melee and ranged stats, morale, traits) and links (what a building trains and unlocks, what a troop is trained at, evolves into, is strong and weak against). **Upgrades** has a button for each upgrade and shows the chosen one's cost, what it does for you and its numbers: for a main building its tier upgrade and what that unlocks; for a production building the units it trains; for the Brood's Chrysalis Chamber the evolutions; for a troop its evolutions and abilities (a hero's ranked abilities and its level-6 ultimate); for a hero building the heroes it hires. Research (Granary, Forge, Harmonics Lab) is shown as "not designed yet" because it is not (see [21 · Buildings](21-buildings.md)).
- **Keys and mouse.** Up and Down walk the list, Left and Right change faction, the wheel scrolls the list or the card under the mouse, Escape closes. Each tab and faction remembers its own selected row.
- **Quick Start.** The first tab, and the one the Guide opens on: five short illustrated steps (Gather, Build, Train, Command, Win), each a picture drawn from rectangles and the HUD's pixel icons (`gc_ui::quick_start`) and one or two lines of text. It has no list or card, so the faction buttons and the arrow keys sit idle on it.
- **Building tips.** Each building has two or three succinct tips (a pixel icon and one sentence of at most 100 characters), written in `data/guide/<faction>.ron` ([format](17-unit-rosters.md#guide-data)). They show in three places: on a **tips card** over the field beside the tray the whole time a building is armed to be placed (so the tips are in front of you when you choose where it goes); behind the **info button** (a small **i**) on the card of a selected building of yours, which toggles the same card with a close box and stays on for the next building you select; and under **Tips** on the building's Overview card in the Guide. Conquest only for now; the Sandbox's troop picker has no buildings.
- **Honesty.** Every number comes from the faction rosters (`data/rosters/*.ron`); only the prose is written by hand, in `data/guide/<faction>.ron` ([format](17-unit-rosters.md#guide-data)). The class counters are the design table of [17 · Unit Rosters](17-unit-rosters.md#classes-and-counters), which battles do not all apply yet.
- **Built so far:** the screen (`gc_ui::guide`, headless and unit tested like the other screens) and its data (`gc_game::guide_data`, with tests that every roster building and unit has an entry, that text fits and is plain ASCII, and that numbers match the rosters). **Wiring:** the main-menu Guide button and the `U` hotkey open it (`Guide::new(guide_data::load())`, `Guide::frame` while open, `GuideEvent::Close` to leave, `Guide::show` to jump to a selected building or regiment, `Guide::wheel` and `guide::over_panel` for the mouse).
