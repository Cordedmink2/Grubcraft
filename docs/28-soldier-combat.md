# 28 · Every Soldier Fights

When two regiments clash, every soldier picks a man and fights him: guard, swing, block, stagger, kill, as in Total War and Mount & Blade, instead of two blocks of bodies leaning into each other. This is idea 20 on Mario's list (2026-10-23).

**Decided (Mario, 2026-10-23):** option B, **duels in the simulation**, is the official plan and is built first when the project resumes. Option C, **every soldier simulated**, is a later stage on top of it. Stage 1, the scrum, and stage 2, duels in the simulation, are built (2026-10-24). The concept page with sketches, a playable before-and-after toy and the sources is [Every Soldier Fights](https://claude.ai/artifact/3faYPzf1Njf69cHa4fWyUc); its files are in the project files under `design/soldier-combat/`.

Numbers here are first-pass and move in balance passes, like everywhere else in these docs.

## Where the game stands (2026-10-23, main at `7084966`)

About a third of the look is already built, all of it on screen only ([09 · Animation](09-art-and-audio.md#animation), [10 · The two-tier simulation](10-architecture.md#the-two-tier-simulation)):

- **Built:** front-rank duel pairs that face each other and swing out of step; blows on screen drawn from the simulation's own hit chance (parries, shield blocks, staggers, crush-through, charge knockdowns, the partner's killing blow); champions before battle; the fallen and spent arrows stay for the whole battle.
- **Half there:** only the front rank fights. Everyone behind stands in his slot, and no soldier strays more than 0.6 tiles from it (`DUEL_LEASH` in `gc_game/src/puppets.rs`), so the meeting line stays straight. Each soldier has ten clips (`ClipKind` in `gc_assets/src/model.rs`: idle, walk, run, two attacks, hit, die, cheer, shoot, block).
- **Missing:** the battle maths doesn't know who fights whom. Melee strikes once a second per soldier who can reach, with `hit_percent` = 50 + attack − defence kept between 10% and 90% (`gc_sim/src/combat.rs`), and the damage goes to the regiment as a whole: `take_damage` (`gc_sim/src/regiments.rs`) removes hit points from the back ranks forward. The screen then picks a front-rank man to fall, so two players can see different men die. There are no matched kills, no bodies pushing each other, no men knocked back by a charge, no feet placed on slopes and no heads turning toward the enemy.

## What we learned from Total War and Mount & Blade

From developer talks, official and community modding documentation, measurements players publish, and watching the games. **We do not reverse-engineer or copy other games' files** (Mario's rule of 2026-10-13; on 2026-10-23 we noted that fair use does not cover lifting another game's animations even for a non-commercial game, New Zealand has only narrow fair dealing, and the repository is public). The source list, marked read or search-only, is on the concept page and in `design/soldier-combat/sources.md`.

- **Every man has his own fight.** Total War rolls each soldier's blow against his own target (players measure about 35 + melee attack − melee defence, kept between 8% and 90%). Mount & Blade makes every soldier a full character.
- **The line breaks into a scrum** two or three men deep at contact, and the men behind step up as gaps open.
- **Matched kills** (two soldiers playing one move together) are loved but costly: players rate Shogun 2's the best for ordinary soldiers; Warhammer, Troy and Three Kingdoms keep them mostly for heroes and big monsters. Long ones lock men in place and make fights feel random, so ours stay short.
- **Bodies have weight.** In Warhammer II a collision's force comes from the two bodies' speed and their difference in mass.
- **Blows interrupt**: a hit staggers and cancels the victim's swing, and heavy weapons crush through blocks (Mount & Blade; we already have it on screen).
- **Smooth, varied movement** comes from many clips per weapon, layering (legs apart from arms), additive flinches, inertialization (Gears of War) and foot IK, and **detail goes where the camera looks**.
- **Not to copy:** Mount & Blade's big melees turn into blobs where formations vanish in seconds. Grubcraft keeps regiments readable from the commander's height.

## The three ways, and the decision

| | A · Look only | **B · Duels in the sim (chosen)** | C · Every soldier simulated (later stage) |
|---|---|---|---|
| What the battle maths knows | Regiments, as today | Regiments, plus who fights whom inside a locked melee | Every soldier as a body with position, weight and collisions |
| On screen | Total War-like up close | The same, plus gaps where men really died and two-on-ones that really cost | The most natural: charges plough and scatter, lines bulge and wrap round a flank |
| Who decides who dies | The screen | The simulation | The simulation |
| Balance and replays | Unchanged | Balance tables re-run, golden replays re-recorded, replay format bumped | Start again |
| Simulation cost | None | Small: about 2,000 men in melee in the biggest benchmark battle, one blow each a second or so (to be measured) | Large: about 6,000 bodies colliding every tick, perhaps ten times today (to be measured) |
| Risk | Low | Medium | High |

Close up, B and C look the same: the duels, staggers and kills are animation either way. They differ in how the crowd moves. In B the scrum is a ragged band and the blocks behind still move as blocks; charge impacts are decided by the maths and played on screen. In C the bodies really collide, so those things happen by themselves.

**Why B first, then C (Mario agreed 2026-10-23):** almost all the work carries over to C. The scrum, the animation engine, the fight library, matched kills and effects are the same for both, and C also needs B's per-soldier pairing, blows and hit points. What B-then-C wastes is some glue that keeps pairs inside a block and one extra round of balance tables and replay re-recording. In return there is a playable result much sooner, battles never sit broken for weeks, and we learn whether B already looks good enough. B's soldier records are designed so C only adds bodies and collisions.

## Option B: duels in the simulation

Blocks still march, turn, push, brace and rout as blocks, so pathfinding, formations, lines that push, the computer players and the balance work all carry over. Only the inside of a locked melee changes.

- **Pairing.** When two regiments lock in melee, the simulation pairs soldiers one to one across the contact line: the front rank, the second rank of units with reach (spears), and two-on-ones where one line overlaps the other's flank. Pairing is deterministic (slot order, fixed-point distances, PCG32 substreams), recomputed only when a pair breaks.
- **Blows.** Each pair trades blows on its own clock (about one a second, offset per soldier), using the same hit chance and damage rules as today, including flank, rear, charge, formation and morale modifiers.
- **Deaths.** A soldier dies when his own hit points run out, so the man who falls is the man who was beaten. The slot he leaves is filled from behind as now.
- **Events to the screen.** The simulation reports every blow (striker, target, landed or parried, stagger, crush-through, kill), and the screen plays exactly that, so every player and every replay sees the same fight.
- **What it changes.** Kills come a little later and in bursts, because damage no longer piles onto one man at a time, so the balance tables (faction, team and terrain) are re-run and retuned. `SIM_VERSION` is bumped, the golden replays are re-blessed, the replay format changes, and the `field-8000pt-2v2` benchmark is checked against the tick budget (combat and morale 0.5 ms of the 2.0 ms p99).
- **Ready for C.** Each soldier gets a small record in the simulation (slot, hit points, partner, blow clock), laid out so C can later add a position, velocity and mass without reshaping it.

## On screen

- **The scrum.** Lines loosen into a band two or three men deep. Every man in reach finds a partner, two-on-ones form where lines overlap, rear ranks press in and step into gaps, spacing follows weapon reach (spearmen hold a spear's length, Brood claws get right on top of their man), and men waiting their turn shift their weight and shout.
- **Exchanges, not single swings.** Each pair plays short exchanges of two to four moves (feint, swing, block, shove, riposte) chosen to end on the blow's result. Good defence parries more, veterans stagger more, wavering regiments fight sloppily.
- **Matched kills.** When a blow kills, killer and victim play one move together, lined up during the wind-up by motion warping so the blade meets the body. About a second long; heroes and champions get longer ones. Played near the camera and in the ride-with-the-banner view; far away, a plain swing and fall.
- **Charges that hit home.** Riders and monsters knock men back or flat, the line bends around the wedge, riders slow and start cutting, and braced spears stop a charge dead (the weights already come from the maths).
- **One soldier, several layers.** Legs play footwork while arms swing, flinches are added on top from the side the blow came from, heads and chests turn toward the man, feet sit on slopes, moves blend smoothly and each man plays them a touch faster or slower, mirrored or not. Technically: a pose step on the graphics card mixes a few clips per soldier before skinning, and clips are stored once per skeleton and shared, so the library costs megabytes, not hundreds of them.
- **Each faction fights its own way** (to confirm with Mario): Ember Clans keep disciplined spacing and thrust Yari from the second rank; the Concord's dead barely flinch and collapse into bones; Choir crystal chips and shatters; Brood Skitterlings leap two or three onto one man and the Colossus Maw tosses men aside.
- **Aftermath and wear** (built 2026-10-24, `gc_game/src/aftermath.rs`, a picture only). The dead already lie where they fell for the whole battle. A regiment wiped out (not a group of workers) plants its torn standard where it stood, in the dimmed colour of its team, for four minutes (it shrinks away over the last eight seconds). About fourteen seconds after the first regiment fell somewhere, crows start to come, one every second or so, a crow for every five of the fallen within twelve tiles (two to fourteen): they circle for a while, settle among the dead to peck and hop, and leave after two and a half minutes. A second regiment wiped out nearby adds to the same flock; one wiped out after the flock has left brings a new one. Soldiers the player can see walking within five tiles flush the crows into the air, and they settle again once it has passed. Regiments that fight pick up grime, drawn or not: after two minutes of melee their cloth is painted 28% darker, and their skin and armour a little (the soldier shader allows the per-soldier tone byte only 8%, and this work leaves the shaders alone). The dead are laid down about half as dirty. A viewer catching up on an online match plants nothing for fights already played. Nothing here reaches the simulation, so replays and online matches are untouched. Still to come: trophies taken from the dead, caws for the soundscape, a heavier grime if the shader grows a channel for it, and prisoners (idea 14, which needs a design).

## The fight library and where moves come from

A human weapon type (sword and shield, spear, two-hander, polearm, axe or mace) needs about 50 moves: guards and idles, attacks from four directions, parries and blocks, hits from the front, sides and back, stagger, knockdown and get-up, eight or more deaths, footwork and six or so matched kills. Brood and Choir bodies need about 30 each, made for their own skeletons in Blender as now.

- **Mixamo** (Adobe): free, commercial use allowed, no credit needed; Mario downloads, we fit to our skeletons in Blender. Raw files may not be redistributed as an asset pack, and may not train AI.
- **Bought motion capture**: only packs whose licence allows our own engine (some Unreal Marketplace and Unity "Restricted" packs don't).
- **Phone video to motion** (Rokoko, Move AI, Plask, DeepMotion): terms unclear or paid-only for commercial use; get a written answer first.
- **Blender keyframes**: right for insects and crystal bodies, stiffer for human swordplay.
- **Non-commercial sets**: Mario said on 2026-10-23 that Grubcraft is non-commercial, so sets licensed for non-commercial use may be usable; sets that forbid derivatives (such as Ubisoft's LAFAN1) are still out, because fitting moves to our skeletons makes a derivative.
- **Never**: moves taken from other games.

## Build stages

Each stage is one or two pull requests. Stages marked *sim* change the battle maths and run the full golden-replay and balance checks.

1. **The scrum** (screen only). **Built 2026-10-24** (see [10 · Architecture](10-architecture.md#the-two-tier-simulation)); per-soldier tone, height and animation speed came with it. The band, partners for every man in reach, two-on-ones, rear ranks stepping into gaps, weapon spacing, waiting men alive. Uses today's ten clips, so it shows quickly. Opus lead with a Sonnet helper.
2. **Duels in the sim** (*sim*, option B). **Built 2026-10-24** (see [10 · Architecture](10-architecture.md#the-two-tier-simulation)): each striker strikes the man across from him, every blow is rolled and reported, men die when their own hit points run out and the screen lays down the man the sim killed. A second pull request made the screen play the sim's wounds and parries instead of its own rolls. The replay format did not need to change, since blows are worked out from the recorded orders. Planned: pairing, per-pair blows, per-man deaths, blow events, balance tables re-run, goldens re-blessed, replay format bumped, benchmark checked. Opus (battle maths stays with the lead), Haiku for benchmark runs. Two pull requests.
3. **The animation engine**. Many tagged moves per skeleton, layers, additive flinches, smooth blends, foot IK, look-at, and a picker that chooses moves by weapon, direction and result; a new model format. Sonnet 5.5 at high effort, in the materials and animation thread of the [restart plan](29-restart-plan.md). Can run beside stage 2.
4. **The fight library**. About 50 moves per human weapon type and 30 per Brood and Choir body, one faction at a time, starting with the Ember Clans. Sonnet 5.5 alone (Mario's 3D modelling rule, 2026-10-23). Needs Mario's call on where moves come from.
5. **Matched kills and impacts**. Charge knock-backs came first, with physical formations (idea 11): **built 2026-10-24**, a heavy enough charge ploughs into the ranks, drives them back and dents the line, knocking down the men it hits and staggering their neighbours ([05](05-combat.md#charges-that-plough-in)). **Kills that connect: built 2026-10-24** (screen only, no new moves): when the simulation's blow kills a man, his killer steps in to his fighting distance from where the man stands, squares up and swings, then holds his follow-through for 0.3 s before he looks for a new man; the man struck faces him and, as the blow lands, is driven back along it as he falls, 0.15 tiles for an even blow, 0.3 when a heavier man's blow crushes into him, over 0.35 s (arrows still drop a man on the spot). In code: `Puppets::killing`, `Act::Kill` lasting `SWING_SECONDS + KILL_HOLD`, and the recoil step kept in two spare bits of `Fallen::owner` and applied as a function of age in `Puppets::corpses`. **Matched kills in battle: built 2026-10-24** (screen only): when both men's models have a kill made for them (the three of [33](33-animation-engine.md), on every katana and spear man of the Ember rig, against any man of it), the two play it instead. The killer starts his half at once and the victim his, together and at the moves' own speed, timed so the contact lands on the simulation's blow, which is told then; the killer steps in to the kill's reach rather than his fighting distance, holds the act for the whole move (about 1.8 s, ending in his guard), and the victim is not driven back, since his half plays his fall and he lies as it ends. Only a front-rank man who can close to the reach from where he stands (within 0.6 tiles of it) plays one, and the kills are picked afresh each time, so a regiment doesn't repeat one. A man who falls to a kill before its blow lands falls to that blow with the single moves; one who falls to a killer playing out a kill that has landed just drops, rather than cut it short. A man behind the front rank and everyone without a kill keep the single moves above (champions have their own, below). In code: `Clips` numbers every model's kills once; when a match starts, `Clips::kills_of` gives the puppets a `puppets::Kills` table (each kill's length, contact and reach by that number, and the best-fitting kills for each pair of unit types) through `Puppets::set_kills`; the killer keeps the kill and its timing in `Killing::pair`, the victim its number in `Fallen::pair` (which grows the record to 28 bytes); `Move::Kill { pair, at }` and `Corpse::kill` hand the number back, and `Clips::fight` and `Clips::death` draw the two halves. The killer closes on the reach with his usual steering, and motion warping (below) puts him on it at contact. **Tumbling bodies: built 2026-10-24** (screen only): a man killed by a blow two classes of mass heavier than his own (a heavy man's on a light one, or a heavy man's great blade on a man of middle weight) or by the regiment charging into his is thrown rather than driven back: he flies 0.9 tiles back along the blow over 0.6 s, the first 0.4 s of it in the air (rising 0.3 tiles), and turns over four tenths of a turn one way or the other as he goes, playing his death move as he flies and lies where he comes down. In code: `THROWN` and `SPIN_LEFT` bits beside the recoil step in `Fallen::owner`, and `Fallen::pose` as a function of age, giving `Corpse::lift` and the turned facing. **Finishers: built 2026-10-24** (screen only, no new moves): a man the simulation kills while he lies knocked down (more than half way down his fall) dies where he lies, as he lies, rather than getting up to fall again; his killer steps in to stand over him, 0.55 tiles off, and swings his killing blow bent over him, his chest and head pitched about 40° down (eased in over the first 30% of his killing blow, swing and held follow-through, and out over its last 25%, through the renderer's look, so on models fitted to a skeleton; others swing upright). A man already swinging at a standing man who also has a man on the ground fall to him (or the other way about) doesn't share the blow: that man just drops. A man knocked down who simply drops lies as he was too. In code: `Killing::finish`, `Soldier::stoop`, recoil step 3 (`FINISHED`) in `Fallen::owner` and `Corpse::lying`, drawn at the end of his death move. **Finishing moves: built 2026-10-24** (screen only): a man whose model has finishers made for striking down ([33](33-animation-engine.md): the Ember men's katana stab and overhead chop, and the yari stab) plays one instead of the bent swing, picked by a roll for each kill: once through at its own speed (about 1.6 to 1.75 s, its blow landing at `BLOW_AT` of it, when the simulation's blow is told), from its own reach from the man's chest, as the move was made (measured from where each man's feet are drawn, so a yari man stands much further off than a katana man), and he is not bent. The chest is where the man's own model lies it in the death he plays (`Clips::lying_chest`, by his model and look): about 0.7 tiles behind where he stood for an Ember man, more for a bigger one, as all fall on their backs; others keep the bent swing. A man who falls to a killer whose finishing blow has already landed just drops, rather than cut it short. In code: `Kills::add_finisher` (each unit type's finisher reach and length) and `Kills::add_chests` (each unit type's lying chest by look), both from `Clips::kills_of`, `Killing::strike`, `Move::Finish` and `Clips::fight`. **Motion warping: built 2026-10-24** (screen only): a man of the front rank dealing any killing blow (his own swing, a matched kill or a finisher) is drawn onto the distance it needs from where his man stood, the swing's fighting distance, the kill's reach or the finisher's (0.55 tiles bent over a swing, else the finishing move's), along the line between them: he makes for it whatever his leash from his place in the ranks, and each frame covers his share of what is left of the way in what is left of the time until the blow lands (his pace set to match, so his feet keep up), facing his man all the while, so he arrives as it lands, and stands there as he follows through. Like any man dealing a killing blow, he neither shuffles, sways nor glances about meanwhile. Whether he warps is settled when the blow begins, from the rank he is in then. Only the killer moves, so the man struck falls where he stood. A man behind the front rank strikes from his place as before. In code: `Killing::front`, and the warp's target and step in `Puppets::update_on`. **Champions' matched kills: built 2026-10-24** (screen only): a champions' duel ([05](05-combat.md)) whose winner and loser both have a kill made for them, of reach up to 1.6 tiles, ends in one: the kill is picked when the duel begins, from the challenge, so every machine shows the same one, and timed so its contact lands as the duel ends, the winner warped onto its reach from where his man stands as it plays, and keeping that spot as he cheers after; the loser plays his half and lies as it ends, and the winner cheers once his half has played out. A duel abandoned (one man's regiment broken or gone) ends as before. In code: `Duel::kill`, `Duel::killing` and `Move::Killed`. **Head turns and flinches near the camera: built 2026-10-24** (screen only, no new moves): on soldiers drawn at the nearest level of detail (100 px tall or more on screen), fading in over the next 30 px, the upper body turns on top of the move through the renderer's look (so on models fitted to a skeleton). A man taking a blow flinches, his head snapped back, and aside by half as much, furthest 15% into his stagger, block or parry (about 26° back for a blow that lands, 13° for one blocked, 8° for one parried) and back by 60% of it. Men not fighting turn their heads to the latest of their regiment's men to fall within 2.5 tiles of them, ahead of them or to the side (not behind, nor at their very feet), looking a little down, for 1.2 s from when he falls, each up to a quarter of a second slow to react; where they look eases toward it (and back) rather than snapping, and a man who falls while they are still looking at the last doesn't take their eyes off him. The two add up. A finisher's bend wins over either, and a man turned by the renderer itself (to a wall he attacks, a tool, or his stand on a wall top) turns no head. In code: `Soldier::head` (`puppets::Head`), `flinch`, `fall_glance`, `Puppets::last_fall` and `Puppets::glance`, and `LodView::detail` in the game's soldier drawing. **The general as a hero: built 2026-10-24** (screen only): when the simulation kills an enemy standing within 2.5 tiles of a regiment's general ([37](37-commander.md)) at the hand of one of that regiment's men (or the general's own), and the general is free (not already striking, reeling or down, nor out as a champion) and has rested 4 s since his last brandish, the general deals the killing blow himself, if he can get there by the time it lands (at 4 tiles a second at most, so usually only with a matched kill, whose blow lands later; a single swing he takes only from close by). He comes in beside his man, half a radian off his man's line to the enemy, warped onto the blow from whatever rank he stands in; after it he stays where he struck and brandishes his weapon (the cheer) for 1 s, facing the man, unless his regiment breaks; a blow he takes, blocks or parries meanwhile shows instead. In code: `Puppets::hero_for`, `Puppets::general`, `Puppets::brandish` and `Killing::from`. Stage 5 is built.
6. **Sparks, blood and sound**. Sparks on parries; blood, ichor, crystal shards or bone dust by faction; hit sounds by weapon and armour. Shares particles with the cinematic lighting plan and sound with the positional soundscape idea. **Sparks and wounds built (2026-10-24, battle effects thread):** the puppets tell every blow as it lands (`Puppets::drain_impacts`: parried, blocked, wounded, crushed, killed; a kill lands as its man falls) and `gc_game/src/effects.rs` draws it with the particle recipes ([30](30-cinematic-lighting.md#built-stage-3)): sparks off a parry, sparks and shield splinters off a block, then the victim's own wound (blood for the Ember Clans, ichor for the Brood, crystal shards for the Choir, pale ghost-light for the Concord's dead (blood for its living soldiers), with sparks too if he is armoured), more for a crushing blow and most for a kill. Only blows the camera sees are drawn, at most 48 a frame, thinned by the Graphics setting. **Blood setting built (2026-10-24):** Settings, the right-hand column (below Contextual hints) has **Full blood: heavier wounds** (off by default, saved with the settings). Off, wounds are the stylised ones above. On, flesh and ichor wounds throw about twice the spray plus a second, darker spray low toward the ground, while the dust of heavy blows is as before; crystal shards, the Concord's ghost-light and sparks are the same either way. Still to come: hit sounds.
7. **Every soldier simulated** (*sim*, option C, a later stage). Soldiers become bodies in the simulation with position, velocity, mass and collisions; regiments steer their men. Planned separately as a milestone of its own once B is in and Mario has seen it.
