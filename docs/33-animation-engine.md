# 33 · Animation engine

*Stage 3 of [28 · Soldier combat](28-soldier-combat.md), the engine half of the "Materials, animation engine and fight library" thread of the [restart plan](29-restart-plan.md). Each part is marked built when it lands.*

## Where it started

A soldier played one clip at a time. `gc_artbake` sampled each clip at 12 frames a second into the model's own rows of the bone animation buffer (a 3×4 matrix per bone, bind pose already folded in), the instance named two rows and a blend, and `cs_skin` blended each vertex's bones from those two rows. The game picked a clip by `ClipKind` (ten kinds) through `gc_game::models::Clips`. So every model carried its own copy of the stock clips, a soldier could not swing his arms while his legs walked, and a flinch could not lie on top of a swing.

## What the engine adds

- **A pose pass (built).** `cs_pose` in `world.wgsl` runs before skinning: one workgroup of 64 threads per soldier writes his final bone matrices once into a palette buffer (48 bytes a bone; it starts at 64 Ki bones and grows by powers of two up to 1 Mi, 48 MB), and `cs_skin` only reads the palette. Before, every vertex re-blended its bones' frames itself. Both passes share one compute pass and one bind group (palette at binding 15, the animation tables at 16). The planner cuts soldiers for the palette's cap the same way as for the vertex buffers.
- **Shared skeletons, clips as joint poses (built).** Models on the same skeleton (same joint names and parents; the Quaternius rig under most humanoids, each Brood creature its own) share one set of moves. A move frame stores, per joint, a rotation and a translation plus uniform scale relative to its parent joint, as eight half floats (16 bytes a joint, the precision the bone rows had), and each skeleton's frames sit in a block of their own, rows as long as it has joints. Each model keeps a *fit*: the matrix that takes the skeleton to the game's axes and, per bone, the rest inverse, bind fix and axis change the bake used to fold in. The pass rebuilds the joints' global matrices level by level in workgroup memory (a barrier per depth; the roots take the model's turn and size) and ends each bone with its fix. The halves are decoded with bit arithmetic, since the shading language's own unpacking needs a capability not every GPU has. A model whose clips squash or shear a joint (not expressible as a rotation, translation and uniform scale within 0.001) keeps its own bone rows and the old path, so nothing changes for it.
- **Layers (built in the pass).** Each instance carries up to three layers on top of its base frames. A layer names two frame rows, a blend, a weight, a bone mask (all, legs, upper body, head, left arm, right arm) and whether it is *additive*. A plain layer mixes towards its own pose on the joints of its mask (legs from a walk, arms from a swing); an additive layer adds its change from its first frame, scaled by weight (a flinch on top of anything). Masks and left/right mirror pairs come from the joint names (Quaternius `.L/.R`, Mixamo `Left/Right`). Models still on bone rows take whole-body plain layers only, as a matrix blend.
- **Moves, not kinds.** A move carries tags: an act (idle, walk, run, guard, attack, parry, block, hit, stagger, knockdown, get up, death, cheer, shoot, step, flinch), a weapon type, a side (front, back, left, right, high, low), a mask and flags (loops, additive, mirrors). Today's ten clip kinds become one move each.
- **A move picker (built, `gc_game/src/moves.rs`).** `Moves::pick(model, want)` takes what the fight wants (an act, a weapon, the side a blow came from, a seed) and returns the best move the model has: the first act along a fallback list that the model has a move for (a parry falls back to an attack, raised partway; a block to a parry; a stagger to a hit; a knockdown to a death), then the move whose weapon and side fit best, then the seed among equals so a regiment does not move as one. A move made for a particular weapon or side is only picked when the fight names it, so a soldier whose weapon is not named never borrows a spear's parry from another model on his skeleton. A model offers its own clips first and, when fitted, its skeleton's tagged moves. Layered moves (additive, or on part of the body) are never a base move: they are picked only when a layer of that very act is asked for. `gc_game::models::Clips` now picks every soldier's clips through it, and a test holds today's soldiers to exactly the clips they played before. T4 keeps `puppets.rs`; the picker is what grows underneath it.
- **Feet on the ground (built in the pass).** After the joints' matrices are built, one thread of the soldier's workgroup bends his legs to the ground: it finds the ground under each foot (as the skinning pass stands him, on the terrain or the water line), drops the whole body to the lower foot's ground (never raising it, so in a dip both legs bend) and raises each foot to its own. The leg is solved exactly: the knee turns about its hinge until hip and foot are as far apart as hip and goal, then the leg swings at the hip onto the goal. The hinge is the leg's own bending plane while the knee is forward of the hip-to-foot line and else runs across the leg, so a straight or slightly overstraightened knee (common in motion capture) still bends forward. The foot keeps the turn the move gave it. A foot counts only while it is planted: from 0.3 to 0.6 of the leg's reach above the ground it fades out, so a leap or a kick is left alone and a walking foot eases in and out. Each foot's ground counts up to 0.4 of the reach, and no foot is raised more than that. `SoldierInstance::feet` says how much the feet keep to the move instead (0 to `FEET_AS_POSED`): the game fades it up as a soldier is knocked down (and back as he gets up) and over the first 0.3 seconds of a death, so nothing snaps. It never runs on a model lying flat, lifted or sinking, and costs nothing for a soldier who neither bends nor looks.
- **Look-at (built in the pass).** An instance can name where a soldier looks, as a yaw (up to a quarter turn either way) and a pitch (up to an eighth of a turn up or down) and how much of it shows. The same thread shares the turn out over chest (0.3), neck (0.3) and head (0.4), each about its own joint, so the arms and the weapon turn with the chest; a rig missing one gives its share to the others. `--showroom-clip look` idles while looking round. Each joint turns by its share more than the one below, so the head ends looking exactly as asked, without tilting.
- **The rig** for both is worked out per skeleton when the pack is uploaded: the joints named as legs (only when both legs are a chain of upper leg, lower leg and foot), chest, neck and head, and for each joint the nearest of those at or above it, whose fix it takes. Brood bodies (six legs, no such names) and the rigid siege engines get none and stand as before.
- **Still to come:** the game aiming heads (at a foe, at the commander); mixed speeds and mirrors per soldier; foot IK for the Brood's six legs and for horses.

## Format: optional tables, packs stay readable

The model schema stays 4. A pack may carry six optional tables (`anim/skeletons`, `anim/joints`, `anim/moves`, `anim/frames`, `anim/fits`, `anim/fixes`, format in `gc_assets::anim`, their own `ANIM_SCHEMA` 1); a pack without them, such as every building and scenery pack, loads as before. A model's clips are pose rows of its skeleton's block when it has a fit, else bone rows of its own. Models share a skeleton only when their joints, parents and rest pose all match, since a move's poses carry its bones' lengths; playing one rig's moves on a rig of other proportions (most Mixamo characters share joint names) needs retargeting, which comes with the fight library. Moves are stored once per skeleton (frames deduplicated by content, each move keeping its own record), so a fifty-move library costs its frames once per skeleton instead of once per model, and fitted models drop their bone rows. With every unit model fitted, today's clips take about 3.7 MB as poses against 9.8 MB as bone rows, so `models.pak` shrank from 86.3 MB to 79.7 MB when it was re-baked (2026-10-24), leaving room under GitHub's 100 MB file limit for the fight library. A frame row is counted from the start of its skeleton's block, and an instance names rows in 16 bits, so a skeleton may hold up to 65,536 frames. Each joint carries one bit per mask that covers it; depths are worked out from the parents when the pack is loaded. The engine change re-baked nothing. The units' pack was then re-baked with the new tables on their own (stage 4, part 1), which bumped the baker version to 2; the units' surface detail of [31 · Surface detail](31-surface-detail.md) comes in a second re-bake.

## Stages

1. **Pose pass** (built; no visible change). Checked by poses rendered both ways in the showroom.
2. **Shared skeletons, layers and the picker** (built). Tagged moves come from a model's `moves` in its config (act, weapon, side, mask, additive and loops per glTF animation), additive moves baked as changes from their first frame; no model has any yet. Checked by poses rendered both ways in the showroom, and `--showroom-clip march` (attack on the upper body over a walk) shows a masked layer.
3. **Look-at and foot IK** (built). Checked on a test map with a steep hill, from a low camera, with the IK on and off (the uphill foot rises, the body drops to the downhill one), and with the look demo. It shows in the game since the units' re-bake (stage 4).
4. **Units re-bake.** Part 1 (built, 2026-10-24): every unit model re-baked with the new tables, from unchanged sources and config. All 55 models are fitted to a shared skeleton, so the pack carries no bone rows and every soldier is posed by the new pass, with foot IK on slopes and the feet settling as they fall. The pack's bytes match the one checked in stage 3. The baker version is shared with the building packs, so they were re-baked too, from unchanged configs: the only change is each vertex's unused detail byte, which older bakes wrote as 255 and today's baker writes as 0 (both mean no detail), so they look the same. Part 2: the units' surface detail (`detail_tile` about 0.15). Each unit glTF first needs its faces tagged with surface classes in `COLOR_0` (doc 31), which is modelling work in Blender.
5. **Fight library**, Ember Clans first (about 50 moves for sword and shield and for spear), from Mixamo sets Mario downloads and Blender fitting; then the other human weapon types, Brood and Choir bodies.
