# 33 · Animation engine

*Stage 3 of [28 · Soldier combat](28-soldier-combat.md), the engine half of the "Materials, animation engine and fight library" thread of the [restart plan](29-restart-plan.md). Each part is marked built when it lands.*

## Where it started

A soldier played one clip at a time. `gc_artbake` sampled each clip at 12 frames a second into the model's own rows of the bone animation buffer (a 3×4 matrix per bone, bind pose already folded in), the instance named two rows and a blend, and `cs_skin` blended each vertex's bones from those two rows. The game picked a clip by `ClipKind` (ten kinds) through `gc_game::models::Clips`. So every model carried its own copy of the stock clips, a soldier could not swing his arms while his legs walked, and a flinch could not lie on top of a swing.

## What the engine adds

- **A pose pass (built).** `cs_pose` in `world.wgsl` runs before skinning: one workgroup of 64 threads per soldier writes his final bone matrices once into a palette buffer (48 bytes a bone; it starts at 64 Ki bones and grows by powers of two up to 1 Mi, 48 MB), and `cs_skin` only reads the palette. Before, every vertex re-blended its bones' frames itself. Both passes share one compute pass and one bind group (palette at binding 15, the animation tables at 16). The planner cuts soldiers for the palette's cap the same way as for the vertex buffers.
- **Shared skeletons, clips as joint poses (built).** Models on the same skeleton (same joint names and parents; the Quaternius rig under most humanoids, each Brood creature its own) share one set of moves. A move frame stores, per joint, a rotation and a translation plus uniform scale relative to its parent joint, as eight half floats (16 bytes a joint, the precision the bone rows had), and each skeleton's frames sit in a block of their own, rows as long as it has joints. Each model keeps a *fit*: the matrix that takes the skeleton to the game's axes and, per bone, the rest inverse, bind fix and axis change the bake used to fold in. The pass rebuilds the joints' global matrices level by level in workgroup memory (a barrier per depth; the roots take the model's turn and size) and ends each bone with its fix. The halves are decoded with bit arithmetic, since the shading language's own unpacking needs a capability not every GPU has. A model whose clips squash or shear a joint (not expressible as a rotation, translation and uniform scale within 0.001) keeps its own bone rows and the old path, so nothing changes for it.
- **Layers (built in the pass).** Each instance carries up to three layers on top of its base frames. A layer names two frame rows, a blend, a weight, a bone mask (all, legs, upper body, head, left arm, right arm) and whether it is *additive*. A plain layer mixes towards its own pose on the joints of its mask (legs from a walk, arms from a swing); an additive layer adds its change from its first frame, scaled by weight (a flinch on top of anything). Masks and left/right mirror pairs come from the joint names (Quaternius `.L/.R`, Mixamo `Left/Right`). Models still on bone rows take whole-body plain layers only, as a matrix blend.
- **Moves, not kinds.** A move carries tags: an act (idle, walk, run, guard, attack, parry, block, hit, stagger, knockdown, get up, death, cheer, shoot, step, flinch, and the two halves of a matched kill, kill and killed), a weapon type, a side (front, back, left, right, high, low), a mask and flags (loops, additive, mirrors). Today's ten clip kinds become one move each.
- **A move picker (built, `gc_game/src/moves.rs`).** `Moves::pick(model, want)` takes what the fight wants (an act, a weapon, the side a blow came from, a seed) and returns the best move the model has: the first act along a fallback list that the model has a move for (a parry falls back to an attack, raised partway; a block to a parry; a stagger to a hit; a knockdown to a death), then the move whose weapon and side fit best, then the seed among equals so a regiment does not move as one. A move made for a particular weapon or side is only picked when the fight names it, so a soldier whose weapon is not named never borrows a spear's parry from another model on his skeleton. A model offers its own clips first and, when fitted, its skeleton's tagged moves. Layered moves (additive, or on part of the body) are never a base move: they are picked only when a layer of that very act is asked for. `gc_game::models::Clips` now picks every soldier's clips through it, and a test holds today's soldiers to exactly the clips they played before. T4 keeps `puppets.rs`; the picker is what grows underneath it.
- **Feet on the ground (built in the pass).** After the joints' matrices are built, one thread of the soldier's workgroup bends his legs to the ground: it finds the ground under each foot (as the skinning pass stands him, on the terrain or the water line), drops the whole body to the lower foot's ground (never raising it, so in a dip both legs bend) and raises each foot to its own. The leg is solved exactly: the knee turns about its hinge until hip and foot are as far apart as hip and goal, then the leg swings at the hip onto the goal. The hinge is the leg's own bending plane while the knee is forward of the hip-to-foot line and else runs across the leg, so a straight or slightly overstraightened knee (common in motion capture) still bends forward. The foot keeps the turn the move gave it. A foot counts only while it is planted: from 0.3 to 0.6 of the leg's reach above the ground it fades out, so a leap or a kick is left alone and a walking foot eases in and out. Each foot's ground counts up to 0.4 of the reach, and no foot is raised more than that. `SoldierInstance::feet` says how much the feet keep to the move instead (0 to `FEET_AS_POSED`): the game fades it up as a soldier is knocked down (and back as he gets up) and over the first 0.3 seconds of a death, so nothing snaps. It never runs on a model lying flat, lifted or sinking, and costs nothing for a soldier who neither bends nor looks.
- **Look-at (built in the pass).** An instance can name where a soldier looks, as a yaw (up to a quarter turn either way) and a pitch (up to an eighth of a turn up or down) and how much of it shows. The same thread shares the turn out over chest (0.3), neck (0.3) and head (0.4), each about its own joint, so the arms and the weapon turn with the chest; a rig missing one gives its share to the others. `--showroom-clip look` idles while looking round. Each joint turns by its share more than the one below, so the head ends looking exactly as asked, without tilting.
- **The rig** for both is worked out per skeleton when the pack is uploaded: the joints named as legs (only when both legs are a chain of upper leg, lower leg and foot), chest, neck and head, and for each joint the nearest of those at or above it, whose fix it takes. Brood bodies (six legs, no such names) and the rigid siege engines get none and stand as before.
- **Still to come:** the game aiming heads (at a foe, at the commander); mixed speeds and mirrors per soldier; foot IK for the Brood's six legs and for horses.

## Format: optional tables, packs stay readable

The model schema stays 4. A pack may carry six optional tables (`anim/skeletons`, `anim/joints`, `anim/moves`, `anim/frames`, `anim/fits`, `anim/fixes`, format in `gc_assets::anim`, their own `ANIM_SCHEMA` 1); a pack without them, such as every building and scenery pack, loads as before. A seventh, `anim/pairs` (the matched kills), is optional even beside the others, so a pack with no kills leaves it out and its bytes do not change. A model's clips are pose rows of its skeleton's block when it has a fit, else bone rows of its own. Models share a skeleton only when their joints, parents and rest pose all match, since a move's poses carry its bones' lengths; playing one rig's moves on a rig of other proportions (most Mixamo characters share joint names) needs retargeting, which comes with the fight library. Moves are stored once per skeleton (frames deduplicated by content, each move keeping its own record), so a fifty-move library costs its frames once per skeleton instead of once per model, and fitted models drop their bone rows. With every unit model fitted, today's clips take about 3.7 MB as poses against 9.8 MB as bone rows, so `models.pak` shrank from 86.3 MB to 79.7 MB when it was re-baked (2026-10-24), leaving room under GitHub's 100 MB file limit for the fight library. A frame row is counted from the start of its skeleton's block, and an instance names rows in 16 bits, so a skeleton may hold up to 65,536 frames. Each joint carries one bit per mask that covers it; depths are worked out from the parents when the pack is loaded. The engine change re-baked nothing. The units' pack was then re-baked with the new tables on their own (stage 4, part 1), which bumped the baker version to 2; the units' surface detail of [31 · Surface detail](31-surface-detail.md) comes in a second re-bake.

## Stages

1. **Pose pass** (built; no visible change). Checked by poses rendered both ways in the showroom.
2. **Shared skeletons, layers and the picker** (built). Tagged moves come from a model's `moves` in its config (act, weapon, side, mask, additive and loops per glTF animation), additive moves baked as changes from their first frame; no model has any yet. Checked by poses rendered both ways in the showroom, and `--showroom-clip march` (attack on the upper body over a walk) shows a masked layer.
3. **Look-at and foot IK** (built). Checked on a test map with a steep hill, from a low camera, with the IK on and off (the uphill foot rises, the body drops to the downhill one), and with the look demo. It shows in the game since the units' re-bake (stage 4).
4. **Units re-bake.** Part 1 (built, 2026-10-24): every unit model re-baked with the new tables, from unchanged sources and config. All 55 models are fitted to a shared skeleton, so the pack carries no bone rows and every soldier is posed by the new pass, with foot IK on slopes and the feet settling as they fall. The pack's bytes match the one checked in stage 3. The baker version is shared with the building packs, so they were re-baked too, from unchanged configs: the only change is each vertex's unused detail byte, which older bakes wrote as 255 and today's baker writes as 0 (both mean no detail), so they look the same. Part 2: the units' surface detail (`detail_tile` about 0.15). Each unit glTF first needs its faces tagged with surface classes in `COLOR_0` (doc 31), which is modelling work in Blender.
5. **Fight library**, Ember Clans first; then the other human weapon types, Brood and Choir bodies.
   - **Source (decided 2026-10-24, with an Opus check, while Mario was away):** Quaternius's Universal Animation Library 1 and 2, the free Standard files (CC0, see [16](16-art-sourcing.md)), rather than Mixamo, which needs Mario's account. They hold 86 animations on a UE-mannequin-style rig, about 22 of them for fighting (sword cuts and combos, a block, shield guard, bash and dash, hits, a knockback, a death, punches, a roll) and none for the spear. The paid Pro files, or Mixamo, can top the library up later.
   - **Part 1 (built):** a Blender helper retargeted 40 moves onto the Ember men's rig (the Farmer body), in one glTF kept apart from the models, `shared:grubcraft/fight-library/ember-men/ember-men-moves.glb`; scripts, review sheets and the moves table (source, length, suggested tags and contact frame per move) are in the shared folder's `design/faction-looks/fight-library/`. The source's sword moves are one-handed, so each also has a two-handed katana version (the left hand held on the hilt below the right by IK, baked in; the men's arms are shorter, so the sword's path moves in by 14 to 26 cm). Three spear moves (a guard, a thrust driven by the hips and front foot, a parry) are posed by hand from key poses. The baker reads a model's tagged moves from such a file with `moves_from`, after checking it carries the model's rig (every joint by name, under the same parent, in the same rest pose; since the kills across rigs below, the model's own extra joints and the library's spare ones may differ). A model names what it holds with `weapon`, which the pack keeps in the model's fit (`FitRecord::weapon`, a byte that was padding, so older packs read as any weapon), and the picker then prefers the skeleton's moves made for that weapon over the plain clips. An attack may give the second its blow lands (`contact`): the baker retimes it so the blow falls at `gc_assets::anim::BLOW_AT` (0.45) of the move, where the game's swing lands one. In the game now: the Katana Samurai, Spectral Samurai and Ronin stand in a two-handed guard, thrust two-handed, and block and parry two-handed; the Yari Ashigaru stands side-on in a spear guard, thrusts and parries with the shaft. Each had one blow at first, so its first and second attack (and its shot, which falls back to an attack) were the same move; part 2 gives a katana man a second. Walking, hits and deaths are still the models' own clips, and the Oni-Bound's kanabo has no moves yet. A library's channels on nodes that are not the model's joints (an armature's own motion, an extra joint) are left out.
   - **Matched kills (built 2026-10-24):** two acts, `Kill` and `Killed`, for the killer's and the victim's halves of a move made together. Both halves must carry the same `contact` and play once on the whole body. Unlike an attack a kill is not retimed to `BLOW_AT`, since squeezing its wind-up and fall into a swing's shape would spoil them: it plays as made, and its record keeps how far through it the blow lands, so started together and played at one speed the two halves meet there. A kill is longer than a swing, since the victim falls and lies still in it. In `models.ron` both halves name their `pair`, and the killer's half its `reach`, the distance between the two men's origins in the glTF's units; the baker keeps it in the skeleton's space, stores one record per kill in `anim/pairs`, and refuses a pair that lacks a half or whose halves differ in length or contact; the game turns the reach into tiles with the killer's size. The fight asks for kills only through `Clips` (`gc_game/src/models.rs`): `Clips::kill_pair(killer_model, victim_model, seed)` in the tests, and `Clips::kills_of` for a match's unit types; each takes the kills on the killer's skeleton whose victim half is on the victim's, made for the killer's weapon before those made for any, and lets the seed pick among them; it refuses a victim more than a tenth bigger or smaller than the killer (the Oni-Bound shares the men's rig at half as big again), since a kill made for two men of one size would not meet; with none it returns none and the fight plays a plain attack with the victim's own hit and death ([28](28-soldier-combat.md), stage 5). The free library has no kills, so a Blender helper made the first three on the men's rig, each with the two men in one scene: a two-handed katana thrust to the chest (the victim folds, kneels and falls to his side; reach 1.54 m), a katana cut across the chest (he recoils and falls backwards; 1.44 m) and a spear thrust to the belly (he staggers back a step and falls; 3.75 m). At contact the tip sits about 10 cm into the body. They are in `shared:grubcraft/fight-library/ember-men/ember-men-moves-2.glb` with the 40 part 1 moves unchanged, and the scripts, review sheets and measurements in the shared folder's `design/faction-looks/fight-library/kills/` (`KILLS.md`). Every katana and spear man has them, on any man of the rig as victim; the halves are 1.7 to 2 s long, and the killer's ends in his guard and the victim's lying still. They are made procedurally with IK rather than from motion capture, so they are stiffer than the library's clips. Battles play them whenever both men can ([28](28-soldier-combat.md), stage 5, through `Clips::kills_of`).
   - **Part 2, started (2026-10-24):** a second katana blow, `F_Katana_Cut_A`, a library cut joined with its recovery so it starts and ends in the guard, which a katana man now plays as his second attack. The helper also made `F_Katana_Cut_B` and sword-and-shield versions of both, and the library has hits and a death, but they are not tagged yet: the picker chooses once per model and a model's own clip wins a tie, so they would never play until the game varies its picks from blow to blow.
   - **Kills across rigs (built 2026-10-24):** every Choir and Concord man on foot turned out to stand on the Ember men's own joints (the same Quaternius body: names, parents and rest pose match to the last digit), each with a few joints of its own (a Concord weapon and shield, Choir floating crystals) and without the men's fingers; each is still a skeleton of its own, since those extra joints differ. So a library now plays on any rig that shares its joints that way: the baker maps the joints both have by name and checks they hang from one another and rest alike, leaves out the library's joints the model lacks, and keeps the model's own extra joints at rest, as long as no shared joint hangs from one. A Concord weapon or shield hangs from the chest and its own clips reach the hands to it, so a model names it in `held_by` (`"Weapon": "Wrist.R"`), and in library moves the hand carries it as the model's Idle holds it. Moves several models take are written once in `models.ron` as a named `move_sets` entry (a glTF and its tagged moves), which each model lists in `move_sets` and gets its own copy of on its own skeleton. The halves of a kill now pair by name across skeletons, each killer's half with each victim's half of the same name, so the "Kill victims" set (the three victims' halves) gives every Choir and Concord man on foot, the Onmyoji and the Yumi Ashigaru the Ember men's kills as victims: a katana or spear man of the Ember Clans can kill any of them. The Harmonic Walkers (a third bigger than a man) and the riders are left out. The showroom plays a half of a kill with `--showroom-clip kill` (the killer's half of the model's first kill, else a victim's half). Their own kills came next (2026-10-24): a Blender helper made four on the killers' own rigs, each with an Ember man as the victim, both men in one scene: the Bone Pikes' two-handed pike thrust (reach 4.4 m, the pike being about 4.5 m long), the Grubfolk Militia's spear thrust past the buckler (2.8 m), the Gravewardens' mace smash with the tower shield up (1.65 m) and the Shardlings' lunging forearm-blade thrust (1.72 m); 1.7 to 1.9 s long, the blow landing 0.33 to 0.43 s in, the tip about 10 cm into the body, and the killer starting and ending in his own Idle. Each killer's half is in a library on his own rig (`shared:grubcraft/fight-library/foes/<model>-kills.glb`, keyed on its weapon and shield joints, which it moves itself, so `held_by` stands aside for it), and the victims' halves are in `ember-men/ember-men-victims-2.glb` on the men's rig, which the "Kill victims 2" set gives to every man on foot that has the first, so a Concord or Choir soldier can kill an Ember man or any other. Notes, measurements and review sheets are in the shared folder's `design/faction-looks/fight-library/kills-2/` (`KILLS-2.md`). The victim slides a few centimetres while staggering in the militia kill, and the mace meets the head on one frame only.
   - **Finishers (built 2026-10-24):** a blow struck down at a man already lying on his back, asked for by the fight's finishers ([28](28-soldier-combat.md), stage 5), which bend a plain swing down for now. A new act, `Finish`, carries a `contact` like an attack and is retimed the same way, so its blow lands at `BLOW_AT`; like a kill it must have one and play once on the whole body. A Blender helper made three on the Ember men's rig, in `shared:grubcraft/fight-library/ember-men/ember-men-finishers-1.glb` (the "Ember finishers" set): a two-handed katana stab and an overhead katana chop at a man whose chest is 0.91 m in front of the killer (the killer crouches deep, his back leaning in), and a low yari stab from 3.38 m, since a yari's tip is 3.3 m from the hands that hold it; each 1.5 to 1.6 s, from and back to the guard, the tip about 9 cm into the chest armour. The fight is to ask for one through `Clips::finisher(model, seed)` (`gc_game/src/models.rs`), which gives the move and how far in front of the killer the lying man's chest should be, in tiles at the killer's size (the reach is per weapon in the code, `finish_reach`, as the moves were made); it gives none for a model without finishers (the Oni-Bound and the other factions, so far). Notes, measurements and review sheets are in the shared folder's `design/faction-looks/fight-library/finishers/` (`FINISHERS.md`). The showroom plays a model's first with `--showroom-clip finish`. The helper found that the library's spear guard (`F_Spear_Guard`) has its two foot bones well off the legs in its first frame, as the kills' helper had noted; the spear finisher starts and ends on a corrected guard, so its feet differ from the guard's there (the game's feet pass hides most of it).
   - **Still to come:** more single blows and kills (about six kills per weapon), cut from the combos; the game varying a soldier's blows from blow to blow (the picker still chooses once per model); hits and deaths from the library per weapon; sword-and-shield units; kills made for the other factions' weapons; kills for the riders and the Brood, whose rigs are their own.
