# 23 · Choir models

The Shard Choir is the crystal faction of Grubcraft. Its units are crystal constructs: bodies of frosted glass, glowing with cyan veins, with violet accents and parts that float free of the body. This document covers its eleven 3D models: how they are made, where the sources live, and the numbers behind them. The look itself is described in [09 · Art and Audio](09-art-and-audio.md), and the credits for the free packs we build on are in [CREDITS.md](../CREDITS.md).

**Built so far:** all eleven models are built to final quality and listed in `assets-src/models.ron`; Shardlings replace the old Alien stand-in. The game draws only one of them today (see [Known limits](#known-limits)).

## What this is

The Choir's style is called Cathedral glass. Each body looks like frosted glass with light inside it, set in cold pewter frames. Four things carry the look across all eleven models:

- **Frosted glass bodies** are the main surface of each unit. Violet glass (`#8A5CF6`) makes the accents, and pewter (a grey metal) makes the trim.
- **Vein glow** (`#3FD8E8`) is the cyan light that runs through the glass in thin veins, like light caught in a crystal.
- **Floating parts** such as heads, shoulder shards, crowns, lenses and rings hover a little apart from the body and drift slowly.
- **Team colour** is the colour of the player who owns the unit. It sits on enamel bands and on a floating team triangle, so you can tell whose unit it is at a glance. Everything else keeps its own colour.

The eleven models cover the faction's whole roster: a worker, the line soldiers, the ranged and heavy troops, the cavalry, the flying and hovering units, a support priest, a monster and three heroes.

## How they are made

Each model is made by a Python script rather than by hand in a 3D editor, so anyone can rebuild it and get the same result. The steps, in order:

1. **Blender, run from code.** Blender is an open-source 3D modelling program. We use its Python module, `bpy`, which is installed with pip into a virtual environment (a private folder of Python packages called `bvenv`). Each script builds one model from nothing.
2. **Stock bodies and rigs.** The humanoids start from Quaternius's CC0 Farmer rig. CC0 means the files are free to use with no conditions. A *rig* is the skeleton of bones inside a model that moves it. The rig has 62 bones. The body mesh (the skin of the model) is removed, and the finger bones are dropped, because crystal hands do not open. The stock animation clips (named animations such as Walk, Run and Die) still play on the rig, with a few extra ones added by the Choir scripts.
3. **Rigid crystals.** Every crystal is a faceted gem fixed to one bone. Nothing stretches. The gaps at the joints are part of the look. `choir.py` builds the crystals and their materials: frosted glass with a cell-like vein glow (a pattern called Voronoi), violet glass, pewter and team enamel. Materials are *procedural*: generated by code from rules, not painted by hand.
4. **Floating parts.** Floating parts sit on extra bones named `Float.*`. `animate_floats` adds a slow drift to them, a small lag behind the body when it moves, and a fall to the ground in the death clips. It adds this to every clip. The motion is *keyed*, meaning it is set at chosen frames, not worked out by a physics engine.
5. **Glass Striders on a horse.** The Glass Striders use Quaternius's CC0 Horse, rebuilt as one convex crystal for each horse bone. Convex means each crystal bulges outward with no dents. `mount.py` puts the rider on the horse, so the rider and the horse share one 60-bone rig.
6. **Hovering units.** Skyglass and Monolith each have their own small rig, with clips made in their own scripts. They rest on the ground in the rest pose. Their clips then lift them into a hover. The game has no flying layer, so the hover comes from the clips alone.
7. **Game mesh and baked maps.** `lowpoly.py` turns the high-poly build into a much simpler game mesh and *bakes* the surface detail into texture images, which means it copies the detail onto the simpler mesh. The maps are albedo (base colour), team (which parts take the team colour), normal (fake surface bumps for light to catch), ORM (occlusion, roughness and metalness, which control how light sits on the surface) and emissive (where the model glows). The baker keeps only the emitted colour, so the strength of a glow is written into the colour itself.
8. **Four levels of detail, then the pack.** `gc_artbake` makes four versions of each model with fewer triangles each: LOD 0 (level of detail 0) is the closest and most detailed, and LOD 3 is the simplest, for the farthest view. It then writes all the models into `assets/models.pak`, the pack the game reads.

## Budgets

Every model is held to a *budget*: a maximum triangle count for each of its four LODs. The bake checks each level against its budget. The budget depends on what kind of unit the model is.

| Budget class | Models | LOD 0 | LOD 1 | LOD 2 | LOD 3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Soldier class | Tuner, Shardlings, Prism Lancers, Harmonic Walkers, Skyglass | 8,400 | 3,000 | 1,000 | 350 |
| Hero | Conductor, The Maestro, The Refractor, The Hollow Bell | 12,000 | 4,000 | 1,400 | 450 |
| Rider and mount | Glass Striders | 14,000 | 5,000 | 1,700 | 550 |
| Single big model | Monolith | 20,000 | 7,000 | 2,400 | 800 |

The faceted style is naturally low-poly, so the source meshes sit well under these budgets.

**Height rule.** A model's height in `assets-src/models.ron` is set from its size in its rest pose, which is the neutral pose it stands in before any animation. Take the height of the smallest box that holds the model in that pose, multiply it by 1.6, and divide by 2.64. This is the rule the Yari Ashigaru was scaled with, so the Choir models share its scale on the field. Two units are drawn larger on purpose: Harmonic Walkers at 1.3 times the rule, and Skyglass at 1.4 times.

## Where the sources live

The sources live in the shared project folder, not in the repository.

- **Build scripts and helpers:** `/mnt/project-files/design/faction-looks/choir-roster/source/`. One script per unit is in `units/`. The helpers are `kit.py` (the shared parts, materials and rig helpers), `choir.py` (the Choir materials and crystals), `lowpoly.py` (high-poly to game mesh and maps), `mount.py` (the rider and horse rig), `stage.py` (the shared render lights, ground and camera), `review.py` and `preview.py` (contact sheets of renders), `bake.sh` (the build script for one model) and `gen_models.py` (writes the `models.ron` entries and `facts.json`).
- **Facts for each model:** `/mnt/project-files/design/faction-looks/choir-roster/facts.json`, with the name, slug, triangle and bone counts, height, budget, clips and script for every model.
- **Game-ready files** (the glTF model and its PNG maps): `/mnt/project-files/assets-src/grubcraft/choir/<slug>/`.
- **Review renders** (pictures made to check each model): `/mnt/project-files/design/faction-looks/choir-roster/review/<slug>/`.

## Rebuilding one model

The build tools need Blender's Python module (`pip install bpy` in a virtual environment called `bvenv`) and Quaternius's CC0 Farmer and Horse files.

1. From the `source/` folder, run `bash bake.sh <script> <slug> <LOD 0 budget> <work folder>`. The LOD 0 budget is the first number in the budget table for that unit. The script builds `units/<script>.py` as a Blender file, runs `lowpoly.py` to make the game mesh and its maps, and copies the result to `/mnt/project-files/assets-src/grubcraft/choir/<slug>/`. The script names are in `facts.json`.
2. Run `python3 gen_models.py <work folder>`. It prints the `models.ron` entries for the models built so far and writes `facts.json`. Copy the entry for the model into `assets-src/models.ron`, and check its path, height and maps.
3. From the repository root, run `cargo run --release -p gc_artbake`. This rewrites `assets/models.pak` and `assets/models.manifest`. Commit those with the `models.ron` change, as [09 · Art and Audio](09-art-and-audio.md) describes.

## Known limits

- **Only one of the eleven is drawn in the game today.** The game draws models only for unit types listed in `data/units.ron`, and Shardlings is the only Choir unit listed there. The rest appear in the Unit viewer on the main menu and in the `--showroom all` view, which shows every model.
- **Floating parts are rigid and keyed, not physics.** They drift, lag and fall because their motion is set at chosen frames. Nothing is simulated.
- **Glass is opaque.** The engine has no transparency for soldiers, so the glass is suggested by colour, veins and glow, not by seeing through it.
- **Skyglass and Monolith hover by their clips only.** The game has no flying layer, so their hover is what their clips show and nothing more.
- **Rigs are capped at 63 bones.** The Glass Striders' rider and horse use 60 of them, close to the limit.
