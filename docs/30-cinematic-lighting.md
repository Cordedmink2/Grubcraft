# 30 · Cinematic lighting

*Plan from the "Grubcraft Lighting Study" (2026-10-22); stage 1 built 2026-10-23. The illustrated study is the "Grubcraft Lighting Study" page (https://claude.ai/artifact/MwkJpkNDwSN9XY9Kry8QVf), with the throwaway prototype and its screenshots in the project's shared folder under `design/cinematic/`.*

Players judge a scene by how the light behaves long before they count polygons. Grubcraft was lit like a flat noon in thick haze, so it read grey. This plan changes the light, the air and the colour, with the same models, and keeps everything render only: nothing here touches the simulation, so golden replays and multiplayer sync are unaffected. It is the "lighting presets per map, rim light, colour-graded look, shared particle atlas" that [09 · Art and Audio](09-art-and-audio.md) already promises.

## The stages

1. **Light and Air (built, 2026-10-23).** See [Built: stage 1](#built-stage-1).
2. **Clean edges (built, 2026-10-24, except two items).** See [Built: stage 2](#built-stage-2). Anti-aliasing, softer sun shadows, contact shadows under soldiers, water that reflects the sky, and light motes. Hit flash belongs to [28 · Every Soldier Fights](28-soldier-combat.md) (it is a soldier's own moment) and trampled ground to [27 · Living Land](27-living-land.md) and the living-ground work (it is ground that remembers), so neither is built here.
3. **Dust and Smoke (built, 2026-10-23).** See [Built: stage 3](#built-stage-3). One GPU particle system with a shared atlas: dust trails, hit sparks, Brood ichor, Choir crystal shards, Concord ghost-light motes, smoke, fire, embers, debris. [26 · Fortifications](26-fortifications.md) stage 7 and several other threads use it, so it comes first in wave 2.
4. **Camera craft (built, 2026-10-24).** See [Built: stage 4, shake and bars](#built-stage-4-shake-and-bars), [Built: stage 4, the director camera](#built-stage-4-the-director-camera) and [Built: stage 4, tilt-shift and sun shafts](#built-stage-4-tilt-shift-and-sun-shafts). Screen shake, the battle-start letterbox, the director camera and replay films (with slow motion, for watched replays only), a switchable tilt-shift look and sun shafts for the ride camera.
5. **Weather and night (parts 1, 2, 3a and 3b built, 2026-10-24).** See [Built: stage 5, the look of weather and night](#built-stage-5-the-look-of-weather-and-night). Rain, snow and fog, and a Night look, as a render preview. Part 2 adds point lights from lit windows and fires, and part 3a wet ground, rain splashes and lightning. Part 3b makes the picture follow the simulation's weather and clock ([32](32-changing-land.md)). Still to do: torches and Grub as lights, river fog banks at dawn, wind that follows the simulation's, a bolt drawn and thunder.

The camera tilt and backlit foliage came with stage 1. A look per land, the lobby's time of day, Clarity and a tuning panel followed stage 2: see [Built: a look per land](#built-a-look-per-land). Not on the list on purpose: ray tracing, screen-space reflections, volumetric fog volumes, temporal anti-aliasing (it smears hundreds of thin spears) and real-time global illumination; each costs a lot on a Mac for little gain in a top-down game.

## Built: stage 1

The light is one `Look` (`gc_render/src/look.rs`): plain numbers for the sun's direction and colour, the sky and ground ambient light, the air (colour, haze toward the sun, density, how fast it thins with height), rim light, how much leaves glow, cloud shadows, ground occlusion, and the grade (exposure, contrast, saturation, vignette, film grain, a cool tint for the dark end and a warm one for the bright end), and the colours of the sky. `Renderer::set_look` takes one; nothing is read from files.

**Five prepared looks** (`LookPreset`), the game's default being Golden hour:

| Look | What it is |
|---|---|
| Noon | A high sun in clear air: short shadows, light haze |
| Golden hour | A low warm sun behind the field (about 21° up), blue shade, long shadows, warm haze toward the sun, slow cloud shadows, strong rim light |
| Overcast | Soft grey light, thick air, few shadows, desaturated |
| Storm | Steel light, fast dark cloud shadows, low colour, high contrast, heavy vignette |
| Dusk | The sun just above the hills, violet shade, an orange sky |

**What draws it:**

- **Sky.** One triangle at the far plane, drawn last in the world pass (so the HDR image keeps its 4 bytes a pixel on a Mac, and the depth test keeps it off every pixel the world covers): a gradient from the horizon colour to the colour overhead, a glow and a disc round the sun. At the usual bird's-eye tilt it hardly shows; it is there at the horizon in the ride camera, close zoom, and in the void past a map's edge.
- **Height fog.** Replaces the flat distance fog that greyed everything. The air is thick in low ground and thin on hills (density falls off with height, integrated along the view ray) and is warmer toward the sun.
- **Film curve and grade.** The composite pass applies a filmic curve (the ACES fit) to the HDR image, then split toning, saturation, contrast and a vignette. A little film grain (strongest in the mid tones) and a one-step triangular dither run every frame, which also removes colour banding in the sky.
- **Cloud shadows.** Soft patches drifting over the land (the `sunlight` function in `world.wgsl`).
- **Rim light** on soldiers and buildings, strongest where the sun is behind them.
- **Backlit foliage.** Leaves and blades (scenery that sways in the wind) glow yellow-green when the sun is behind them.
- **Ground occlusion.** Valleys and the foot of slopes get less light. It is baked once per map from the heights alone (`look::ground_occlusion`: 16 horizon samples a tile, in parallel, about 0.1 s on a 768-tile map) into a one-byte-per-tile texture, 0.6 MB on the biggest map.
- **Zoom-linked camera tilt** (`gc_game/src/camera.rs`). The bird's-eye camera looks down at 55° from the default zoom out to the widest, and eases to 30° at the closest zoom, so zooming in brings the scene toward eye level and the sky starts to show. The unit viewer and the showroom keep their own pitch.

**Using it:** `--look noon|golden-hour|overcast|storm|dusk` opens with a look; **F9** steps to the next one while playing (it is in the Marshal's Scroll under View). Colours that are drawn as they appear (fog, haze, sky) are written in sRGB in a `Look` and converted to HDR by `Look::hdr`, the inverse of the film curve, so they land near what was written.

**Cost.** No new full-screen pass: the sky is a triangle inside the world pass and everything else is folded into the world and composite shaders that already ran. The bloom is a little stronger than before to suit the new curve. Memory: the occlusion texture (up to 0.6 MB) and a 48-byte grade buffer.

## Built: stage 2

- **Anti-aliasing.** The world pass draws the HDR image with four samples a pixel and resolves it, so the edges of spears, tree crowns, roofs and rocks are clean and thin shapes do not shimmer as the camera moves. Every world pipeline (terrain, scenery, soldiers, boxes, sky, particles) takes the sample count; the shadow map and the screen pass stay at one. On a Mac the multisampled colour and depth are transient (they live in the GPU's tile memory, zero bytes of RAM) and only the resolved image is kept; elsewhere they cost 4 × the frame's pixels × (4 + 4) bytes. Where the GPU cannot draw the HDR format multisampled the renderer falls back to no anti-aliasing. `--no-aa` turns it off (`RendererOptions::msaa`, 4 or 1); it is not yet a Graphics preset, because the presets are read after the renderer starts. Every lit varying in the world shaders is `centroid`-interpolated: without that, a pixel on the edge of a thin triangle (a blade of grass) is shaded outside the triangle, comes out as not-a-number, and the glow spreads it over the screen.
- **Softer sun shadows.** The shadow lookup takes eight filtered taps on a fixed spiral over a disc 1.6 texels across, in place of four (`sunlight` in `world.wgsl`, `SHADOW_SOFTNESS`); the pattern is fixed so moving shadows do not crawl. Glows and particles, which only tint, take one tap (`sunlight_soft`).
- **Contact shadows.** A soft dark patch on the ground under every soldier (`vs_contact` and `fs_contact`, one four-vertex strip per soldier from the soldier instance buffer, drawn after the soldiers, so they hide it). It works in any light, including the overcast and storm looks where the sun's shadows are faint, and under the feet where the shadow map is too coarse. It follows the ground height and fades with height lifted, with distance in the air and where fog of war hides the ground. Only soldiers drawn at the two finest levels of detail get one (farther off it is a pixel). Size `CONTACT_RADIUS` (0.34 tile at scale 1), strength `CONTACT_DARK`.
- **Water that mirrors the sky.** The surface has gentle ripples that drift (`water_normal`), mirrors the sky colour more the lower the camera looks (Schlick-like, at least 10%), and catches the sun in glints that the glow blooms (`sky_color`, shared with the sky pass).
- **Light motes.** Specks of pollen and dust hanging in the light near a close camera: warm, tiny, additive, drifting up and with the wind for 5 to 8 seconds (`Effects::light_motes`, raised in `gc_game/src/effects.rs`). They show below 25 tiles of camera distance and fade out by 60 (and in the ride camera, a little ahead of the rider), each following the land's height, at most 40 a second, thin with the Graphics preset like every effect, and are never made while the game is paused.
- **Dev flag** `--distance 14..260` starts a match with the camera that far from the ground, for close-up screenshots.

**Not measured:** cost and looks on a real Mac. By estimate the anti-aliasing is nearly free on Apple GPUs (tile memory, resolved on chip), contact shadows add one tiny quad per soldier (30,000 soldiers, about 0.1 ms), and the eight-tap shadow filter doubles the shadow lookups (which also run per particle vertex). `--no-aa` and `--no-shadows` measure them. Known limits: the resolve averages the HDR values before tone mapping, so very bright glints can still shimmer a little at their edge; no soft contact shadow under buildings, rocks or trees.

## Built: stage 3

One particle system for every effect, in `gc_render` (`particles.rs`, `effects.rs`, `shaders/particles.wgsl`). Render only: nothing here touches the simulation, and the game never makes an effect for something the viewer cannot see.

**How it works.** The CPU never moves a particle. `Renderer::spawn_particles(&[Particle])` gives each one its start, velocity, forces (`gravity`, `drag`, `lift`), size and colour at birth and at death, shape and flags, once. They go into a ring buffer of `MAX_PARTICLES` (32,768 since weather; 16,384 before) 64-byte slots, the oldest overwritten first (2 MB), and the vertex shader works out where each particle is from its age, in closed form (constant force with drag), so a burst costs the CPU nothing after it is spawned. Particles age by the **effects clock**, which follows the frame clock and stops when the game is paused or behind the menu and runs slow in slow motion (`Renderer::set_effects_speed`); `Particle::age` back-dates one that began between frames.

**What draws it.** One instanced draw (two when the ring wraps) of the slots that may still hold a live particle (the ring tracks when each batch is certainly dead: a life is capped at 8 seconds), four-vertex strips, in the world pass after the sky, tested against the world's depth and writing none (so there is no extra pass or render target). Each particle is a camera-facing quad (spinning, growing, fading in fast and out slowly), or with `STRETCH` a streak along its velocity with its head at the particle. Pictures come from one 512 × 256 single-channel atlas (128 KB with mips) made in code at start-up (`particles::atlas`): four ragged puffs, a soft glow, a streak, a crystal shard and a ring. The lighting is the world's: the sun with its shadows and the cloud shadows sampled at the particle's centre, sky light, a sphere-shaded face so puffs have a lit and a dark side, a glow when the particle is between the eye and the low sun, then the height fog, and a fade where the fog of war hides the ground. Blending is premultiplied: dust and smoke blend by alpha, and `ADDITIVE` particles (fire, sparks, motes, shards) add HDR light, which the glow then blooms. Flags: `STRETCH`, `ADDITIVE`, `GROUND` (come to rest on the ground), `SOFT` (fade out toward the ground under the particle, in place of a depth fade, which would need a second pass), `WIND` and `FLICKER`.

**Recipes** (`Effects`, which collects particles for the frame; `take()` hands them to the renderer): `footstep`, `dust_burst`, `smoke`, `flames`, `embers`, `sparks`, `debris`, `spray`, `shards`, `motes`, and `hit(pos, dir, HitStyle, armoured, power)` which picks by whose wound it is: **Flesh** (blood drops; Ember Clans and Iron Concord), **Ichor** (yellow-green drops and motes; the Brood), **Crystal** (glittering shards; the Choir), **Ghost** (pale motes; the Concord's summoned dead) and **Ember** (embers and sparks). Recipes thin themselves with `set_density` (the Graphics preset: High 1, Balanced 0.75, Battery 0.35) and draw their randomness from their own generator, never the simulation's.

**In the game** (`gc_game/src/effects.rs`): marching soldiers kick up dust (about a puff a second at a walk, more at a run, from every soldier on screen), arrows raise a puff and a few clods where a volley comes down, and a charge that hits home rolls a cloud and throws clods. Effects of regiments the viewer cannot see are not made. Building hits, fire, smoke and collapse use them since stage 7 part 1 of [26](26-fortifications.md) (`gc_game/src/siege.rs`, with the recipes `chips`, `trail` and `dust_cloud` added for it). Sparks and wounds of soldiers come from the puppets' blows ([28](28-soldier-combat.md) stage 6, `Field::blow` in `gc_game/src/effects.rs`); crows and fallen standards are boxes and scenery models, not particles ([28](28-soldier-combat.md), "Aftermath and wear"); the ambient motes are still to come. `--fx-demo` plays every effect in a row at the camera's focus at a quarter speed, and `--fx-still` freezes the same gallery at four ages for screenshots (`gc_game/src/fx_demo.rs`).

**Cost.** One draw call, 2 MB of ring buffer and 0.2 MB of atlas. Vertex work is 4 vertices per slot with a terrain height lookup and a few shadow taps; the fragment cost is overdraw, so smoke is made of few, large, faint puffs. About 1,000 puffs of dust in a big battle is the expected load; the ring and the density keep the worst case bounded. Measured on a Mac when Mario tries it.

**Limits.** No soft depth fade against buildings and scenery, only against the ground (a second pass would be needed). Dust colour is one tan; it will follow the ground tint of the land later.

## Built: a look per land

*Built 2026-10-24, the pull request after stage 2. Render and lobby only: the simulation never reads any of it, so golden replays are unchanged.*

**Each land brings its own air** (`gc_game/src/atmosphere.rs`). A land changes whatever look is in use rather than replacing it, so every land follows every time of day: a `LandAir` (`gc_render/src/look.rs`) adds to the air, haze and sky colours and multiplies the air's thickness, how tightly it hugs low ground, saturation, contrast, the sun's colour and the split tints. Meadow is the look as it is.

| Land | Its air |
|---|---|
| Waste | A dusty amber haze: warmer air and sky, thicker air (×1.35), a yellower sun, less colour |
| Fen | A green mist that hugs the low ground (×1.8 thick, ×1.5 lower), a greener sun, less colour |
| Highland | Cold clear air: cooler air and sky, thin air (×0.55) so the far hills show, a bluer sun and shade, a little more contrast |
| Forest | Darker, greener shade under a slightly thicker air |
| Valley | A river's slight green mist |

The lands blend with the camera, not one look per map, because a grown map is a patchwork: nine by nine samples over the ground round the camera (0.6 of its distance from the ground, 20 to 70 tiles; the middle counts most) give each land a share, the shares ease toward the new ones over a few seconds (`SETTLE`, 2.5 s), and the airs are mixed by share. So scrolling from a meadow into the Waste turns the haze amber over a couple of seconds, and a match starts right in the air of the lands round its first view. In the ride camera the samples follow the rider's view.

**Time of day, in the lobby.** The host picks **Golden hour** (the default), **Noon**, **Dusk** or **Overcast** on a "Time of day" stepper at the right of the lobby's map name; the other players see it as text. It is one of the lobby's settings (`LobbySettings::time_of_day`, sent to every player, net protocol 26), so everyone plays in the same light. Storm is left for the weather of stage 5. `--look NAME` overrides it for every match, and **F9** still steps through the five prepared looks during play. Matches outside a lobby (the sandbox, `--battle`, replays) open in golden hour. Since the weather (2026-10-24, [32](32-changing-land.md#weather-and-the-time-of-day-ideas-1-and-9)) the time of day also starts the simulation's clock, which then runs (night falls on a long dusk battle and cuts sight and range), a fifth value **Night** starts at 23:00 (drawn with the Night look), and a **Weather** stepper sits beside it.

**Clarity** (Settings, Display: "Clarity: plain light, no grain"). For competitive play: no vignette, no film grain, no split toning, contrast at most 1.05 and saturation between 1 and 1.1, in every look and every land. The light itself (the sun, the shade, the air) stays, so the world still has depth; banners, team colours and fog of war read the same everywhere. It applies at once and is saved with the settings.

**The tuning panel** (F10 during play), for tuning the looks by eye on a Mac. It lists sixteen knobs (the sun's height and heading in degrees, the sun's strength, sky light, exposure, contrast, saturation, vignette, film grain, air thickness, how tightly the air hugs the ground, rim light, leaf glow, cloud shadows, valley shade, and warmth) with their values now. Up and Down choose one, Left and Right move it (Shift for ten steps, as in the sandbox's tuner), Backspace puts it back (Shift+Backspace puts all back), **L** forces one land's air everywhere in turn (to see the Waste's look on any ground; the dev flag `--air waste` starts that way), and Enter writes every value to the log, with how far each was moved, ready to be copied into a look. The knobs move the finished look (after the land's air and Clarity), so the values shown are the ones on screen. The panel keeps the arrow keys while it is open.

**Checked:** in the cloud's software renderer, each land's air on the same view (`screenshots/look-per-land/land-airs.png` in the project's shared folder) and Storm with and without Clarity (`clarity.png`). **Not yet:** the factions' own lands (the Brood's veins, the Choir's fields, [33](33-faction-lands.md)) do not change the air. **Not checked yet:** the looks and readability (team colours, selection rings, fog of war) under every look and land on a real screen; Mario's Mac with the tuning panel is where the numbers get settled. Cost: 81 lookups of the land grid and a few dozen multiplications a frame.

## Built: stage 4, shake and bars

*Built 2026-10-24. Presentation only: the picture is moved after the world is drawn, nothing reaches the simulation, so golden replays and multiplayer sync are unaffected.*

**Screen shake** (`gc_game/src/cinema.rs`). A charge that hits home, an ambush sprung and a building that falls each add to a "how shaken is the screen" number (`trauma`, 0 to 1; 0.35, 0.3 and 0.5 a blow). It dies away at 1.5 a second, and the picture moves by its square, so a small blow is a small tremble and a pile-up of blows is a real shake. A blow is felt less the farther it is from the camera's focus (nothing beyond 30 tiles plus half the camera's distance) and more the closer the camera is (0.4 to 1.6 times). At full shake the picture moves 0.7 % of its size, with a slight zoom so the edge of the screen never shows. It is the composite pass that does it (`Renderer::set_cinema`, `camera` in the grade block of `post.wgsl`), so **the HUD stays still, and clicks land where they were aimed**. **Settings, Accessibility, "Reduced screen shake"** switches it off (this is the first setting of that name that does anything).

**Letterbox bars.** When the deployment ends and the battle begins, a dark bar slides in at the top and bottom of the world view (8.5 % of the screen each, 0.7 s in, 2.2 s held, 0.9 s out), then goes. The HUD is drawn after the bars, so it is never covered. Clarity turns the bars off, as it does the other showmanship. A player rejoining an online match is caught up through the old fights without shake or bars.

**Checked:** in the cloud's software renderer, bars on screen a moment after Ready is pressed. **Not checked:** how the shake feels on a real screen (the sizes above are guesses for Mario's Mac to settle), and the bars on an ultrawide window.

## Built: stage 4, the director camera

*Built 2026-10-24. Presentation only: it moves the bird's-eye camera and, in a watched replay, the playback speed; nothing reaches the simulation, so golden replays and sync are unchanged.*

**F8 turns the director camera on and off** (`gc_game/src/director.rs`; "Director camera" in the Marshal's Scroll). It finds the fight and frames it, easing over rather than cutting, and drifts the view a little round the action so a still moment is not a still picture. **Watching a replay turns it on by itself**, so a replay plays like a film; moving the camera in any way (arrows, edge scroll, wheel, turning, the minimap, riding with **Y**) takes the view back at once, and F8 gives it away again.

**What it watches.** Blows leave a spot of heat that cools over a few seconds (a charge that hits home 3.6, an ambush or a falling building 4, a rout 2, a tenth for each soldier lost; blows within 4 tiles of a spot join it, up to 12, and there are at most 128 spots, so a long battle costs no more than a short one), a regiment in melee is a steady warmth of 1, and every regiment counts a little (0.3), so a calm moment frames the biggest army. Each frame it frames the weighted middle of the warmest cluster (the heat within 24 tiles), and how far out from how widely the cluster spreads (34 to 120 tiles from the ground). It stays with a fight for at least 3.5 seconds and only leaves it for one 1.35 times hotter (it looks for a hotter fight every 0.4 seconds, and between looks only follows the fight it is on). Heat from a fight the viewer cannot see (under fog) is not noted, so it cannot give away a hidden fight; in a replay everything is seen.

**Replay films.** While a replay is watched with the director camera on, letterbox bars stay in (Clarity still turns them off; the HUD is drawn over the bottom bar), and a big moment (heat of 3.5 or more: a charge hitting home, an ambush, a building falling) slows the playback to about a third of its speed for 1.6 seconds, easing in and out, then waits eight seconds before it does it again. **Slow motion is for watched replays only.** A live or online match always runs at its own pace, because every machine must run the same simulation at the same speed; the director camera still frames a live match, without slow motion or bars. `--watch FILE` opens a recorded battle as a film straight from the command line (`grubcraft --watch replays/field-1v1.gcr`).

**Checked:** in the cloud's software renderer, the camera framing an army in a replay with the bars in. Unit tests cover finding a fight and closing in, staying with a fight until another is clearly hotter, going back to a cooled fight, taking over when the player moves the view, and the slow motion's easing and cooldown. **Not checked:** how it feels on a real screen, with real fights (the numbers above are first guesses for Mario's Mac to settle), and exporting a film to a video file (not built; a screen recording does it).

## Built: stage 4, tilt-shift and sun shafts

*Built 2026-10-24. Render only (two small additions to the composite pass in `post.wgsl`, `Grade.focus`); the simulation never reads any of it.*

**Tilt-shift** (Settings, Display: "Tilt-shift: soft top and bottom", **off by default**). The top and bottom of the picture blur, a sharp band stays in the middle of the field (the row 40 % down the window, above the middle because the command bar fills the bottom), and a battlefield looks like a table of miniatures. The blur grows from nothing at 0.12 of the screen's height from that row to nine texels at 0.4 from it (the top edge of the window), over a twelve-tap spiral; pixels in the sharp band cost nothing extra. It is a screen-space effect, not true depth of field: the game does not keep its depth after the world is drawn (the 4× anti-aliased depth is transient, [10](10-architecture.md)), and a tilt-shift look needs none. It is off in the ride camera (where depth of field would blur the soldiers you ride with) and with **Clarity**, and it saves with the settings.

**Sun shafts** (the ride camera only; not on the Battery graphics preset, not with Clarity). From each pixel the composite marches fourteen steps toward the sun's place on the screen, gathering the bright sky it passes (anything over about 1 to 2.2 in the HDR scale, easing in), fading each step by 7 %, so light streams through gaps in trees and out of the sky past dark shapes. The strength is 0.6 at most, full with the sun on the screen, fading to nothing as it goes off to the side (gone at 0.4 of the screen beyond its middle), and samples that fall off the picture count for nothing, and nothing with the sun behind the camera (`cinema::sun_shafts`, from the view and projection and `Look::sun_dir`). It is tinted by the look's light tint, so it follows the time of day and the land's air. Cost: fourteen taps a pixel while riding with the sun in front, none otherwise.

**Checked:** in the cloud's software renderer, the tilt-shift on the same view as before (sharp in the middle, soft at the top edge), and the shafts' shader running without errors. A unit test covers the sun's screen place and fade. **Not checked:** the shafts' look (the headless run could not ride a regiment toward the sun), and how either looks on a real screen, where Mario tunes the numbers. Not built: a tilt-shift in the ride camera, depth-aware shafts (the sky's brightness stands in for what is in the way).

## Built: stage 5, the look of weather and night

*Part 1, 2026-10-24. Render only, driven by a preview: the simulation does not know about weather yet.*

**Who owns what.** The weather and changing land thread ([32](32-changing-land.md)) owns the rules and the state in the simulation (a weather kind and strength, a time of day, wind), and this document owns every picture of them. Until its state lands, `gc_game::atmosphere::Atmosphere` holds a local weather that `--weather NAME` or **F12** sets, with the same shape as the simulation's (a kind, and how much of it from 0 to 1), so wiring the real values is one line.

**The Night look** (`LookPreset::Night`, the sixth look; F9 and `--look night`). A moon in place of the sun (cool blue, a third of the noon sun's strength, still casting shadows), low in the sky (0.18 high), because the ambient touches and lit windows ([34](34-ambient-life.md)) decide it is dark from the sun's height (below 0.3) and a high moon would leave the lamps off and the pollen drifting. Deep blue shade, a dark sky, thick blue air, exposure lifted to 1.2 so the unlit side still reads, colours drained to 0.75, a strong vignette and grain. The numbers are a guess seen on the software GPU only; the F10 panel is how they get settled on a real screen. Night is not offered in the lobby yet.

**Weather** (`gc_render::Weather`: Clear, Rain, Snow, Fog). `Look::in_weather(weather, amount)` bends any look toward the weather's: the sun dims (rain to 0.35 of its strength, snow 0.45, fog 0.55), the sky light changes a little (snow brighter, rain slightly darker), the air thickens (rain ×2.2, snow ×2.6, fog ×6, and fog also hugs low ground more), the haze and sky grey toward their own brightness (snow lifts them, rain lowers them), rim light and leaf glow fall to 0.4, cloud shadows deepen, colour drains. So rain at dusk is dusk's rain and snow in the Waste is the Waste's snow. In the game it comes after the land's air and before Clarity and the tuning panel. A weather eases in with a 3 second time constant (`WEATHER_SETTLE`), and when F12 changes it the old weather eases out at the same pace as the new one comes in (`Atmosphere::leaving`), so the picture never jumps. A match starts clear unless `--weather` says otherwise. Under Clarity a look keeps only 35% of its weather (`CLARITY_WEATHER`), so a storm or fog cannot hide the field from a player who asked for plain light; Clarity also still removes the vignette, grain and heavy grading. The tuning panel and its log line name the weather and how much has come in, because values tuned in the rain are not the plain look's.

**Rain and snow** (`Effects::precipitation`, `Field::weather`). They are particles of the one system in [Built: stage 3](#built-stage-3), no new draw. A drop is a streak (`STRETCH`) falling at 10 to 13 tiles a second with a little slant; a flake is a soft speck falling at 1.1 to 1.9 tiles a second, swaying on the wind. Each begins up to 14 tiles above the ground and lives just long enough to be gone before it reaches it (a flake's life is also kept under the ring's 8 second longest), so nothing is drawn through the ground and no splash is needed yet. The ground is the land or the water's surface, whichever is higher, so rain does not fall through a river. They fall over a disc round the camera's focus (half the camera's height from the ground, between 14 and 60 tiles), at 0.8 drops or 0.25 flakes a second for each tile of ground under it, capped at 1,200 drops or 350 flakes a second, fading out between a camera height of 110 and 175 tiles (the streaks are under a pixel wide from there, and there is none in Commander View). The cap is set by the ring, which reuses its oldest slot whether or not the particle is still alive: the rule is that everything spawned in a second, times the longest life (8 seconds), must fit in the 32,768 slots, so about 4,000 a second for the weather and a battle together; rain at its cap leaves the battle about 2,500. Everything is scaled by the Graphics preset's density (Battery a third) and thinned to 0.4 under Clarity so team colours stay readable. A slow frame makes no more than a quarter second of rain, and none falls behind the menu or in the unit viewer. Drops are lit by the world's light, so they are dull at night and bright in a storm; the fog of war hides any that fall over ground the viewer cannot see. The particles' shared fade curve (in fast, out over the last two thirds of life) makes a drop dim as it nears the ground, which suits rain seen from above; a ride camera (eye 2.5 tiles up) sees rain mostly overhead, and a fade of its own for precipitation is a later tweak.

**Point lights (part 2).** Lit windows and fires light the ground and walls round them at night (`gc_render/src/lights.rs`, `gc_game/src/lights.rs`). `Effects::lamp` and `Effects::flames` already ran every frame a window is lit or a building burns; each now also records a `Glow` (position, colour, reach, strength, and whether the darkness scales it: a fire is scaled, a lit window is already only reported at dusk). Glows within half a tile of each other in one frame are one (the stronger stays), so a squad chopping at one flame cannot fill the list of 256 and crowd out the lamps. The game passes only the glows on ground the viewer sees now (a point light, unlike a particle, would show a fire in the fog, and a remembered enemy building keeps its live damage), and none behind the unit viewer or in an online catch-up. `Lights::frame` ages what it holds, then notes this frame's glows (so a glow reported this frame is full strength however long the frame was, or at four times speed), merges glows within 0.45 tile or half of the bigger one's reach (a building collapsing throws flames over its whole footprint and is one fire; the brighter of two joined in a frame wins, and a new frame replaces the old report), keeps each lit for half a second after its last report (a fire stoked every few frames does not blink, and one that goes out fades), ranks them by brightness × reach² over distance, and sends the best 24 (`MAX_LIGHTS`) to `Renderer::set_lights`, the last four fading by rank so panning does not pop a light on or off. Lights fade out between 110 and 140 tiles of camera height. They are uniforms in the shared `Globals` (+784 bytes), read by `shade` and `shade_material` in `world.wgsl` (`local_lights`), so terrain, scenery, buildings and soldiers all take them with no extra pass: a wrapped diffuse term (a surface edge-on to the lamp still takes some), a smooth square fall to nothing at the light's reach (2.4 tiles for a window, 2.5 to 6.5 for a fire by its size), a small flicker. They do not cast shadows and the water does not take them yet. Strength times 5 in HDR; with no lights the shader loop does not run, so daylight pays nothing. Checked on the software GPU in the siege demo at night (`--siege --look night`): the burning building and the lit hall throw warm light on the ground and walls; the strength was first set too low to see (2.2) and raised to 5. Untuned on a real screen. The Grub and torch lights of the plan wait for torches to exist as things in the game (towers and gates, [26](26-fortifications.md)); they would report a `Glow` the same way.

**Following the simulation's sky (part 3b).** In a match the picture now reads the sky the simulation keeps ([32](32-changing-land.md): `world.weather()`, `world.daylight()`), through `SkyView` in `gc_game/src/atmosphere.rs`, once a frame (`Atmosphere::follow`). *Weather*: the kind and the strength over 255 are the renderer's `Weather` and amount directly. The simulation already eases a weather out before the next grows in (about 25 seconds), so the atmosphere does no easing of its own and leaves nothing behind; the ground still soaks and dries on its own clock. Lightning, wet ground and splashes follow from it (a lobby's Rain at strength 200 is 0.78, enough to storm). *The light*: `Look::of_day(hour)` blends the prepared looks along the day by the clock's hour (`DAY` in `look.rs`): Night to 4:48, up through dusk's colours at 6:12 (they serve the dawn too), golden hour at 7:30, Noon from 9:30 to 14:30, golden hour at 17:00, Dusk at 18:00, Night from 19:30. The lobby's times of day are just where the clock starts (Golden hour 17:00, Noon and Overcast 12:00, Dusk 18:00, Night 23:00), so a long dusk battle darkens into night and a night one stays dark. The sun's compass side turns with the hour (east in the morning, west in the afternoon, round the sky between 10:00 and 14:00; the moon stays put through the night, turning between 3:00 and 6:00) at the height of the blended look, so shadows swing across the field over the day and the moon stays low enough for lamps and fireflies. The sun is held still for five minutes of the clock at a time (a hundred seconds of play, moving a few degrees at each step), because the shadow map only snaps to its texels while the sun does not move; the colours blend smoothly meanwhile. The lights come on gradually as the sun sinks (the ambient and lamp tests read the sun's height). *Cloud*: `Look::clouded(cloud, light)` dims the sun to 0.6, fills the shade (not at night), drains the sky and air toward grey and softens the rest, scaled by the simulation's cloud cover; weather that falls (rain, snow, fog) already carries its own cloud in `Look::in_weather`, so only the cloud it leaves over is applied. The lobby's Overcast is clear weather under heavy cloud, so it is noon seen through `clouded` (close to the old prepared Overcast). Under Clarity the cloud, like the weather, keeps only 35 percent. *Taking over*: `--look NAME` and **F9** keep their look whatever the hour (F9 for the rest of the match) and take none of the simulation's cloud, and `--weather NAME` and **F12** keep their weather (F12 for the rest of the match) and take no cloud either, so a hand-picked look or weather is the whole picture; the F10 panel names the clock's hour while the light follows it; a new match follows the simulation again. Matches with a still sky (the sandbox, tests, older replays) are a fair 17:00, the game's usual golden hour. For looking at it without waiting for the clock, `--battle LEVEL --time-of-day noon|golden-hour|dusk|overcast|night --sky fair|changing|rain|snow|fog` set the lobby's choices (`--conquest` takes them too). Not done: wind (`Weather::wind`) does not slant the rain or drive smoke yet, the clouds' shadow speed is fixed, and nothing of the sky is drawn but the colours (no cloud banks, stars or moon disc).

**Wet ground, splashes and lightning (part 3a).** Three pictures of heavy rain, all render-only. *Wet ground*: `Look::wet` (0 dry, 1 sodden) is set by the atmosphere (`gc_game/src/atmosphere.rs`) from the rain that has fallen: it soaks toward the rain's strength with a 12 second time constant and dries with a 90 second one (and counts as dry under 0.2 percent), so the ground stays wet for a few minutes after the rain stops, and a new match starts dry. Snow and fog do not wet it. The terrain shader (`fs_terrain`, the `soaked` block; the value rides in `Globals.misc.w`) darkens the earth by up to 28 percent and gathers puddles in the hollows of flat ground (a noise threshold that lowers as the ground wets, and none at all until it is well under way; none on slopes, rock or the dirt fringe; only puddles take the glossy, sky-mirroring shading, so ground that is merely damp changes smoothly): a puddle is darker, flattens the surface normal, is glossy, and mirrors the sky with a Fresnel edge (the same `sky_color` the water uses, so at night or under a flash it mirrors that). Buildings, scenery and soldiers are not darkened yet. *Splashes* (`Effects::splashes`): small rings that spread from about 0.01 to 0.1 tiles in a quarter second, spawned at random points of a disc round the focus (0.4 of the camera's height, 6 to 26 tiles) at 0.35 a second for each tile, at most 300 a second, fading out between a camera height of 35 and 90 tiles (they are specks from further up), rain only. They are independent of the drops (a drop's own particle is gone just above the ground), so a splash is not tied to one drop; at 300 a second they add 2,400 slots to the ring's 8 second budget, still inside the 4,000 a second the weather and a battle share. *Lightning*: in rain at 75 percent strength or more, a flash every 5 to 16 seconds (a small random generator of the picture's own, so it is not part of the match: nothing about it is sent or recorded, and each machine draws its own), a sharp first stroke reaching full in 0.04 seconds, a dimmer second stroke 0.22 seconds later, each dying away in about a tenth of a second (`bolt` in the atmosphere). `Look::flashed(level)` does the lighting: the sky light and the shade lift (sky light up to 7 times), the sun's light takes a cold edge, and the sky and air go white-blue; the grade, the air's thickness and the shadows stay, so a flash lights the land from above for an instant rather than fading the screen. A flash that has begun finishes if the weather changes, and it lights the picture only, not the values in the F10 panel. The picture's wet and flashes run on the frame clock, so they go on behind a pause. Under Clarity wetness and flashes are both held to the 35 percent the other weather keeps. There is no bolt drawn and no thunder yet (the sound thread owns sounds; the atmosphere could hand it each strike).

**Checked:** each look and weather on the cloud's software renderer (the numbers and the haze read as intended, snow and rain visible in a field battle once slowed down to the frame rate of the software GPU, which runs at about one frame a second, too slow to show real-speed rain). **Not checked:** rain and snow at 60 frames a second on a real screen (how dense and how bright they should be), the night look's readability, and the fog's reach in a big battle; Mario's Mac and the F10 panel settle those. **Checked, part 3a:** wet ground and puddles in a field battle after the preview ran for two minutes of frames (darker, grey-green puddle patches mirroring the overcast sky), and a flash with its decay slowed so the one-frame-a-second software GPU could catch it (the land goes pale and cold). Splash rings were too small and quick to see at that frame rate. **Not built yet:** a bolt drawn in the sky, thunder, wet buildings and soldiers, torches and Grub as point lights, dawn fog banks along rivers, snow settling on the ground, and wind that follows the simulation's.
