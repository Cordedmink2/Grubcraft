# 18 · AI: Opponents, Allies and the Living World

How Grubcraft's computer-controlled sides think: AI opponents and AI allies in Field Battle and Conquest, and the minds of everyone else on the map, from Grubfolk hamlets and Titan-spawn to mercenary companies and, later, factions you meet while exploring. It also defines the **relations system** they all share, because the game is not only a battle against the other players: who likes you, who fears you, who remembers what you did, and who will fight for you if the price is right are part of the design from the start.

Only the battle layer is designed in full detail here, because it is built first. Everything else is designed far enough that the first pieces of code don't have to be torn up when it arrives.

**Built so far (AI-1, all three steps, 2026-10-13; AI-2, 2026-10-15; AI-3 stages 1 to 3, 2026-10-17):** the battle AI, playable; it reads the ground; the AI lens shows what it is thinking; the Warlord reads the enemy's plan and rehearses its own in copies of the battle; and the Conquest computer players gather, build, train, expand, attack, defend and go for the Titan's Heart, each level weighing its odds and playing the economy differently. [Built so far in detail](#built-so-far) says what works, how well it plays and what is still missing; [Engine hooks](#engine-hooks) lists the small changes the simulation needs, and [Build plan](#build-plan) says when each part lands.

## What "intelligent" means here

1. **It plays like a commander, not a calculator.** It wins with the same things a good human uses: position, timing, morale and concentration of force. It forms a line, anchors a flank on a cliff, keeps a reserve, hits the regiment that is about to break, rides round to the archers, chases routers and knows when to retreat. It does not win by clicking 400 times a minute.
2. **It is fair.** It sees only what its player could see, plays by exactly the same rules, and its orders arrive with the same network delay as anyone's. Difficulty comes from how well it thinks, never from map hacks or free resources (a separate, visible [handicap](#difficulty) exists for anyone, human or AI).
3. **It adapts.** It scouts, works out what you're building and builds the counter; it changes plan mid-battle when the plan stops working; it doesn't attack down the same lane at the same minute every game.
4. **It has a personality you can learn.** A cautious Clan lord, a ravenous Brood hive, a greedy Concord auditor. Personalities make AI opponents recognisable and beatable, and make allies feel like people.
5. **One brain for every non-human side.** Enemy AI players, AI allies, mercenaries, neutral settlements and factions met while exploring all run the same architecture with different parts switched on. Diplomacy, contracts and grudges work the same way whoever is on the other side.
6. **It can never break multiplayer.** It cannot cause a desync, cannot slow down the simulation on anyone else's machine, and changing it never changes replays or the golden determinism hashes.
7. **It is lean.** A few milliseconds of one CPU core and a few megabytes per AI player, on the host only ([budget](#performance-and-memory)).

## The big decision: rules in the simulation, minds outside it

There are two ways to put an AI into a lockstep game:

| | **A · AI inside the simulation** | **B · AI as a player** (chosen) |
|---|---|---|
| Where it runs | On every machine, every tick, inside `gc_sim` | On one machine (the host, or the solo player's), on its own thread |
| How it acts | Changes the world directly, identically everywhere | Sends commands that travel in the turn bundle like a human's |
| Rules it must follow | Integer maths only, zero allocations, a share of the 2 ms tick budget on the *slowest* machine | Anything: floats, time budgets, thinking ahead over many ticks |
| Changing it | Changes every golden hash and breaks old replays | Changes nothing in the sim; replays keep working (they record its commands) |
| A bug in it | Can desync the match | Can only make it play badly |
| Weakness | Expensive to make clever | Needs the host; must be made to play fair on purpose |

**Grubcraft takes B for anything that makes choices, and keeps A for rules.** The line between them:

| Lives **in the simulation** (deterministic, identical everywhere, hashed) | Lives **in a mind** (runs on the host, acts only through commands) |
|---|---|
| **Reflexes** every regiment has, human or AI: fight back when attacked, skirmish, brace, rout, rally | Which regiments to send where, and why |
| **World rules:** creep camps guard and leash, hamlet garrisons shoot hostiles, prices drift, reputations change when you raid | Whether a mercenary company takes your contract, wanders off or turns on you |
| **The relations ledger:** who is allied, friendly, neutral, wary or hostile to whom, and how much they like and trust each other | What to propose, whom to threaten, when to declare war |
| **Deals and contracts:** their terms, payments, expiry and what happens on a breach | Which deals to offer and when to walk away from one |
| **The answer to an offer** (a rule, so the UI can show you in advance whether it will be accepted, and why) | Plans, beliefs about the enemy, battle tactics, build orders |

```
                    ┌──────────── host machine (or solo) ─────────────┐
  sim snapshot ───▶ │ AI thread (gc_ai)                               │
  (read only,       │  perception: what this party can see            │
   after each tick) │    → mind: strategy, economy, armies, battles   │
                    │    → commands ──────────────────────────────────┼──▶ host's turn bundle ──▶ every machine's gc_sim
                    └─────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

What follows from this:

- **The AI is just another player.** It issues the same `Command`s a human can (move, attack, and later build, train, cast, propose a deal). There are no AI-only commands, so anything it does a human could have done.
- **Replays play back without the AI.** They contain its commands like anyone else's. "Restage this battle", save games and desync reports work unchanged.
- **AI work never touches the sim's budget.** The sim tick stays within its 2 ms budget on every machine; the AI's thinking costs only the host, on a separate thread. If the AI is late, its order lands a turn later, exactly like a human on slow Wi-Fi.
- **Minds keep nothing important only in their heads.** Anything that must survive a save, a rejoin or (later) host migration, or that other players should be able to see, lives in the sim: relations, contracts, personality ids. A mind's private working memory (beliefs, plans) can be rebuilt from the sim in about a second, so a loaded game or a migrated host simply re-scouts. A save may also carry each mind's working memory as an optional, unhashed blob.
- **The AI is reproducible when it's tested.** In **lab mode** (headless tests and the [arena](#testing-tuning-and-explaining)) the AI runs on the sim thread between ticks with fixed work budgets instead of wall-clock ones, so the same seed gives the same game on the same platform. In real play it uses real time budgets on its own thread; nothing depends on that being reproducible.
- **Any machine could run an AI.** Each AI is a player whose commands are sent by some machine. At first that is always the host; an AI slot could later be assigned to another friend's machine to spread the load, or take over a disconnected friend's army.

## Parties: everyone who can own an army

Today the sim knows *players* 0 to 5 (the six lobby slots, since 2026-10-16), and `MAX_PARTIES` is 8, so two numbers are left for neutrals until engine hook 8 widens it. This design names that idea: every side that can own regiments or structures is a **party**, identified by the same small number (`PlayerId`, a `u8`). A match reserves up to **32 parties**: the six player slots plus up to 26 world parties.

| Kind | Examples | Controlled by | Mind? | Takes part in diplomacy | Arrives |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Player** | You, your friends, AI opponents and allies | A human, or an AI mind | AI players: full mind | Teams fixed in the lobby; full diplomacy later as an option | Now (humans); M2 (AI) |
| **Settlement** | Grubfolk villages, towns, the Bazaar | Rules in the sim | None: rules answer parleys and call for help | Yes: trade, hire, rumours, raid; reputation | M4 |
| **Wildlife** | Titan-spawn creep camps, the Old Mouth | Rules in the sim | None | No: always hostile, but territorial (they guard, they don't hunt) | M4 |
| **Company** | Mercenary bands that camp or wander between hamlets | A mind | Operations, battles, envoy | Yes: contracts, loyalty, desertion, word of mouth | M4 |
| **Faction** | Peoples you meet while exploring: a Titan cult, a nomad caravan town, a splinter of the Choir | A mind | Full mind, minus anything it doesn't need | Yes: friendship, alliances, tribute, war | With the campaign (C-2 in [19](19-campaign.md#build-plan)) |

The Titan itself (Tremors, Heartbeat) is not a party; its events are world rules.

## Relations

### The ledger

For every ordered pair of parties *(A, B)* the sim keeps a small record of what A thinks of B. It is asymmetric: the hamlet you burned hates you; you may not care about it at all.

| Field | Range | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| **Stance** | Unknown, Hostile, Wary, Neutral, Friendly, Allied (and Serving, for a mercenary under contract) | What A's units actually *do* about B. This is the only field the combat rules read. |
| **Liking** | −100 to +100 | A's opinion of B: A's starting attitude to B plus every memory below, as each fades. |
| **Trust** | 0 to 100 | Whether A believes B keeps its word: built slowly by honoured deals, lost fast by broken ones. Also a starting value plus memories. |
| **Memories** | one per kind of event per pair | The notable things B did to A, each with a weight that fades over time. Repeats add to the same memory (up to a cap), so twenty trades are one strong memory, not twenty. Liking and trust are *made of* memories, which is why the UI can always say why: "They remember you burned Millbrook." |

A third number, **fear**, is not stored: it is worked out when needed from how much military strength B has near A compared with A's own. Liking, trust and fear are enough to make almost any diplomatic behaviour feel motivated: a hamlet that dislikes you but fears your army still trades, sullenly; a mercenary captain who likes you but doesn't trust you wants payment up front.

**What each stance does in the simulation:**

| Stance | Units attack on sight | Trade and hire | Passing through their land | Shares vision | Can command the other's units |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Unknown** | No (treated as Neutral until first contact) | Not until met | Allowed | No | No |
| **Hostile** | Yes | No | Fought | No | No |
| **Wary** | Only if attacked, or trespassing near their home | Trade at +25%, no hiring | Tolerated | No | No |
| **Neutral** | No | Yes | Allowed | No | No |
| **Friendly** | No | Better prices | Allowed | No | No |
| **Allied** | No | Resource transfers (10% tax, as in team games) | Allowed | Yes | No (except taking over a disconnected teammate) |
| **Serving** | Attacks whoever the employer is Hostile to | n/a | n/a | With the employer | **The employer can** |

Stances between players come from the lobby: teammates are Allied, everyone else Hostile, and those don't change during a match unless player diplomacy is turned on (a later option, see [Open questions](#open-questions)).

### Memories and time

Relations change through **events**, a fixed table in data (`data/relations.ron`) so that the rules are visible and tunable. First-pass values:

| Event (B did this to A) | Liking | Trust | Fades by half every | Word spreads to A's kin |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Traded with A | +2 per trade (up to +30) | +1 | 5 min | No |
| Gave A a gift | +1 per 20 Grub of value (up to +25) | 0 | 3 min | No |
| Killed creeps near A's home | +5 | 0 | 5 min | Yes, at half |
| Attacked A's units | −50 | −10 | 10 min | Yes: other hamlets −25, fading by half every 4 min, so they stay Wary for about ten minutes |
| Trespassed in A's territory | −1 every 10 s | 0 | 2 min | No |
| Raided (razed) A | −80 | −40 | 30 min | Yes, as for an attack |
| Honoured a contract to the end | +5 | +15 | 20 min | Companies only, at half |
| Paid late or short | −10 | −15 | 10 min | No |
| Broke a contract (stopped paying, attacked a partner) | −30 | −60 | 40 min | Yes, to every Company |
| Fought A's enemy | +5 per enemy regiment routed near A | +2 | 5 min | No |

- **Kin** are parties that talk to each other: all Grubfolk hamlets are kin, all mercenary companies are kin. That is how "word spreads": a betrayal of one mercenary company makes every company want more money from you.
- **Starting attitudes** are set per party kind and faction in the same data file: Grubfolk start mildly warm (+10) to everyone, a little warmer to the Ember Clans and cooler to the Concord, for instance. As memories fade, liking and trust drift back to them on their own.
- **Big things are remembered for a long time.** A burned hamlet's memory fades over half an hour, not a minute, so a raid at minute 10 still costs you at minute 40. Trust is lost in one stroke and rebuilt over many honoured deals.
- **Memory is bounded.** A fixed pool holds every memory in the match; when it is full, the faintest memory is forgotten first.
- **Across matches:** the [campaign](19-campaign.md#relations-over-time) keeps one ledger for its whole length, with memories fading by season instead of by minute (a second half-life column in the same event table). Each campaign battle starts from the ledger and hands back the memories it made.

### From numbers to stance

Rule-driven parties (settlements, wildlife) don't have minds that choose a stance; their stance follows their liking toward you, with a gap so it doesn't flicker:

| Liking | Stance |
|---|---|
| +30 and above | Friendly |
| 0 to +30 | Neutral |
| −30 to 0 | Wary |
| −30 and below | Hostile |

A stance only changes once liking is 5 points past the line, so it doesn't flicker. That reproduces the Grubfolk reputation rules in [07 · World and Maps](07-world-and-maps.md#reputation) exactly: trading nudges a hamlet toward Friendly; attacking it or raiding it makes it Hostile and every other hamlet Wary for about ten minutes. Hamlet reputation becomes *one use* of the ledger rather than its own system.

Parties with minds (AI players, companies, factions) choose stance changes themselves, through commands, weighing the same three numbers.

### Cost

Thirty-two parties make 1,024 ordered pairs. At 4 bytes each (stance, liking and trust as last worked out, flags) that is 4 KB, plus a fixed pool of 512 memories at 16 bytes (8 KB) and 64 contracts at 32 bytes (2 KB). All of it is hashed sim state, updated when events happen and once a second as memories fade: well under 0.01 ms a tick.

## Deals and contracts

A **deal** is a proposal from one party to another; an accepted deal becomes a **contract**, a sim record with terms that the sim enforces on its own.

| Deal | Between | Terms | Arrives |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Trade** | Anyone and a settlement or faction | Grub for Marrow at the market price (the existing hamlet market) | M4 |
| **Hire** | Anyone and a settlement (militia) or company | Up-front fee, then upkeep every minute, for a set time or until cancelled | M4 |
| **Rumours** | Anyone and a settlement | Pay to reveal an area for 30 s | M4 |
| **Passage** | Anyone and a territorial party | Your armies may cross their land without trespass penalties | After 1.0 |
| **Tribute** | Anyone and anyone | A payment every minute, in exchange for peace or protection | After 1.0 |
| **Ceasefire, alliance** | Players (if player diplomacy is on), factions | Stance change for a set time | After 1.0 |
| **Request aid** | Anyone and a Friendly or Allied party | "Send help here"; the other side's mind decides how much | After 1.0 (allied AI players answer pings from M3) |

**Lifecycle.** A deal is proposed with a command. A human answers offers made to them; for everyone else the answer comes on the spot from the shared **rule** below. Minds don't answer offers, they make them: what to propose, to whom and when is where their judgement goes. Accepted deals become contracts; each minute the sim moves the payments; if the payer can't pay, the contract is **breached**, ends, and records the matching memory. Expiry and cancellation are just as mechanical.

**The answer is a rule, and you can see it before you ask.** An offer's score is a small integer formula over what the deal is worth to them, their liking, their trust, their fear of you, and their personality's greed and honour. Because it is a pure function of sim state, the parley panel can call it while you are still adjusting the offer and show: *"Likely to accept: they like you (+30), they need protection from the Brood (+15), you're asking a lot (−20)."* No hidden dice, which keeps to the [UI principles](08-ui-ux.md#ui-principles): numbers on demand, nothing important hidden.

**Mercenary contracts.** A hired company's regiments stay owned by the company, but while the contract runs the company's stance to the employer is **Serving**, which gives the employer the right to command them (the command check becomes "you own it, or you hold a contract over it"). Unpaid mercenaries stop obeying, then leave. A company with low honour may also be **outbid**: if someone else offers enough more and its trust in you is low, it switches sides, which is a mind decision and so always arrives as a visible command, never a silent rule. A company that was cheated tells every other company.

## Inside a mind

A mind is a small chain of command, the way a real army is organised. Each layer works at its own pace and passes orders down; reports come back up.

```
Sovereign        what do we want?                     every 2 to 5 s
 ├─ Spymaster    what do we know?                     continuous
 ├─ Steward      economy, production, building        every 0.5 to 1 s
 ├─ Envoy        relations and deals                  every 5 to 10 s
 └─ Marshal      armies and objectives                every 1 s
     └─ Captain  one per battle: formations, flanks,  2 to 4 times a second
                 targets, engage or withdraw
                   └─ regiment roles ──▶ commands
       (the sim's reflexes keep running underneath, for every party)
```

Different parties switch different layers on:

| Party | Sovereign | Spymaster | Steward | Envoy | Marshal | Captain |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| AI player, Field Battle | (fixed goal: win the battle) | ✓ | | | one army | ✓ |
| AI player, Conquest | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ (allies, settlements, companies) | ✓ | ✓ |
| Mercenary company | small (keep paid, stay alive) | ✓ | | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ |
| Faction met while exploring | ✓ | ✓ | small | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ |
| Settlement, wildlife | rules only, no mind | | | | | |

### Spymaster: perception and belief

- **Perception is filtered.** The mind reads the same public views the renderer reads (`RegimentView`, volleys, and later structures and resources), cut down to what its party can see through fog of war. A mind never reads sim internals, and never anything its player couldn't see on screen.
- **Belief** fills the gaps: every enemy regiment last seen is remembered with where, when, what type and how many soldiers; its possible position spreads out at its marching speed until it's seen again, and it is forgotten after a few minutes. Seen production buildings and tech imply what the enemy can field. This is how the AI estimates an army it can't see, and how a feint or a hidden reserve can fool it, just as it would fool a human.
- **Influence maps**, at 4-tile cells (64 × 64 on a Warfield map), refreshed twice a second: enemy threat (who could hit this cell soon), own control, resource value, and danger for raiders and caravans. They answer "where is the front?", "where is it safe to expand?" and "which route avoids the enemy army?".
- **Terrain analysis**, done once at match load from the nav grid and heightmap: regions and the chokepoints between them (from the nav sectors and portals), open fields where lines can form, high ground, river fords, and **flank anchors** (stretches of impassable ground that can protect the end of a battle line). The map validator (`gc_mapcheck`) can bake this so it costs nothing at load.
- **Opponent habits:** it keeps a running tally of how each opponent fights (cavalry swinging round the flanks, early rushes, raids on workers) and the other layers weigh it. If you have flanked it with cavalry twice, it posts spears on its wings.
- **Scouting** is a standing task: cheap fast units (cavalry, Drone-Moths, War Kites) are sent where the belief is oldest and matters most.

### Sovereign: strategy

The Sovereign chooses **intents** by scoring each one and keeping the best, a technique called utility scoring. Each intent's score is built from a few considerations (numbers from 0 to 1 passed through simple response curves) and weighted by personality:

| Intent | Raised by | Lowered by |
|---|---|---|
| Expand | Safe nearby seeps, bank of unspent Grub, enemy busy elsewhere | Enemy army nearby, own army weak |
| Tech up | Healthy economy, no immediate threat, enemy teching | Being attacked |
| Build army | Enemy army larger than ours, upcoming timing attack | Supply near upkeep bands |
| Attack *X* | Our army clearly stronger than *X*'s nearby forces, *X* weakened by someone else (opportunism) | Our base exposed, *X* on good ground |
| Raid *X*'s economy | Fast units available, *X*'s economy exposed (workers in transit, a vein relay, a chord pylon, a caravan route) | *X* has a mobile defence |
| Defend | Threat near our base or expansions | Nothing threatened |
| Take objective | Shrines, the Titan's Heart once it unlocks, a Bloodseep | Contested by a stronger army |
| Answer an ally | An ally pinged "Attack here" / "Need help" / "Defend here" | We are in trouble ourselves |
| Hire or bargain | Rich, a company or friendly hamlet nearby, a war that needs bodies | Poor, low trust |

Two rules stop the classic AI dithering: an intent, once chosen, gets a **commitment bonus** that decays over its expected duration, and switching intents has a cost proportional to the forces being redirected. Big plans (a timing attack) are announced internally ahead of time so the Steward builds toward them.

### Steward: economy and production

- **Openings are data,** like a human's build order: `data/ai/openings.ron` lists them, each naming the personalities that play it, and one is picked at random per match, so openings vary. **Built (stage 3):** workers before the first production building, early production buildings, a worker cap, an early expansion, towers by the hall and a smaller first wave, for the opening's first minutes; see [Built so far](#built-so-far).
- **After the opening**, a production planner keeps every production building busy toward a **target army**: a composition chosen to counter what the Spymaster believes the enemy has (using the [counter table](17-unit-rosters.md#classes-and-counters)), blended with the personality's doctrine. It plans supply ahead, never floats a big bank, and respects upkeep bands.
- **Each faction's economy is its own module**, because each is a different game:

| Faction | What the Steward has to get right |
|---|---|
| **Ember Clans** | Hands per seep (5 saturate), granaries close to seeps, pulling Hands to safety when raiders appear, spending Honor at the right moment (Rice Tax when banking is better than fighting; Banner of the Ancestors is the Captain's call) |
| **Brood** | Growing the vein network as a tree from the Hive with **redundant loops**, so one dead Pustule doesn't cut off a whole flank; larvae split between Gnawers and warriors; sending Reclaimers to every battlefield |
| **Choir** | Placing Resonators to form chords that cover the most seeps with the largest triangle the army can actually defend: a small geometry optimisation scored by income gained against the influence map's danger |
| **Concord** | Choosing caravan routes (longer pays more, danger from the influence map costs more), escorting the rich ones, and building Reliquaries next to *other players'* battles to collect their dead |

### Marshal: armies and objectives

- Regiments are grouped into **armies**, each with an objective from the Sovereign (defend, attack, raid, escort, take an objective, clear a creep camp) and a **rally point**.
- **Concentration of force:** reinforcements gather at the rally point and join as a group; the Marshal never trickles regiments into a fight one at a time.
- **Marching and deploying:** armies march in column along safe routes and **deploy into a battle line before contact** (about 30 tiles out), never blundering into a fight strung out in a pass.
- **Should we fight?** Before committing, the Marshal asks the [battle estimator](#captain-battle-tactics) for the expected result and compares it with the personality's appetite for risk. A Marshal that expects to lose looks for better ground, waits for reinforcements, or goes somewhere else.
- **Raids** are small fast armies with a precise target and an escape route: the Clan worker line, the Brood Pustule whose loss cuts the most seeps, the Choir Resonator whose loss collapses the most chord area, the Concord caravan on its longest leg.

### Captain: battle tactics

This is the heart of the AI and the part built first. A Captain is created for each battle (two armies within about 40 tiles of each other) and runs until the fight is won, lost or broken off.

**The battle frame.** The Captain lays out the battle in its own coordinates: the axis between the two armies, the expected front line, the terrain along it (flank anchors, high ground, fords, chokepoints), and both sides' regiments classified by class (light, spear, heavy, ranged, cavalry, air, artillery, monster, hero).

**Doctrines** are battle-plan templates. The Captain picks one from those its faction and personality allow, by scoring each against the situation (and, at higher difficulty, by [rehearsing](#rehearsal-thinking-ahead-with-the-real-simulation) the best few):

| Doctrine | Shape | Good when | Leans toward |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Hold the line** | Continuous melee line, ranged behind, cavalry on the wings, one reserve | Defending, evenly matched, good ground | Ember Clans (Formation discipline rewards standing still), Concord |
| **Hammer and anvil** | Infantry pins the front while cavalry swings wide to hit the rear | Faster or more cavalry than the enemy | Clans (Mounted Samurai), Concord (Debt Collectors), Choir (Glass Striders) |
| **Refused flank** | One strong wing advances, the weak wing hangs back at an angle | Outnumbered, with a flank anchor on the strong side | Everyone |
| **Envelopment** | A wide, thin crescent that wraps both enemy flanks | Clear numbers advantage, fast units | Brood |
| **Skirmish screen** | Ranged regiments in front, falling back through the line at contact | Ranged advantage | Clans (Yumi), Choir (Prism Lancers) |
| **Last stand** | Backs to a cliff or river, short front, everything in reserve | Badly outnumbered, waiting for help | Everyone |
| **Lens line** | Shardlings placed so Prism Lancers fire through them and refract | Choir with both unit types | Choir |
| **Dead wall** | Undead line that never routs, living crossbows behind it | Concord with undead | Concord |
| **Ambush** | A hidden force strikes the flank once the lines are locked (**built**, [20 · Ambushes](20-ambushes.md#setting-an-ambush)) | Burrowers, forests or tall grass nearby | Brood |

**Roles.** The doctrine assigns each regiment a role, and each role is a small, testable behaviour that turns into ordinary commands:

| Role | Job |
|---|---|
| **Anchor** | Hold the centre of the line, face the enemy, don't chase |
| **Wing** | Extend the line and protect the anchor's flank; turn to face a flanker |
| **Hammer** | Cavalry or shock troops: wait out of reach, then hit a flank or the rear once the enemy line is locked in melee; withdraw and charge again rather than staying in a grind |
| **Screen** | Skirmishers in front: shoot, then fall back through gaps before contact |
| **Battery** | Ranged and artillery: shoot the densest or most dangerous target in reach, stay behind friends |
| **Reserve** | Wait behind the line; plug a gap, catch a flanker, or finish a wavering enemy |
| **Hunter** | Chase routers and kill exposed artillery, within a leash |
| **Guard** | Stay with a hero, an artillery battery or a caravan |
| **Bait** | Feign a retreat to draw an enemy out of its line (top difficulty only) |

**Playing the morale game.** Shogun-style battles are lost when armies *break*, and the AI is built around the same morale rules the sim uses ([05 · Combat](05-combat.md#morale), [10 · Architecture](10-architecture.md#formations-and-combat)):

- It aims to **start rout cascades**: a rout costs every regiment on the router's side within 16 tiles 8 morale, so the best target is a wavering regiment standing next to other shaken ones.
- It **stacks shock**: flank hits cost 2.1 morale a second and rear hits 4.2 (70% of the old 3 and 6 since the battle pace change of 2026-10-18), so it times the Hammer's charge to land while the enemy is already losing men at the front.
- It **makes local odds**: a regiment in melee loses morale every second it is outnumbered 2 to 1 or 3 to 1 within 12 tiles, so the AI concentrates on one part of the enemy line rather than matching it evenly.
- It **saves its own**: a wavering regiment is pulled back behind the line before it routs and drags its neighbours down with it; a reserve steps into the gap.
- It **chases with purpose**: routers rally after 10 s with no enemy within 20 tiles, so a Hunter stays close enough to stop the rally (and a second rout within two minutes shatters them), but no further than its leash from the line.
- It **knows its exceptions**: Concord undead never rout, so it doesn't waste shock on them; heroes, terror, veterancy and Honor Edicts join the reckoning as those systems arrive.

**In Field Battle** the Captain also does the army builder's and deployment's jobs: it picks an army list from its personality's presets (the same "Balanced", "Aggressive" and "Defensive" lists newcomers see), fitted to the points budget; it deploys inside its zone according to its doctrine and the terrain, without seeing the enemy's deployment unless the lobby reveals it; and it values victory points by how long is left on the clock.

**Using ground.** The Captain anchors line ends on impassable ground, prefers to receive an attack on high ground, fights enemies while they cross a ford (by the combat rules, units in water take +20% melee damage), and never fights in column in a pass.

*Built (AI-1 step 2):* every 3 seconds while forming up or holding, with the enemy still 20 tiles or more away, the Captain weighs the places its line could stand within 30 tiles to either side, 16 back and 4 forward. A place counts only if 90% of the ground under the formation and its archers is dry and standable. It scores **cover** (impassable ground, a cliff, deep water or the map's edge, within 6 tiles of an end of the line all along its depth; worth more the worse the odds), **rise** over the ground 12 tiles in front (a little: weighting it more after the terrain rules landed made the Veteran and General lose more often to the Recruit on the generated maps), and, with water between the armies, **keeping back from the bank** (out of reach of better archers on the far side) **facing the nearest ford**, against the distance to walk there. It moves only if the new place is clearly better. A line with one end covered leads with that end and puts its hammers on the open side. With a river between the armies it **waits for the enemy to cross**, and doesn't attack across the water; it crosses only when expecting to win by a clear margin and after twice its usual patience. Expecting to lose, it waits on its ground rather than marching into the fight. Veteran and General use ground; the Recruit doesn't.

*Terrain rules (2026-10-13):* the simulation now has [05 · Combat](05-combat.md#terrain)'s high ground and fords: fighting downhill hits 10% harder, archers on high ground reach 15% further, and waders march at 60% pace, take 20% more melee damage and can't charge. In the ford drill with the enemy charging across (`holding_the_ford_pays_off`), the General holding the far bank now wins 3 of the 8 personalities' fights instead of 1, and keeps far more of its army. On the generated maps (the same 24 seeds before and after) the results moved by no more than chance: General beat Veteran 14 times (12 before), Veteran beat Recruit 16 (15), General beat Recruit 20 (21).

*Queuing (2026-10-13):* friendly regiments now queue in tight ground instead of stacking (see Crowding in [10 · Architecture](10-architecture.md#pathfinding)), so a column wading out of a ford meets the line one block at a time. The General holding the far bank now wins the ford drill against all 8 personalities (3 of 8 with the terrain rules alone) and keeps half to four fifths of its army. The arena on open ground plays out exactly as before; on the same 24 generated maps General beat Veteran 14 times, Veteran beat Recruit 16 and General beat Recruit 19, no more than chance away from before.

*Room to fight (2026-10-15):* once friendly regiments that overlap pay for it in the simulation (they slow down and fall out of order, see [05 · Combat](05-combat.md)), the Captain stopped piling them onto one enemy. A Veteran or General regiment joins a friend's fight only where it can meet the enemy without standing in that friend's block (or in the block of a friend nearer that enemy and already closing on it), and prefers an enemy it has room to meet over one it would have to push in to reach. An end of the line held against cover meets only what comes straight at it, instead of leaving the cover to help the next regiment along. Hammers wait behind the archers standing behind the line rather than among them. A regiment standing at rest is sent on when its place in the line shifts by a tile and a half, so a short shuffle of the line isn't lost. The General also looks at the field every 0.2 s (it was 0.35 s). Regiments still overlap a friend by more than a tile for about a third of their time in melee, as before; the gain comes from not throwing extra regiments into a fight with no room for them. A stricter rule, where a line regiment also needs room before meeting an enemy that isn't squarely in front of it, cut that overlap further in small drills but lost more battles in the arena, so it wasn't kept.

*No waiting for ever (2026-10-15):* a side expecting to lose waits on its ground for the enemy to come, and before this it could wait for good. Now, after half a minute of waiting with nobody coming and none of its regiments fighting, it **goes to fetch them**: every regiment goes after the nearest enemy, as when finishing off a beaten army (the journal says "Tired of waiting", the panel "going to fetch them"). And the estimate now counts allied regiments on its side, so in a 2v2 each side no longer expects to lose to both enemy armies at once and wait. The 2v2 golden battle (seed 0x2d2) used to stand off for all 5 minutes with nobody in melee; it now fights: on today's map with woods, 187 soldiers in melee at the peak and five ambushes sprung, over at 3:07. A Veteran against an army that only stands still (`matchups` in `gc_game`, every pair of factions, 4 battles each) now wins 42 of 64 battles (37 before); before woods came to the maps it was 49 (33), and with the push-through rules 39 (7). Drill: `it_goes_to_fetch_an_enemy_that_never_comes`.

*Finishing the job (2026-10-15):* after the enemy's army breaks, its survivors rally where nobody is chasing them, and the battle isn't won until every regiment that still stands is beaten. Two things left battles undecided after eight minutes. Rallied survivors scattered round the advancing line (some behind it) kept the line's march point from moving on, so the army stood where it had arrived: now, when the line has reached its march point and stood there 15 s with no enemy coming and none of its regiments fighting, it **goes to fetch them**, as above ("Nobody stands to fight"). And the last survivors often rally in a wood, out of sight: when no enemy that still stands is in sight and those it lost sight of are a beaten remnant (or it has gone to fetch them), the army **goes looking** where the nearest one was last seen (however long ago: the estimate forgets an enemy unseen for a minute, but where it was last seen is kept while it is on the field), its regiments abreast and 8 tiles apart, one of them right onto the spot, close enough to see anyone hiding there; then the next place, and so on. A whole army that steps into a wood is left alone: walking into it abreast would be walking into an ambush ("Looking for them where they were last seen"). A Veteran against an army that only stands still (`matchups`, with lines that push) left 8 of 64 battles undecided; now none, winning 44 (37 before) and losing 20 (19). The arena's 200-battle results move by two battles or fewer.

**Engage or withdraw.** The **battle estimator** is a fast analytic model of a fight, built from the sim's own rules rather than guesses:

- Each side's **damage rate**: soldiers who can reach (spears and pikes count half their second rank) × hit chance (50 + attack − defence, with the formation's defence; clamped 10 to 90) × damage × the formation's weapon room, adjusted by the counter table and by how much of the enemy's flank and rear the doctrine can reach; ranged fire counted for the time it takes the lines to close.
- Each side's **breaking point**: not all hit points, but how many losses each regiment takes before it routs, from its morale stat (losses cost 0.9 morale per 1% of strength) and the shock it is likely to take. Undead break at 100%.
- The result is an expected winner, a margin, and the expected cost in Grub of each side's losses.

The estimator reads the sim's numbers rather than copying them: the morale drains and the casualty cost from `gc_sim::morale`, the flank multiplier from `gc_sim::combat::Side`, and a test checks its hit chance against `gc_sim::combat::hit_percent`. The AI's timings are written in `TICKS_PER_SECOND`, and its `Faction` is `gc_data::Faction`, the same type the lobby, replays and unit table use.

The Captain re-runs it every second during the fight. If the expected result turns bad and stays bad, it orders a **fighting withdrawal**: fresh and ranged regiments cover, locked regiments are written off rather than chasing them with the whole army, and the army falls back toward friendly ground. The estimator's coefficients are fitted against real sim battles in the arena; it should call the winner of the drill battles at least 80% of the time. **Measured (AI-1 step 2):** it calls 91% (167 of 183 decided battles out of 200; `crates/gc_ai/tests/estimator.rs`, with CI checking 80% on 60).

### Rehearsal: thinking ahead with the real simulation

The estimator is quick but approximate. For the decisions that matter most (which doctrine to use, whether to commit the reserve, whether a charge will break the line) the strongest AIs do what a real general wishes they could: **they rehearse the battle in their head, using the real game rules.**

Because `gc_sim` is deterministic, headless and fast, the AI can copy the battle area into a scratch world, apply a candidate plan, and run the real simulation 15 to 20 seconds ahead. It tries a few plans against a few likely enemy responses ("keeps doing what it's doing", "charges", "pulls back") and picks the plan with the best result.

- **It can only rehearse what it believes.** The scratch world is built from the Spymaster's picture, so a hidden reserve or an ambush still surprises it. That keeps it fair and keeps ambushes worth setting.
- **Cost:** a battle of 40 regiments ticks in a few hundredths of a millisecond, so 20 seconds ahead (400 ticks) is around 20 ms. Eight rehearsals, re-planned every 2 to 3 seconds, fit easily in the AI thread's budget. These figures are estimates to measure when it's built.
- It needs one engine hook: a cheap way to copy part of a world (see [Engine hooks](#engine-hooks)). "Restage this battle" needs nearly the same thing.
- Rehearsal is reserved for the top difficulty and for big decisions; lower levels use the estimator alone.

*Built (AI-2, 2026-10-15):* the Warlord rehearses **which plan to fight with** (`rehearsal.rs`). A plan is a doctrine its army can fight with and a stance: wait for the enemy (going to fetch them after a minute with nobody coming on or fighting, later than any commander gives up waiting, so two waiting armies still meet), march on them, or decide as it goes. That makes 12 plans on open ground, and 15 where there is cover to lay an ambush in, until the fighting starts.

- **A round.** Every 10 seconds from the moment its line is laid out until it is finishing off a beaten enemy, the Warlord sets the field out in scratch worlds exactly as it sees it (engine hooks 5 and 6): every regiment it can see, where it stands, with the soldiers and the nerve it shows. In each it plays one plan 90 seconds ahead under the real rules, or until one side has held the field for 5 seconds, six times with different luck. A rehearsal scores the share of its own strength it kept less the share the enemy kept (a wavering regiment counts three quarters, a running one a fifth), plus half the estimator's margin for the fight still to come. The plan with the best average replaces the one it follows if it scores at least 0.1 better. The journal says "Rehearsed 12 plans: Hammer and anvil, advancing comes out best (+0.41)".
- **Reading the enemy** (`reading.rs`). A rehearsal is only as good as its guess at what the enemy will do. The first version rehearsed against a sound commander of middling temper starting afresh, and won only 58% against the General: that made-up enemy often chose a different plan from the real one, so the rehearsals barely predicted which plan would win. Now the Warlord keeps a **shadow** of each of the eight tempers, seated on the enemy's side and thinking on the same view, whose orders go nowhere. Once a second, until the armies meet, it checks where the enemy's regiments went against each shadow's places for them: marching toward a shadow's place or standing in it bears that shadow out, marching elsewhere or standing far from it counts against. The rehearsals then seat the enemy as a copy of the best fit, with its plan made and its line laid out; once the armies meet, only that shadow is kept. The journal says "Reads the enemy as The Iron Lord (86% fit)" when a round starts against a different temper from the last one. Before contact it names the General's doctrine right in 55 to 60 battles out of 60, and its posture (holding or advancing) as often (`the_reader_names_the_enemy_plan_before_contact`). Against a human it finds whichever temper's plan looks most like theirs.
- **Fair.** A scratch world holds only the regiments the Warlord sees, with none of the real world's hidden state (orders, wounds, charge and reload timers), and every commander in it sees only what its side would, so a hidden reserve or an ambush still surprises it. Its own regiments hidden in cover start hidden there too, and every regiment already seen starts seen. The shadows see what the Warlord sees and judge only by where regiments go.
- **Same every time.** A round runs on worker threads (the machine's cores less two, at least one, each taking the next rehearsal when it finishes one; the threads are tagged `ai` for the memory budget; Warlords' first rounds start on different beats, each party's number times an eighth of the 10 seconds, so several don't all set out their worlds in one tick) while the game goes on, and its answer is taken a second after it was asked for, so the same battle plays out the same way on any machine. Only in a live game does a machine too slow to finish in time take the answer late instead (see Cost); the AI runs only on the host, so this never desyncs a multiplayer game.
- **How well.** In the arena the Warlord beats the General in 195 of 240 battles (81%). With its quicker hands alone (0.25 s reactions, 150 orders a minute, no slips, no rehearsal) it wins 53% (128 of 240), and with rehearsal against the made-up enemy it won 58% (70 of 120, before the push-through rules). CI checks "beats the General in 17 of 24" and that a Warlord battle plays out the same twice, in release mode (`cargo test --release -p gc_ai --test versus warlord`).
- **Cost.** A round is about 90 rehearsals of up to 90 seconds each. On a 4-core machine it keeps the other cores busy for about 1.9 seconds of every 10, about 28 ms of their time per tick (measured when workers took all cores but one; they now leave two, so a 4-core machine runs two): nearly twice the 15 ms the budget below guessed before it was built, and the first thing to trim (fewer runs per plan, a shorter look ahead) if a game needs the cores. The game's own thread pays about 0.03 ms a tick on average and 5 to 9 ms at worst, when the shadows weigh their ground (`AI_LIVE=1 AI_A=Warlord AI_B=General cargo test -p gc_match --release watch -- --ignored --nocapture`). The first version waited for each round's answer and froze the game for up to 0.9 s every 10 seconds on 4 cores. A live game now never waits (`Minds::live`): a machine too slow to answer in a second takes the answer when it comes, so on 4 cores the Warlord acts on its rehearsals about a second later than in the arena. Tests and the arena still wait, so a battle plays out the same way twice.

### Envoy: relations and deals

The Envoy manages the mind's side of the [relations](#relations) system:

- **For AI players:** hire mercenaries when a war needs bodies and the Grub is there; keep useful hamlets Friendly by trading; decide whether raiding a hamlet is worth the reputation; answer allies' pings; (with player diplomacy on) propose ceasefires to whoever is winning least and alliances against whoever is winning most.
- **For companies and factions:** decide what to offer whom, when to raise the price, whether to desert an employer who has stopped winning, and whom to hold a grudge against. Their personality's honour, greed and vengefulness do most of the work.
- Everything the Envoy does is a command, and everything it decides about accepting is the shared rule above, so the player always sees the same answer the AI would get.

### Commands and attention

- **Attention budget.** Each difficulty has a limit on orders per minute, spent by priority (an engaged battle first, then threats, production, scouting). The AI can be clever but not superhuman: it can't micro three battles perfectly at once.
- **Reaction time.** Things it sees are acted on after a delay, from 1.5 s at the easiest level to 0.25 s at the hardest.
- **No order spam.** A regiment's order is only replaced when the new one differs meaningfully, so the AI's commands are few and the turn bundles stay tiny.
- **Group orders:** a player's right-drag or right-click order is arranged by role in `gc_sim::arrange` (see [08](08-ui-ux.md#selection-and-control)). The AI's moves are still one order per regiment and do not go through it yet, so its lines do not look like a player's lines.
- **Only the orders that exist.** Today the sim has move (with facing and frontage) and attack; the first battle AI builds every manoeuvre from those, and picks up Hold, Charge, Brace, Skirmish, Run and Retreat as each order lands. *Brace and Fall back reached the sim on 2026-10-16; the battle AI doesn't use them yet.*

## Difficulty

Four levels, all playing by exactly the same rules:

|---|---|---|---|---|
| Reaction time | 1.5 s | 0.8 s | 0.8 s | 0.25 s |
| Orders per minute (cap) | 20 | 45 | 90 | 150 |
| Battles it micro-manages at once | 1 | 1 | 2 | 3 |
| Battle tactics | 2 simple doctrines, no reserve | All its doctrines | All, with timed hammer charges and morale targeting | All, plus feints and bait |
| Engage decisions | Often fights at bad odds | Estimator | Estimator | Estimator plus rehearsal |

- **No cheats at any level:** no vision through fog, no free resources, no faster production.
- **Handicap** is separate, visible in the lobby and available to every slot, human or AI: income from 50% to 150% in 10% steps. It is the fair way to balance a strong friend against a newcomer, or to make a Warlord AI even scarier.
- **Order of strength is tested:** each level should beat the one below it at least 70% of the time in the [arena](#testing-tuning-and-explaining).

## AI allies

An AI teammate should feel like a friend who plays well and talks:

- **It listens to pings.** "Attack here", "Defend here", "Need help" and "On my way" from the [ping wheel](08-ui-ux.md#communication) become strong considerations for its Sovereign, weighed against its own situation.
- **It talks back.** It pings its own intentions ("Attacking here" at its target, "Danger" where it has seen a big army) and sends short chat lines from its personality ("Holding the ford. Send cavalry if you can spare it.").
- **It shares what it knows.** Allies share vision by the rules; an AI ally also marks where it believes the enemy army is.
- **It can take over a dropped friend** (lobby option, later): when a player disconnects, an AI at a difficulty the host picks runs their army until they rejoin.

## The living world: encounters

This is where exploration and encounters come in. Each kind of world party reuses the same relations, deals and minds:

- **Titan-spawn (wildlife):** creep camps are pure sim rules. They sleep at their camp, wake when an enemy comes within their aggro radius, chase up to a leash distance and walk home to heal. They are Hostile to everyone, but territorial: they never go hunting. Clearing a camp near a hamlet is an event the hamlet remembers kindly.
- **Grubfolk settlements:** rule-driven parties whose reputation is the ledger. They trade, sell rumours, hire out militia (a Hire contract), shoot at Hostile units in range, and spread word of raids to other hamlets. Later they can ask nearby Friendly parties for help when creeps or raiders attack, a small way to make the map feel alive.
- **Mercenary companies:** bands of fighters with a captain, a personality and a price, camped at a hamlet or wandering between them. You meet them, see their price and their opinion of you, and hire them for a fee plus upkeep. They fight for you while paid, may sell out to a higher bidder if their honour is low, and talk to each other about who pays and who cheats. New content for M4: a handful of companies (a Grubfolk free company, a band of Titan-spawn tamers, a crew of disgraced ronin) with their own rosters.
- **Factions met while exploring (with the campaign, [19](19-campaign.md#the-peoples-you-meet)):** peoples with their own territory, goals and full minds: a Titan cult that worships the Heart, a nomad caravan town, a splinter of the Choir that wants its kin back. Some will be Friendly from the start and can become allies, some Neutral and open to trade, some Hostile raiders. They expand, hold grudges and make deals by the same rules. Because they are just parties with minds, adding one is mostly content: a roster, a personality, a home and a few deal preferences.

**Parley** is how you talk to any of them: select one of their units or buildings and a small panel opens beside the command card (not a pop-up, so the game never pauses). It shows their stance, liking and trust as one bar each with the top reasons ("You traded with us", "You burned Millbrook"), and at most four actions for that party (Trade, Hire, Rumours, Gift; later Request passage, Demand tribute). Each action shows its likely answer before you commit.

## Testing, tuning and explaining

- **Drills** are tactical puzzles with an expected outcome, run as tests:

| Drill | Pass when | Built (AI-1 step 2) |
|---|---|---|
| Spears and cavalry against enemy cavalry | The spears face the cavalry and the AI's cavalry isn't fed into the spears | Passes: enemy cavalry only ever meets the spears' front, and the AI's cavalry never charges the front of steady spears (riding down a beaten remnant aside) |
| Outnumbered 2 to 1 on open ground | The AI withdraws or takes a flank anchor instead of fighting in the open | Passes: it never goes forward to fight, and falls back 15 tiles or anchors its right on the nearby cliff |
| Enemy line with an exposed flank | The AI's hammer hits the flank or rear within 60 s | Passes |
| A wavering enemy beside two shaken ones | The AI targets it and at least two enemy regiments rout within 30 s | Passes: it goes for the wavering one within 5 s of its first attack order |
| Own regiment wavering in the line | It is pulled back and replaced before it routs | Passes: 8 tiles back when the lines meet, a friend in its place, and it doesn't run |
| Routers fleeing | The AI pursues with light or cavalry only and stays within its leash | Passes: line regiments stay within 30 tiles of where they stood |
| A cliff beside the battlefield | The AI anchors a line end on it | Passes for the four personalities that hold ground (Iron Lord, Deep Hive, Grand Chord, Auditor), with its cavalry on the open side; the others march out to meet the enemy, as their personalities say |
| Ford between the armies | The AI waits for the enemy to cross rather than crossing under fire | Passes: it doesn't wade in for 90 s and waits facing the ford, a few tiles back from the bank; since stage 3, with the ford watched and nobody coming, it draws back from the water after 45 s to tempt them across, still facing the ford. What waiting wins is measured by `holding_the_ford_pays_off` (see the terrain rules under [Using ground](#captain-battle-tactics)); since 2026-10-17 it fights 8 seeds per personality, because single battles are chaotic, and the AI must win half of each personality's fights and five in six overall (58 of 64) |

The drills are in `crates/gc_ai/tests/drills.rs` and run in CI, each with all eight personalities unless the table says otherwise, a General in command; `AI_TRACE=1` prints the field every two seconds.

- **The arena** (`tools/gc_arena`, headless) plays thousands of AI-against-AI matches across maps, factions, personalities and difficulties, in lab mode so any result can be replayed exactly. It checks that each difficulty beats the one below at least 70% of the time, finds broken strategies and unit balance problems, and runs nightly rather than on every pull request.
- **Tuning by self-play.** Personality weights and estimator coefficients are tuned offline by letting variants play each other in the arena and keeping the winners. The results are committed as data; nothing learns during a match.
- **The AI lens** (dev overlay): shows each AI's current intents and scores, armies and objectives, the Captain's doctrine and every regiment's role, influence maps, and the estimator's prediction against what actually happened. It is how we fix bad decisions, and it's fun to watch in replays. *Built (AI-1 step 3), for the battle layers:* press `L` in any match. On the field, for every computer player: its front as a dashed line in its team's colour, a post at an end it has anchored on ground nobody crosses, a square at each regiment's place in the plan in the colour of its role, a dashed line from each regiment to the enemy it is going for (bright) or to its place when it is off it (faint), and a magenta plate under its point of attack. Marks grow as the camera pulls back. In the panel: its personality, level, plan and posture; what it expects from the fight now against what it expected at the start; its ground; how many regiments have each role; orders sent against its level's cap; and its last three journal entries, timed from the start of the match. *Built (AI-3 stage 3), for Conquest:* the same keys and marks (a Conquest mind's marks and battle lines follow the Captain the Marshal has in the field), and below the journal lines up to three more: its economy (the level's plan in brief, what it is saving Grub and Marrow for, and whether it is arming), its belief (each enemy it knows an army of, with that army's supply as it believes it and the odds it last weighed against it), and its plan (what the army waits for or is doing beyond what the summary line says).
- **The AI journal:** each mind keeps a short log of its decisions ("Attacking Mario's natural: our army 1.6× theirs, they are fighting the Brood"), saved beside the replay so "why did it do that?" always has an answer. *Built:* the last 64 entries per mind, shown in the lens and (the latest) in the sandbox panel; saving them with replays waits for replays (M2).
- **No machine-learning models in the game.** Every behaviour is hand-built and tuned offline. That keeps the AI tiny, explainable and testable, with nothing to download.

## Mistakes we design against

| Classic RTS AI failure | Countermeasure |
|---|---|
| Dithering between plans | Commitment bonus and switching cost on intents |
| Feeding units into a fight one at a time | Rally points; armies attack as one |
| Attacking into a fortress | Battle estimator; personality caution |
| Fighting strung out in column | Deploy into line before contact; never fight in a pass in column |
| Chasing a kiting unit across the map | Leash on pursuit and on Hunter roles |
| Waiting for ever for an enemy that never comes | After half a minute expecting to lose with nobody coming, it goes to fetch them; allies count in its estimate |
| Breaking an army and never finishing it | With nobody left to fight in sight, it fetches the survivors it can see and searches where the rest were last seen |
| Piling regiments onto one enemy until they trip over each other | A regiment joins a fight only where it has room beside the friends already in it |
| Ignoring morale | Morale-aware targeting, shock stacking, saving wavering regiments |
| Same attack, same lane, same minute every game | Several openings per personality, randomised per match; plans scored on the map, not scripted |
| Leaving expansions undefended | Defend intent scored from the influence map |
| Over-reacting to a feint | Threat confidence from belief; a small force draws a small response |
| Inhuman micro that feels like cheating | Attention budget and reaction time per difficulty |
| Cheating to be hard | Not allowed; difficulty is thinking, handicap is separate and visible |

## Built so far

**AI-1 step 1 (2026-10-12).** The `gc_ai` crate and the battle layers of a mind:

- **Perception** (`perception.rs`): one float copy of the field per tick, shared by every mind: each regiment's place, facing, size, strength, morale and whether it is moving or fighting, and its type's class (line, shock or ranged, from its stats). No fog exists in the sim yet, so the AI sees what everyone sees (since 2026-10-15, except enemies hidden in woods and tall grass, see below).
- **Estimator** (`estimator.rs`): plays a fight between two forces forward in one-second steps by the sim's own rules (hit chance, damage, charge bonus, flanking for the side with more melee regiments, outnumbered and half-strength morale drains, arrows while the lines close) and returns a margin from −1 to +1 and when each side breaks.
- **Captain** (`captain/`: `mod.rs` for the types and think loop, `doctrine.rs`, `site.rs`, `posture.rs`, `hunt.rs`, `roles.rs`, `helpers.rs`, `ambush.rs`): picks one of four doctrines (Hold the line, Hammer and anvil, Refused flank, Skirmish screen) by scoring them against the armies and its personality's tastes, with a commitment bonus so it doesn't dither; lays out the battle frame; gives every regiment a role (Anchor, Wing, Hammer, Screen, Battery, Reserve, Hunter, plus Recover for a wavering regiment it pulls back); and chooses a posture (forming up, holding, advancing at the pace of the slowest foot regiment, or falling back, at most twice a battle, when the estimate is bad and the enemy is still far off). It plays the morale game as described above: local odds, focus on wavering enemies, timed hammer charges into an enemy locked in melee, pursuit within a leash to stop routers rallying. Once what stands against it is about a third of its own strength it **finishes them off**, sending every regiment after the nearest remnant, so a rallied straggler can't drag a won battle on. While its side's champion duels between the lines ([05](05-combat.md#champions-before-battle)) it halts its advance and watches ("Halting to watch the champions"); the time spent watching doesn't count against its patience for a standoff.
- **Orders** (`orders.rs`): every decision waits out the level's reaction time and spends from its attention budget; regiments sent the same way in one look go as one order, like a box select and one click; routine moves keep half the budget in hand for emergencies; nothing is re-sent that would change nothing.
- **Difficulty** (`difficulty.rs`): Recruit (1.5 s reaction, 20 orders a minute, two doctrines, no reserve, loose leash, slips often), Veteran (0.8 s, 45, all four doctrines, reserve, timed hammer, saves wavering regiments, focus) and General (0.6 s, 90, everything, rare slips, looks at the field every 0.2 s; its reaction was 0.4 s until charges that plough in, 2026-10-24, after which the quicker General beat the Veteran only 74% of the time over 400 arena battles against 81% before, and at 0.8 s it was back to 81%. Generals on the field ([37](37-commander.md)) cut that to 66%, because a general's fall swings a battle whoever plays better; at 0.6 s it is 73 to 75% (293 and 298 of 400 battles from two seed sets) and 91% against charge straight in. Aiming for the enemy general, or holding one's own back as a reserve, both measured worse, so the computer players don't play around generals yet). The Warlord (0.25 s, 150, never slips, and [rehearses](#rehearsal-thinking-ahead-with-the-real-simulation)) came with AI-2.
- **Personalities** (`data/ai/personalities.ron`): the eight in the table above, with traits, doctrine tastes and an army preset; a match picks one of the faction's two at random.
- **Journal** (`journal.rs`): each mind's last 64 decisions with the tick ("Hold the line: expects a close fight", "Falling back: expects to lose", "Finishing them off: 3 regiments left").
- **Field Battle armies** (`army.rs`): ten regiments from the faction's roster in a preset mix, set out with the line in front, archers behind and fast troops on the wings.
- **In the game** (`gc_match/src/battle.rs`): lobby AI slots become minds at their level; teams reach the sim (engine hook 1); the sandbox panel shows each mind's one-line summary and `I` hands any side to the computer. See [06 · Game modes](06-game-modes.md#field-battle-extras).

**AI-1 step 2 (2026-10-13).** Reading the ground, and the drills:

- **Ground** (`ground.rs`, `terrain.rs`): the AI's copy of the map now has each tile's height and the water line as well as the walkable-ground grid. It answers whether a line has dry footing, whether an end is covered by ground nobody crosses, how much higher it stands than the ground in front, whether there is water between two places, and where a river can be waded.
- **Captain:** picks its ground and waits behind water as described in [Using ground](#captain-battle-tactics); a line with a covered end leads with it and puts its hammers on the open side; expecting to lose, it waits for the enemy instead of marching out. It also chooses a **point of attack** (the wavering enemy beside the most shaken friends, whose rout would spread) that line regiments reach a little further for; its reserve fills a gap in the line before helping elsewhere; and while finishing off a beaten army, a regiment answers anyone charging at it before going after the stragglers.
- **In the game:** the generated map's heights and water reach the minds in the lobby battle and the sandbox.
- **Tests:** the eight [drills](#testing-tuning-and-explaining) and the estimator check, both in CI.

Its CI bar is back to 21 of 30 (it wins 26). The Warlord, with the same reach, still beats the General 78% of the time (94 of 120).

On the generated map with the real units (Ember Clans mirror armies, `cargo test -p gc_match --release watch -- --ignored`), every battle ends within two to five minutes, and a Veteran marches out and beats a side that only stands still (a CI test). **AI-1 step 3 (2026-10-13).** The AI lens: `gc_ai::lens` lays out what each mind is thinking (its front, every regiment's role, place and target, its point of attack, and the panel's lines), and `gc_game/src/lens.rs` draws it when `L` is pressed. **Ambushes (2026-10-15).** With woods and tall grass on the maps ([20 · Ambushes](20-ambushes.md)):

- **Fair sight** (engine hook 4's first half): on a map with cover each mind thinks on `Perception::capture_for`, which leaves out the enemies its side can't see and marks its own hidden regiments; the simulation refuses an attack order on a hidden regiment anyway. Each mind keeps its own look and remembers an enemy it lost sight of for 60 seconds (`Perception::remembered()`, where it was last seen), which the Captain counts when it judges whether the enemy is beaten (so it doesn't throw everything forward because two regiments stepped into a wood), but never attacks. On maps without cover nothing changes.
- **The Ambush doctrine and the Ambusher role** (`captain/ambush.rs`): the Veteran and General hide up to three regiments (fast troops, or with none the ends of the line) in a wood or tall grass off an end of the line while the armies are apart, hold until the enemy comes on, and spring when an enemy is locked in melee nearby, walks close, or spots them; a sprung ambusher is a hammer. It scores as Hammer and anvil plus a bonus for cunning, patient personalities when there is somewhere to hide, and each personality's `ambush` liking. The AI lens shows ambushers in moss green, and on a map with cover marks only the regiments the player's side can see.
- **Tests:** `gc_ai/tests/ambush.rs`.

**Smarter AI stage 3 (2026-10-16): team games and reading the ground.** Groundwork for the Conquest AI, measured with two new tables in `gc_match` (`crates/gc_match/src/battle_tables.rs`), which play the AI with these rules against the same commanders without them (`Knobs::team_play` and `Knobs::read_ground` turned off), Ember Clans mirror armies on 256-tile maps, 12 minutes at most:

- `cargo test -p gc_match --release team_table -- --ignored --nocapture` (`AI_MODE=3v3` or `2v2v2`)
- `cargo test -p gc_match --release terrain_table -- --ignored --nocapture` (`AI_GROUND=hill`, `river`, `fords`, `woods`, `mixed` or `generated`)

Both read `AI_LEVEL`, `AI_BATTLES` and `AI_FROM`; `AI_OLD=1` plays old against old for the baseline, and `AI_OFF=team_play`, `read_ground` or `none` takes away just one of them, or nothing.

**Team games.** With six players the AI used to count every enemy army on the field against it. In a 2v2v2 that meant four armies, so every side "expected heavy defeat" and fell back, and the pair that arrived last won: from the east 90% of the time, from the west 15% and from the middle never. Now (`captain/team.rs`, every level):

- **Who is really coming.** Every army on the field is turned towards its nearest hostile army. The Captain weighs only the army it faces and the enemies turned on its side, with the friends turned on those; an enemy busy with a third side doesn't count against it. Its front is measured to those enemies alone, so an army fighting behind it doesn't stop its line.
- **Letting two others fight first.** When the nearest enemy army is hostile to another one nearer it, the Captain holds and lets them fight ("Letting player A and player B fight it out"). If it stands nearer the first of them than the second does, or either comes within 40 tiles, it steps back out of the way, away from both ("Falling back: keeping out of their fight"). Once either is locked in melee with anyone but its side, it falls on them ("Falling on player X while it fights"). It waits at most 90 seconds, and stepping aside doesn't use up the two times it may fall back from a losing fight. Once only two sides are left it fights as in any battle.
- **Measured** (General, 2v2v2, 90 battles with the new pair taking each column in turn): the new pair wins **61%** (old against old: 33%), 26 of 30 from the west, 29 of 30 from the east and none of 30 from the middle, where two pairs close in from both sides whatever it does; the Veteran 60%. Three new pairs never stall (30 battles, all decided). In a 3v3 there are only two sides, so nothing changes (46% over 24, the same as before within chance); three ways to share out targets across a team (the target nearest the team, the far ally holding, going to a locked ally's help) all measured no better and were dropped.

**Reading the ground.** For the Veteran and above (`captain/site.rs` and `ground.rs`):

- **Fords.** With a river between the armies and both sides waiting, after 45 seconds the Captain looks along the bank for a ford (`ground::fords`, one per wadeable stretch) whose far side no enemy is within 30 tiles of and that it reaches at least 20 tiles before them, and crosses there ("Crossing at a ford they aren't watching"), unless it expects to lose by more than a little. It forms up on the far bank, then fights as usual; if they come to watch the ford before anyone wades in, it waits again. With every ford watched, it draws back from the water to tempt them across ("Drawing back from the water to tempt them across"). What it buys is an end to standoffs: with both sides new, 13 battles in 100 are still undecided after 12 minutes on a river with two fords far apart and 13 on one with one or two fords close together, against 35 and 40 in 100 with both sides old. Against the old AI it gains nothing: over 200 battles on each, 59 won to 73 lost with two fords (old against old 64 to 66), 58 to 62 with one or two (63 to 57), each within chance of even. One AI holding its one ford against another still never comes to blows; that is right, and a player can always cross.
- **Hills.** The estimate counts the 10% harder blows of whoever will fight downhill (where contact will be, at least half a tile above the other). Picking its ground, it also looks 40 tiles ahead for a rise, worth more the worse its odds, and goes forward to one it reaches 15 tiles before the enemy. Measured neutral: 104 to 96 on battlefields built round a hill (the same as old against old), 188 to 211 on 400 generated maps (191 to 208). The downhill bonus is small, and a line that advances doesn't stop on a hill to receive the enemy; if hills should matter more in play, that is for the terrain rules.
- **Woods.** Facing better archers it values standing in a wood (where trees catch 30% of arrows), and once there no longer marches out to close with them. Also neutral: 100 to 100 on battlefields with woods (99 to 101 old against old); with a hill, a river and woods together, 76 to 73 (72 to 73).

**Not yet:** avoiding passes and chokepoints; rehearsing smaller decisions (committing the reserve, whether a charge will break a line) and feints and bait (beyond drawing back from a river); in team games, sharing out targets and helping an ally under attack, and a way out for a pair caught in the middle of a 2v2v2; cavalry keeping out of woods; the Marshal, deployment zones and the army builder; Envelopment and the other faction doctrines; belief about enemies out of sight beyond the 60-second memory (the Spymaster): guessing where a lost regiment went, and remembering the enemy's whole army for the battle.

**AI-3 stage 1 (2026-10-17): Conquest computer players.** Every AI slot in a Conquest lobby now gets a computer player (a "mind") at its level, and the mind plays the whole game: gathering, building, training, expanding, attacking, defending and going for the Titan's Heart.

- **Code:** `crates/gc_ai/src/conquest/` holds `kit.rs` (the faction's build menu), `placing.rs` (where buildings go), `steward.rs` (the economy), `marshal.rs` (the army) and `mod.rs`, which ties them together as a `Realm`. The level's orders per minute are split as a player splits their attention between base and battle: 40% go on the economy, through its own `Hands` (`orders.rs`), and 60% on the army. Every order waits out the level's reaction time, and the mind doesn't repeat an order to a building or worker before the last one has landed. `crates/gc_game/src/conquest_ai.rs` gives AI slots their minds and sets up the headless test games.
- **In the game:** in online games the host runs every mind, and they send ordinary player commands, so hosted online lobbies work too; replays are unchanged. With fog on, a mind sees only what its own side sees.
- **Steward (economy):** sends idle workers to the Grub seeps and Marrow veins near its finished drop-off buildings. It gathers Marrow once it trains soldiers or has tiered up, until 600 Marrow is banked; with 1,200 or more banked it moves one Marrow gatherer to Grub every 5 seconds. It trains workers up to a target (at most 48), builds housing before supply runs out, builds production buildings (2 per tier), tiers up, and expands with a second hall after 2.5 to 5 minutes depending on the personality's greed (the Recruit never expands). It trains an army mixed roughly half line troops, a fifth shock troops and three-tenths ranged, plus siege breakers, and rallies them in front of home.
- **Placement:** buildings keep 3 tiles apart (1 tile between two small buildings such as housing, towers and walls) and leave lanes from halls to their seeps free, so regiments can always walk through a base.
- **Marshal (army):** gathers at the rally point and sets out when its wave is big enough (the wave grows with game time and is smaller for aggressive personalities) or its supply is full. It attacks the nearest enemy building it knows of, or scouts the map starts it hasn't seen yet. It doesn't scout starts next to its own or an ally's buildings. At the target (once the middle of the army, or any regiment, is close: a big army crowding round a base in a corner may never get its middle there) it knocks buildings down, towers first (re-sending the order if the first one didn't take), and falls back when the attack has lost 65% of its soldiers; after a beaten attack it regroups at home for 30 seconds before setting out again. Reinforcements join in groups of 3. It defends when enemies are at its buildings in strength (at home, or a force a quarter the size of its army). Once the Titan's Heart opens (20 minutes) it goes to take and hold it when its wave is ready or an enemy holds it. Six regiments stand on the Heart and stay there, out of the battle around it, while the rest screen them; a threat to outlying buildings (an expansion, even a Brood Hive) doesn't pull the army off the Heart, but a threat at home does. An army at full supply that has fought for the Heart for 10 minutes without holding it gives the Heart a rest for 8 minutes and raids the nearest enemy main building it knows of instead: four full armies brawling on the Heart is otherwise a stalemate that never ends. Each fight near the army is fought by the battle AI (the Captain), which presses on across rivers rather than waiting behind them when attacking or defending.
- **Faction economies** (`EconomyStyle` in `kit.rs`, picked from the faction's worker: one that burrows plays the veins, one that only builds plays tuning):
  - **Ember Clans and Carrion Concord:** workers carry loads to drop-off halls; the Clans expand with the Outpost Hall.
  - **Brood (root-veins):** Gnawers burrow in at the seeps and veins on its linked root-veins. It builds its Pustules (its housing) towards the nearest seep the veins don't reach yet, inside the Hive's veins so they link, and expands with a new Hive. It trains only when a larva is ready, adds a Hatchery Pit (one per tier) when larvae run out while Grub piles up, and sends no second Gnawer to a building already growing by itself.
  - **Choir (tuning):** its workers only build, so it keeps three plus one per tier. It puts a Resonator by each seep near its buildings (its housing too) and a Siphon by each Marrow vein once it wants Marrow, and expands with a Resonator by a far seep. Its production buildings go where its fields of power reach. Its economy counts in the workers its tuned nodes stand for, when it decides on production and tiers.
- **How well it plays:** AI-only Veteran games all end in a win: Ribcage Pass one-on-one (Ember Clans v Carrion Concord, Brood v Choir) and four-player Four Teeth games, by Annihilation or by holding the Titan's Heart: with the Brood's root-veins and the Choir's tuning (2026-10-17), 12 of 12 four-player seeds ended in a win between 24 and 75 game minutes (CI allows 90), and every pairing of the four factions one-on-one, two seeds each, between 7 and 25 minutes. A whole hour-long game plays in seconds. Four-player games mostly go to the Carrion Concord; one-on-one, the Choir beats every faction in 7 to 11 minutes and the Brood loses to all, which is for faction balance to look at.
- **Tests and watching:** CI plays these games every run: `cargo test --release -p gc_game ai_only -- --ignored`. To watch one: `AI_MAP=1 AI_SEED=7 AI_TRACE=1 cargo test --release -p gc_game conquest_ai_watch -- --ignored --nocapture` (`AI_MAP` 0 is Ribcage Pass, 1 is Four Teeth; `AI_FACTIONS` picks the factions by their place in `Faction::ALL`, such as `12` for the Brood against the Choir; `AI_TRACE=2` also prints every journal line; `AI_LEVEL` and `AI_MINUTES` also work).

**AI-3 stage 2 (2026-10-17): levels that play the long game differently.** The four Conquest levels now differ in how they run the economy and pick their fights, not only in reaction time and orders per minute, and each beats the one below it at least 70% of the time.

- **Code:** `crates/gc_ai/src/conquest/strategy.rs` holds each level's `Strategy`; `spymaster.rs` holds what a mind believes about each enemy's army.
- **Spymaster (belief):** a mind counts the enemy regiments its side sees now and, at the place and strength it last saw them however long ago, those still on the field (perception's memory forgets a regiment once it is gone from the field). It never knows of a regiment it hasn't seen. The battle estimator weighs that army against its own, the fronts 15 tiles apart and closing at a walk, giving odds from -1 (a rout of ours) to +1 (a rout of theirs).
- **Strategy per level:**

| | **Recruit** | **Veteran** | **General** | **Warlord** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Workers it keeps, of what its nodes take | 60% | 70% | 85% | 100% |
| Production buildings per tier | 1 | 2 | 2 | 3 |
| Keeps an army up and weighs the odds | No | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Counters the army it has seen | No | No | Yes | Yes |
| Strikes a base whose army is away | No | No | No | Yes |
| First expansion, once its seeps are worked | Only when they run low | After 2.5 to 5 min | After 2.5 to 5 min | 30% sooner |

- **Keeping an army up (the Steward's "arming"):** a level that weighs the odds keeps at least 4 supply of soldiers a minute of game time (up to 40), and arms again whenever an enemy army it knows of would beat its own (odds below -0.1), until it is at the supply limit. While arming it trains soldiers before workers and puts off timed expansion and new production buildings; it still tiers up, and expands when its seeps run low. Without this the Veteran grew so greedily that the Recruit's early waves beat it 63% of the time.
- **Picking fights (the Marshal):** a level that weighs the odds goes for the enemy it has the best odds against (the nearest building it knows of for each enemy, the odds less a little for distance) and only weighs enemies whose army it has seen (else it goes as the others do), and sets out when its wave is ready and the odds clear 0.05 plus up to 0.3 more for a cautious personality, or when its supply is full and waiting gains nothing. With odds of 0.5 or better it goes before its wave is full. The Warlord, as an opportunist, counts only the enemy regiments within 50 tiles of the target, so it hits a base whose army is away. Its journal says the odds it attacked at.
- **Countering:** a General or Warlord moves its army make-up towards what beats the army it has seen: half of the enemy's share of riders and shock troops goes to its line, of archers to its shock troops, and of line troops to its archers.
- **How well it plays:** the Conquest arena plays mirror one-on-ones on Ribcage Pass, every faction against itself with the same personality on both sides, six seeds, each game played twice with the sides swapped (48 games a pairing, 45 minutes at most): the Veteran beats the Recruit in 43 of 48 (89%), the General beats the Veteran in 42 of 48 (87%) and the Warlord beats the General in 35 of 48 (72%). Four-player Veteran games on Four Teeth now end sooner: 12 of 12 seeds between 17 and 37 game minutes. The economy is the strongest lever: with equal workers, a General's countering and odds alone beat a Veteran about half the time. To run it: `AI_A=Warlord AI_B=General AI_GAMES=6 cargo test --release -p gc_game conquest_arena -- --ignored --nocapture`.

**AI-3 stage 3 (2026-10-17): openings, Conquest panels in the AI lens, six-player Wilds.**

- **Openings** (`data/ai/openings.ron`, `crates/gc_ai/src/conquest/opening.rs`): how a mind spends its first minutes. Four so far, two or three per personality, picked at random per match: **Steady** (everyone: six workers, one production building, the usual waves), **Early pressure** (the Red Banner, the Hungering, the Dissonance: two production buildings on five workers, at most ten workers, a first wave of 10 supply, for 7 minutes), **Fast expansion** (the Deep Hive, the Grand Chord, the Auditor, the Hungering: a second base at minute 2, then the usual rules) and **Walls first** (the Iron Lord, the Grand Chord, the Long Ledger, the Auditor: two towers by the hall). A Recruit always plays Steady; a Warlord picks one marked as doing well against a faction it faces (its enemies only, not its allies). The small first wave is spent once the army first sets out, for an enemy or for the Heart. Each opening wins 43 to 59% against Steady in Veteran mirror games (32 a pairing: Early pressure 59%, Fast expansion 50%, Walls first 43%), so none is a sure thing, and the levels still beat each other (Veteran over Recruit 91%, General over Veteran 77%, Warlord over General 83%). The journal and the lens say which opening a mind plays. To try one: `AI_OPEN_A="Early pressure" AI_OPEN_B=Steady AI_A=Veteran AI_B=Veteran cargo test --release -p gc_game conquest_arena -- --ignored --nocapture`, or `AI_OPENINGS` (one name per player) on the watch test.
- **Six-player Wilds:** a stalemate on the Heart (an army that has fought for it 10 minutes without holding it) now sends its army raiding once its wave is ready, not only when its supply is full, and to an enemy start it hasn't looked at when it has seen no base: on the big Wilds maps armies often meet only at the Heart. Six Veterans on a Wilds map (`AI_MAP=2 AI_FACTIONS=012301`) ended 6 of 6 seeds, in 37 to 131 game minutes; four-player Four Teeth games end in 18 to 50 (12 of 12 seeds). CI plays one six-player Wilds game.
- **Conquest panels in the AI lens:** `L` in a Conquest match with computer players shows the lens, and each Conquest mind's panel now ends with an `economy` line (`opens with Early pressure until 7:00; keeps 70% workers, 2 producers a tier   saving: 150 Grub   arming: short of an army`), a `belief` line (`Brood 34 supply, odds -0.12 as of 6:30`, one entry for each enemy whose army it knows, the supply counted from the regiments it has seen and the odds the Marshal last weighed against that enemy and when, which are missing until it has weighed any) and, when there is something to add to the summary line, a `plan` line (gathering until its wave is ready, ready with its wave or a full supply, regrouping after a costly attack, raiding, or how long it has fought for the Heart). The Marshal and Steward only record these as they think (`Marshal::belief`, `odds` and `wave`, `Steward::arming`), and nothing reads them back, so the lens changes no decision: the arena gives the same results with and without it. The Kit now lists every faction's unit types with their supply (`all_units`), so a mind can size an enemy army of another faction.

**AI-3 stage 4 (2026-10-17): smarter scouting.**

- **The scout** (`crates/gc_ai/src/conquest/scout.rs`): the Marshal lends one fast regiment out of the army as a scout; no wave, battle or attack counts it. A Veteran sends it once, with an army of 4 regiments or more and at least 2 starts an enemy might hold, to look at each such start, nearest first, then it comes home for good (it gives up after 6 minutes, and gives the regiment back after 90 seconds on the way home wherever it is, and in a one-on-one it sends none: the enemy's start is known). A General or Warlord keeps one out all game, going to the place it knows least about (the starts and the middle of each cluster of seeps), less a little for distance, and replaces a lost scout after a minute. It falls back from enemy soldiers that outweigh it, steers clear for a while of a place where it met enemies, and lets a lone enemy no bigger than itself pass (two scouts meeting would otherwise turn each other back for ever). The lens shows a `scout` line, such as `looking at the start in the north-east, 2 of 3 starts seen`.
- **Reading a build** (`spymaster::read_builds`): from the enemy buildings its side has seen, a General or Warlord reads each enemy's opening in its first minutes: an **early attack** (two production buildings and one hall by 3:30), a **fast expansion** (a second hall by 5:00) or **towers and a slow build** (two towers by 5:00), and keeps one read per enemy. The Brood trains from its Hive and has no production buildings, so its early attacks can't be read from buildings.
- **Answering it** (`Strategy::answer`), until minute 8, the most pressing read first: against an early attack a tower and a second production building and no early expansion; against a fast expansion an early attack of 10 supply; against towers an expansion of its own straight away. The journal says what it saw and how it answers (`Scouted Carrion Concord: a fast expansion; answering with an early attack`) and the lens's `economy` line adds `answering Carrion Concord's a fast expansion`.
- **Guessing where a lost regiment went** (`spymaster::guess`): a regiment it has lost sight of while it was marching is believed to have gone on the way it faced, at its pace, for up to 45 seconds, and to be anywhere within half the ground it could have covered since, up to 25 tiles. The Warlord, as an opportunist, counts a defender of a base if its guessed place could be within reach.
- **Openings now do what they say:** an opening's buildings come before soldiers while one is still due (its towers, its production after the first, its early expansion), for up to 2 minutes if it can't get one up, and soldiers come first otherwise when the army is short. Before this, arming spent the money an opening saved, so early pressure got its second production building late and fast expansions came at the usual time.
- **How well it plays:** in the arena (96 games a pairing, with stage 5 in) the Veteran beats the Recruit 87%, the General the Veteran 72% and the Warlord the General 73%. Reading and answering builds is about even for the General (76% with it off, within the arena's spread of about 5 points either way); the levels still differ most by their economy. CI plays one General one-on-one in which a scout must look at the enemy's start within 5 minutes.

**AI-3 stage 5 (2026-10-17): big plans.**

- **A Sovereign** (`crates/gc_ai/src/conquest/sovereign.rs`) sits above the Steward and the Marshal and thinks every 3 seconds. Formal alliances need diplomacy in the sim (after 1.0), so its alliances are informal: in a game of two or more rival sides, everyone goes for the leader.
- **Ganging up on the leader** (General and Warlord): it scores each rival by what it has seen, its army's supply plus 12 for each hall and 3 for each other building, and goes for the one furthest ahead first, keeping that choice until another is 25% further ahead. A rival whose soldiers (6 supply or more) come at its buildings earns a **grudge**, from 80 seconds to 4 minutes by the personality's vengefulness, and a grudge comes before the leader. The Marshal then attacks that enemy's nearest building it knows of, and with odds weighed counts going for it as worth 0.3 more odds, so it turns aside only for a much weaker target. One-on-one and in a two-team game it changes nothing.
- **Timing pushes** (Warlord): it plans a push between minute 7 (the most aggressive) and minute 10, and then one every 6 minutes. For 100 seconds before it, the Steward arms and the Marshal holds the army back (it still strikes at odds of 0.5 or better); at the push it sets out with everything it has, unless the odds are hopeless (below -0.15). A push the army can't take within 30 seconds (it is defending or already out) is skipped and the next one planned.
- **Towers fire** (2026-10-24, [26 · Fortifications](26-fortifications.md) stage 1): when a mind weighs the odds of attacking a building, the enemy towers it has seen within their range plus 8 tiles of that building shoot for the defenders in the estimate as archers that never rout and must be knocked down (`spymaster::odds_at`, `Force::with_tower`), so towers alone are no walkover, and raiders keep out of each enemy tower's range from high ground plus 4 tiles (it was a flat 18 tiles).
- **Raids on workers** (Warlord, `crates/gc_ai/src/conquest/raider.rs`): after minute 5, with an army of 6 fighting regiments or more and 2 waiting at the rally point, the Marshal lends its 2 fastest to raid enemy workers it has seen in the last 3 minutes: the focus enemy's first, then those with no tower within 18 tiles and no enemy soldiers it knows of within 22, nearest first, or with none known an enemy expansion hall. They attack the workers in sight, move on to the next spot when one is empty, and run home when enemy soldiers in sight outweigh them or they have lost half their men; after a 45-second rest they go again, and once spent they rejoin the army and new raiders are picked 90 seconds later. They rejoin the army at once when it defends home or holds for a timing push. The journal says when a raid sets out and why it comes back, and the lens adds a `raid` line (`2 regiments raiding Brood workers at (210, 48)`). CI plays a four-player Warlord game in which a raid must set out and kill workers.
- **Journal and lens:** the journal says whom it goes for and why (`Choir leads: going for them first`, `Carrion Concord attacked us: going for them first`), when it plans a push and when the push sets out (`Timing push with 10 regiments: odds +0.99`). The lens adds an `aims` line (`going for Choir first: they lead; timing push at 8:57`), and the `plan` line says when it holds back for a push.
- **How well it plays:** the Warlord beats the General 73 to 80% in the arena with pushes and raids (96 games, runs before and after the review fixes). Four-player Warlord games on Four Teeth end in 17 to 59 minutes (12 of 12 seeds); six-player Veteran games on the Wilds in 29 to 131 (6 of 6).

**Not yet:** guessing what an enemy builds out of sight (only what its side has seen of a base is read); the Sovereign's full utility scoring of every intent (it has the leader, grudges and timing pushes so far); formal alliances, which need diplomacy in the sim; allies answering pings; faction powers in the AI's hands; and the faction-specific economies the design lists for each Steward (caravans, chords as geometry).
