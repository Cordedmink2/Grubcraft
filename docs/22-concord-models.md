# 22 · Concord models

The Carrion Concord is the merchant faction of Grubcraft. Its units are Dutch merchants, debt-collecting cavalry and skeletons with debt tags on their wrists, lit by mint ghost light and amber lanterns. This document covers its fourteen 3D models: how they are made, where the sources live, and the numbers behind them. The look itself is described in [09 · Art and Audio](09-art-and-audio.md), and the credits for the free packs we build on are in [CREDITS.md](../CREDITS.md).

**Built so far:** all fourteen models are built to final quality and listed in `assets-src/models.ron`. The game draws only one of them today (see [Known limits](#known-limits)).

## What this is

The Concord's style is macabre merchant. Living merchants wear black coats, ruffs and tall hats. The dead wear the same trade's gear, with debt tags, chains and padlocks. Three things carry the look across all fourteen models:

- **Mint ghost light** (`#C8F2DC`) glows in the eyes, lanterns and cloth of ghosts and the undead.
- **Lantern amber** (`#FFB347`) is the warm light of the Concord's lanterns.
- **Team colour** is the colour of the player who owns the unit. Each model paints it on a few fixed parts, such as a sash, a pennon or a hem, so you can tell whose unit it is at a glance. Everything else keeps its own colour.

The fourteen models cover the faction's whole roster: a worker, a trade wagon, the soldiers, the cavalry, a siege engine, three heroes, and the summoned dead. The Grubfolk Militia are neutral hirelings who are listed under the Concord, so they are included.

## How they are made

Each model is made by a Python script rather than by hand in a 3D editor, so anyone can rebuild it and get the same result. The steps, in order:

1. **Blender, run from code.** Blender is an open-source 3D modelling program. We use its Python module, `bpy`, which is installed with pip into a virtual environment (a private folder of Python packages called `bvenv`). Each script builds one model from nothing.
2. **Stock bodies and rigs.** The humanoids start from Quaternius's CC0 bodies. A *rig* is the skeleton of bones inside a model that moves its skin. The bodies share Quaternius's 62-bone rig, so the stock animation clips (named animations such as Walk, Run and Die) play on them without new animation work.
3. **High-poly build with procedural materials.** Each model is first built as a detailed high-poly mesh (a mesh is the surface of the model, made of triangles; high-poly means many of them). Its materials, such as cloth, iron, bone and glow, are *procedural*: generated by code from rules, not painted by hand.
4. **Game mesh and baked maps.** `lowpoly.py` turns the high-poly build into a much simpler game mesh and *bakes* the surface detail into texture images, which means it copies the detail onto the simpler mesh. The maps are colour, team (which parts take the team colour), normal (fake surface bumps for light to catch), ORM (occlusion, roughness and metalness, which control how light sits on the surface) and emissive (where the model glows).
5. **Weapon and shield bones.** `rigkit.py` adds a Weapon bone and a Shield bone, so a held weapon or shield moves as one piece with the hand. The arm is posed with *inverse kinematics* (IK): you say where the hand should be and the arm bends to reach it. `rigkit.py` works that out in advance and writes the result into the plain clips, so the game plays ordinary animation and needs no IK at run time.
6. **Rigid skeleton parts for the undead.** `skel.py` builds the bony parts of the undead as separate pieces, each fixed to one bone of the humanoid rig. Bones stay bone-shaped and do not stretch the way skin does.
7. **Custom horse.** `horse.py` makes a 26-bone skeletal horse. Its walking and running patterns (its *gaits*) are generated by code instead of being animated by hand. The Debt Collectors and the Caravan both use it. On the Debt Collectors the horse carries the human rider's hips, so the rider and mount are one rig.
8. **Moving parts.** The Caravan's wheels spin on their own bones. The Mausoleum Engine has bones for its hull, four wheels, two doors (left and right), a ram and a bell, so each one moves on its own.
9. **Four levels of detail, then the pack.** `gc_artbake` makes four versions of each model with fewer triangles each: LOD 0 (level of detail 0) is the closest and most detailed, and LOD 3 is the simplest, for the farthest view. It then writes all the models into `assets/models.pak`, the pack the game reads.

## Budgets

Every model is held to a *budget*: a maximum triangle count for each of its four LODs. The bake checks each level against its budget. The budget depends on what kind of unit the model is.

| Budget class | Models | LOD 0 | LOD 1 | LOD 2 | LOD 3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Soldier, summoned or worker | Ledger Clerk, Bone Pikes, Crossbow Auditors, Gravewardens, Lantern Wraiths, Bound Dead, Grubfolk Militia | 8,400 | 3,000 | 1,000 | 350 |
| Hero | The Magnate, The Undertaker, The Usurer | 12,000 | 4,000 | 1,400 | 450 |
| Rider and mount | Debt Collectors | 14,000 | 5,000 | 1,700 | 550 |
| Single big model | Caravan, Mausoleum Engine, The Insolvent | 20,000 | 7,000 | 2,400 | 800 |

The other limits that apply to every model:

- **One material per model.**
- **Maps at 512 × 512** pixels in the game. The baker shrinks larger maps to this size.
- **Up to 63 bones** per rig.

**Height rule.** A model's height in `assets-src/models.ron` is set from its size in its rest pose, which is the neutral pose it stands in before any animation. Take the height of the smallest box that holds the model in that pose, multiply it by 1.6, and divide by 2.64. This is the rule the Yari Ashigaru was scaled with, so the Concord models share its scale on the field.

## Where the sources live

The sources live in the shared project folder, not in the repository.

- **Build scripts and helpers:** `/mnt/project-files/design/faction-looks/concord-roster/source/`. One script per unit is in `units/`. The helpers are `kit.py` (parts, materials and rig helpers), `concord.py` (Concord materials and props), `rigkit.py` (weapon and shield bones, IK baking), `skel.py` (rigid skeleton parts), `horse.py` (the horse), `lowpoly.py` (high-poly to game mesh and maps), `render.py` and `preview.py`.
- **Facts for each model:** `/mnt/project-files/design/faction-looks/concord-roster/facts.json`, with the name, slug, triangle and bone counts, height, budget, clips and script for every model.
- **Game-ready files** (the glTF model and its PNG maps): `/mnt/project-files/assets-src/grubcraft/concord/<slug>/`.
- **Review renders** (three-quarter, back and side views, and a clips sheet): `/mnt/project-files/design/faction-looks/concord-roster/review/<slug>/`.

## Rebuilding one model

The build tools need Blender's Python module (`pip install bpy` in a virtual environment) and the Quaternius "Ultimate Modular Men" glTF files. `kit.py` holds the path to those files and `bake.sh` holds the path to the virtual environment, so edit both for your machine first.

1. From the `source/` folder, run `bake.sh <script> <out folder> <slug>` (for example `bake.sh bone_pikes pikes bone-pikes`). It runs `units/<script>.py`, which builds the model as a Blender file, and then `lowpoly.py`, which makes the game mesh and its maps. The script names are in `facts.json`.
2. Copy the baked model and its maps from `<out folder>/asset/` to `/mnt/project-files/assets-src/grubcraft/concord/<slug>/`.
3. Check the model's entry in `assets-src/models.ron`, including its path, height and maps.
4. From the repository root, run `cargo run --release -p gc_artbake`. This rewrites `assets/models.pak` and `assets/models.manifest`. Commit those with the `models.ron` change, as [09 · Art and Audio](09-art-and-audio.md) describes.

## Known limits

- **Only one of the fourteen is drawn in the game today.** The game draws models only for unit types listed in `data/units.ron`, and Bone Pikes is the only Concord unit listed there. The rest appear in the `--showroom all` view, which shows every model.
- **Caravan and Debt Collectors use a skeletal horse.** Its bones and gaits come from `horse.py`, so any change to the horse changes both units.
- **Ghost-cloth glow is a dim emissive map.** The glow on ghostly cloth comes from a dim emissive map (the map that says where a surface glows), so it is fainter than the mint light in the eyes and lanterns.
- **Riders sit in a fixed seat.** The Debt Collectors' rider stays in one seat on the horse.
