# 09 · Art and Audio Direction

The art direction has two jobs: look great from a bird's-eye camera, and cost almost nothing in memory. These turn out to be the same job.

## Visual style: "painted diorama"

Stylized low-poly with rich lighting, as if the battlefield were a hand-painted tabletop diorama lit by a late-afternoon sun.

- **Flat-shaded, chunky shapes** with strong silhouettes. Detail comes from *form and light*, not texture.
- **Palette texturing:** most models carry no unique texture at all. Each vertex points into a shared **palette texture** (a small gradient atlas, for example 256 × 16). A whole faction's units and buildings share one palette. This is the biggest single memory saving in the game: hundreds of models, a few kilobytes of colour.
- **Team colour** is a palette row swap, free at render time.
- **Lighting does the heavy lifting:** one sun with soft shadows, hemispheric sky/ground ambient, a rim light to pop units off the terrain, a subtle colour grade per map, and emissive Grub that glows. **Built so far (2026-10-23, [30 · Cinematic lighting](30-cinematic-lighting.md)):** five prepared looks (Noon, Golden hour by default, Overcast, Storm, Dusk) with a low warm sun, a sky, height fog warm toward the sun, a film curve with colour grade, grain and dither, cloud shadows, rim light, backlit grass and leaves, and ground occlusion baked from the heights; `--look NAME` and `F9` choose one. The colour grade is a few numbers in the shader, not a lookup table.
- **Terrain** gets the only "real" textures: a small set of tiling materials (soil, hide, grass, rock, bone, bog, Grub-soaked earth) blended by a splat map, plus painted decals.
- **Readability over realism:** soldiers are slightly oversized relative to buildings (classic RTS proportion), heads and weapons slightly exaggerated so you can tell spears from swords from the default zoom.

### Buildings
- Buildings are 3D models built in Blender by script, from a shared kit and bake script in the shared project folder (`design/buildings/source/`, brief in `design/buildings/BRIEF.md`). Built 2026-10-17, starting with the Ember Clans' Clan Hall.
- Each building has four looks (foundation, frame with scaffolding, nearly done, finished), so its construction is visible. Each faction builds in its own way, and each building uses one material shared by its four looks. See [21 · Buildings](21-buildings.md#models-and-construction-stages) for the stages, budgets and packs.

### The world
- The Titan is everywhere at large scale: ridges are visibly ribs, lakes sit in old eye-sockets, forests grow like fur. At play scale it reads as natural terrain with an unsettling hint.
- Grub glows a warm amber-green and pulses slowly. Seeps are the brightest thing on the map, so economy is readable from far zoom.
- Lighting presets per map (dawn, noon, dusk, overcast, moonlit) rather than a day/night cycle.

### Animation
- Soldiers animate through **baked vertex animation textures** (VAT, see [10 · Architecture](10-architecture.md#rendering)). Each unit type has idle, walk, run, attack (2 variants), hit, die (2 variants), rout-run and cheer.
- **No clones:** every soldier gets a stable look of his own: a tone that lightens or darkens and warms or cools his cloth, skin and leather by up to about 8% (team colours vary by a third of that, so teams stay readable), an idle, walk and run playback rate of 0.93 to 1.07 so men do not swing in lockstep (blows keep their own timing), and a height of 0.97 to 1.03. Screen only; the fallen keep the look they had standing.
- Formation motion is *procedural*: soldiers step into gaps, jostle, and turn individually with small random delays so blocks look alive rather than rigid.
- **Soldier-on-soldier fighting** (asked for by Mario on 2026-10-13, inspired by Total War and Samurai Wars): every soldier in a fighting front picks an opponent and is seen to fight him, rather than a block of soldiers bobbing in place. It came in three stages, all on screen only (the battle maths never changes, see [10 · Architecture](10-architecture.md#the-two-tier-simulation)). **Superseded for what comes next (2026-10-23):** Mario chose the Every Soldier Fights plan, [28](28-soldier-combat.md), which moves the duels into the battle maths (option B), widens the fight to a scrum two or three men deep, and folds stage 3 below into its matched kills, charge impacts and fight library:
  1. **Duel pairs and living formations.** Each front-rank soldier locks onto the nearest enemy across the contact line; the two face each other, swing at offset times and drift a step forward or back as they trade blows. When one falls, a neighbour shuffles in to take his place and his partner. Formations get the small movements that make a block look alive: soldiers speed up and slow down instead of hitting full speed at once, a moving block's front sets off a moment before its back, a stopping block overshoots slightly and settles, and a waiting regiment shifts its weight, sways and looks about. Uses the clips we already bake. **Built** (see [10 · Architecture](10-architecture.md#the-two-tier-simulation)).
  2. **Blocks, hits and kills: duels that read the numbers** (Mario's pick MB-2, 2026-10-15). Each paired soldier swings once a round, and every blow is decided with the simulation's own odds, so the stats show without a tooltip. A blow lands with the sim's chance to hit (50 + attack − the target's defence with its formation bonus, kept between 10% and 90%, and lower for a wavering regiment or big blades in close order); a man it lands on staggers back and loses his next swing. Otherwise he parries it with his weapon or, with a shield, takes it on his shield, unless the striker is heavy (a monster, cavalry, or a big swinging blade, which counts one class heavier): each class of mass he has over his man gives a 25% chance to **crush through** the guard and knock him down. A heavy regiment's **charge** knocks men off their feet (40% for each class of mass over the foe) or staggers them. A knocked-down soldier falls, lies a moment and gets up (the death clip played forward, then back; models without one lie flat). When the simulation takes a soldier from a regiment in melee, the one who falls is a man locked in a duel, and **his partner swings the killing blow**: he stands until it lands. **Built** (see [10 · Architecture](10-architecture.md#the-two-tier-simulation)). Today's stand-in models stagger with their hit clip and duck to block (the Quaternius "Duck" clip, baked as the new `Block` clip kind); a shield-raise clip comes with the faction art kits.
  3. **Matched kills.** Paired animations where killer and victim move together (as in Medieval II), kept for heroes, charges, cavalry and the Challenge duel, where the camera is likely to be close. These need custom animation work. Now stage 5 of [28 · Every Soldier Fights](28-soldier-combat.md#build-stages), for ordinary soldiers as well, kept short.
- **Fidelity:** the CC0 models are a baseline. Mario wants high-fidelity, high-quality soldiers in the end. Nothing above depends on the model: pairing and motion move whatever skeleton they are given. Smooth skinning is built (engine upgrade step 1): each vertex follows up to four weighted bones, skeletons may have up to 63 bones (enough for a full humanoid rig with fingers), and vertices carry a texture coordinate and, when the source model has one, a tangent, ready for texture and normal maps. Textured soldiers are built too (step 2): a model may bring painted maps (albedo, team mask, normal, ambient occlusion, roughness, metalness, emitted light), which the baker shrinks to 512 × 512 with mips, and the soldier shader lights them with highlights that follow roughness. The first final-quality model is in: the Yari Ashigaru, made in Blender on Quaternius's Farmer body and rig (8,400 triangles, 1,024 maps baked from a 109,000-triangle sculpt; sources and scripts in the shared folder under `design/faction-looks/ashigaru-test/`). The whole Carrion Concord roster (14 models) is now built the same way: see [22 · Concord models](22-concord-models.md). So is the whole Shard Choir roster (11 crystal models with floating parts): see [23 · Choir models](23-choir-models.md). So is the whole Brood roster (15 models), though not on Quaternius bodies: every Brood creature has its own insect rig and clips made in its script, see [24 · Brood models](24-brood-models.md). Shadows and glow are built too (step 3): the sun casts soft shadows from soldiers, spears, banners, trees and hills, and the frame is drawn in HDR so the brightest light (sun glints on metal, emitted light such as Grub) glows. Four detail levels and BC7 texture compression are built too (engine upgrade step 4), so a textured material now costs about 1 MB in the pack rather than 4 MB. What is still to come is more final-quality models; from now on they are built by Opus at medium effort (Mario's call on 2026-10-16, after comparing the Ember Clans and Concord rosters side by side; see [15 · Staffing Plan](15-staffing-plan.md)). None of it touches the fighting or motion code. The extra memory is well inside the 4 GB budget.
- **Scenery** (trees, bushes, grass and rocks) is 26 vertex-coloured models, each with four levels of detail, placed by each land's own set (meadow, forest, fen, highland, waste and valley). It is decoration only and never touches the simulation. The models, their lands and the bake are in [25 · Scenery models](25-scenery-models.md).
- **The field remembers the fight** (Mount & Blade's lesson: the battlefield tells the story of the fight; built 2026-10-16). Deaths leave bodies that lie where they fell for the whole battle (they are also a Brood and Concord resource), in the death pose of their model (a flat dark box in the team's colour when far away). Only on a field already holding 32,768 of them does the oldest sink into the Titan, over three seconds, to make room; nobody sinks in a battle the size of the grand one (6,052 soldiers). Arrows that come down stay too: each arrow drawn for a volley that reaches the ground sticks in it at a steep slant, partly buried, with a paler fletching leaning back toward the archers, and an arrow that killed a soldier sticks in his body instead. The newest 24,576 stay; the oldest vanish first. The trees keep the arrows they catch (those just vanish when the volley lands), arrows that come down in water, or on a Conquest building, are lost, and a regiment wiped out leaves its last soldiers lying where they stood. Everything starts fresh with the next match, and a replay shows the same field, since it is drawn from what the simulation reports (`Loosed`, `Landed` and `Losses`). **Not yet:** arrows stuck in the shields of living shield-bearers, which means pinning an arrow to a soldier who then walks on; none of this touches the simulation.

### Effects
- Particles are GPU-instanced billboards from one shared atlas. Strict budget per effect.
- Signature effects: Grub splash, ember rockets, acid arcs, light beams that visibly split, spectral raise-dead swirls.

## Where art comes from (practical)

This is a personal project, so the art pipeline must be realistic:

1. **Prototype on free CC0 low-poly kits** (for example Quaternius and Kenney packs: low-poly, animated, permissively licensed). Their style matches the palette approach and they are fine for non-commercial use (CC0 is fine for any use).
2. **Blender as the source of truth.** A Python export script bakes meshes, palette indices and VAT animations straight into our formats.
3. **Procedural where possible:** terrain, foliage scattering, Grub seeps, chord triangles, veins, fortifications built from modular pieces.
4. **Custom models over time** for faction-defining units and heroes.

The vetted shortlist, licences, gaps and download status are in [16 · Art Sourcing](16-art-sourcing.md); credits are in [`CREDITS.md`](../CREDITS.md).

See [14 · Decisions Needed](14-decisions-needed.md) for the open question of who makes custom art.

## Audio direction

Audio carries a surprising amount of RTS readability: you hear a battle start, a worker idle, a building finish.

### Music
- One theme per faction plus neutral map themes; **adaptive layers** that add percussion and brass as battle intensity rises (measured from combat in view and supply lost per minute).
- Clans: taiko, shakuhachi, shamisen. Brood: wet percussion, clicking rhythms, droning choir. Choir: glass harmonica, bowed metal, pure tones. Concord: funeral brass, bells, harpsichord.
- Streamed from disk (see memory budget), never fully decoded in RAM.

### Sound effects
- Layered battle ambience: the sound of a clash scales with the number of models fighting, using a few looping layers rather than hundreds of individual sounds.
- **Voice budget:** at most 32 simultaneous voices, prioritised by distance to camera and importance.
- Positional audio relative to the camera's ground point: quieter, duller (the highs go first) and panned by where a sound is, so a distant battle is a low roar and a near one is clashing steel. Under it run ambient beds for the fighting, wind, water and woods (see Built so far).
- **Battle cries:** regiments shout in their faction's voice as they charge, break and rally, and when their champion wins the pre-battle duel: the Ember Clans' war shouts and kiai, the Brood's hisses, chitter and shrieks, the Shard Choir's ringing sung chords, the Carrion Concord's groans and bone rattle. Built from real recordings layered so a regiment sounds like many soldiers, with a per-regiment cooldown and a cap on cries at once.

### Unit voices
- Warcraft-style acknowledgements ("Hai!", chitinous chitters, a bell-tone, a ledger stamp), with the classic easter-egg lines after repeated clicking. Short, characterful, compressed.

### Built so far
**Art (M1 step 8):** soldiers are real animated models. `tools/gc_artbake` reads the glTF files listed in `assets-src/models.ron` (from the downloaded packs in the shared project folder, or small CC0 files kept in the repo), scales each to its height on the field, picks out the team-colour parts, makes a reduced mesh for distance, samples each animation at 12 frames a second into a bone animation texture, and writes `assets/models.pak`, which is embedded in the game, with `assets/models.manifest` beside it (the baker and model schema versions, a hash of `models.ron`, of every source file and of the pack, with the sources that live only in the shared folder marked as not in the repo). CI runs `cargo run -p gc_artbake -- --check`, which fails if the committed pack, `models.ron` or a source the repo holds no longer matches the manifest; it cannot look at the shared sources, so it reports how many it skipped, and a change to one shows only when someone with the shared folder re-bakes. After changing `models.ron`, a source or the baker, re-bake and commit the pack and manifest together. A test bakes the repo's CC0 Alien Bug in memory and checks that bone weights sum to 255, every level of detail is within its budget and two bakes are byte-identical. Today the Yari Ashigaru is a final-quality model made for Grubcraft (see Fidelity above), and the Ember Clans roster is being built the same way (Blender sources and scripts in the shared folder's `design/faction-looks/ember-roster/`): the Yumi Ashigaru, Katana Samurai, Hands, The Ronin, The Onmyoji, the Mounted Samurai, the Yabusame Riders, The Daimyo, the Spectral Samurai, the Fire Spirit, the Oni-Bound, the Battering Ram, the Ember Rocket Cart and the War Kite are in the pack (the whole roster), and so are the whole Carrion Concord roster (14 models, made the same way; see [22 · Concord models](22-concord-models.md)) and the whole Shard Choir roster (11 models: faceted glass gems fixed to bones, with heads, shards and crowns that float on their own bones; see [23 · Choir models](23-choir-models.md)) and the whole Brood roster (15 models, made for Grubcraft on their own insect rigs; see [24 · Brood models](24-brood-models.md)). The Yumi Ashigaru's bow clips are Quaternius's Ranger clips moved onto the Farmer rig, and a 63rd bone draws its string and shows the nocked arrow only while it aims (the bone shrinks to nothing otherwise). The Onmyoji's casting clips are Quaternius's Wizard clips moved the same way, and his 63rd bone turns the wheel of talismans behind his shoulders (it burns out as he dies). The three Ember riders sit on Quaternius's CC0 Horse (Ultimate Animated Animals): the rider's upper body and the horse share one skeleton (58 bones, 59 for the Yabusame's bowstring), the rider's legs are posed into the stirrups, and each riding clip pairs a horse clip (walk, gallop, rear, death) with a rider clip, the rider leaning into the gallop and slumping forward as the horse falls. The Spectral Samurai glow faintly all over; the baker keeps only a material's emitted colour and the game lights it at one strength, so their dim glow is written into the colour itself. The Fire Spirit, a kitsune of flame, is made entirely for Grubcraft on its own 29-bone rig with clips made in its script; its rest pose stands on the ground (the baker grounds every model by its lowest point) and each clip lifts it into a hover. The Oni-Bound is the Farmer body bulked into a giant's: every vertex is pushed out from the bones that move it, by a factor per bone, and the weight source gets the same push so its gear stays skinned to the bulk. The Battering Ram is built like the Fire Spirit, on its own rig (wheels, the slung log, lanterns, banners and the legs of four crew who walk with the wheels) with clips made in its script. The Ember Rocket Cart and the War Kite keep the Farmer as their crewman with his hands closed in a grip (the finger bones dropped, as for riders), and their clips hold his hands to the push bar or the kite's control bar with IK, baked into the keys. The War Kite, like the Fire Spirit, stands on the ground at rest and its clips lift it, since the game has no flying layer yet. Models for units not yet in `data/units.ron` are seen in the showroom (`--showroom`) and in the Unit viewer (main menu; [08](08-ui-ux.md#unit-viewer)), which lists every roster unit and shows "No 3D model" for those without one yet. The Brood models retired CDmir's Alien Bug as the Skitterlings' stand-in (the baker test still bakes that file in memory). Each plays idle, walk, run, two attacks, shoot, a hit reaction, a block (a duck; the Yari Ashigaru guards with his sword-ready idle instead) and death (where the model has them; a cheer clip is baked for later), and the fallen stay on the field in their death pose. Changing a unit's model is one line in `models.ron` and a re-bake. Credits are on the in-game credits screen (`C`).

**Sound:** the first sound pass is in the game. The sounds are made by `tools/gc_sfx` (see [assets/audio/README.md](../assets/audio/README.md) for the catalogue and how to regenerate): synthesised volleys, melee clashes, the war-drum charge, the wavering tone, the rout horn, the shatter crash, the rally horn, three UI clicks, and a calm and a battle music loop in the Ember Clans' style (flute, drone, taiko, plucked string). The two loops play in step and crossfade as regiments engage. Sounds follow the camera: quieter with distance, panned left or right. Keys: M mutes, N toggles the music; `--no-audio` skips the sound card. Faction themes and unit voices are still to do.

**Positional soundscape (stage 1, built 2026-10-23):** distance now also takes the highs off a sound (a one-pole low-pass per voice, from clear at the camera to muffled at the edge of earshot), so a far clash is dull and a near one is crisp. Four ambient **beds** (looping noise recipes in `gc_sfx`, 8 s each, no licences) play continuously under the one-shots and glide to the level, pan and muffling the game asks for: **wind** (always a breeze; stronger over open ground and when the camera is on a rise), **water** (from lakes, rivers and fords round the camera, panned towards them), **woods** (the same for cover of kind Wood) and the **battle roar** (every regiment in contact that the player can see adds to it by how near it is; a single far fight is a quiet, dull rumble, several near ones fill the bed, and it is panned towards the fighting). The ground is sampled every half second on a 25 by 25 grid out to about 45 tiles round the camera, weighted by nearness, and the beds fade as the camera pulls back and while the menu is up. Presentation only: nothing here touches the simulation, replays or the network. Nobody has listened to the new beds on real speakers; they were checked by their spectrum and levels, so the mix constants at the top of `crates/gc_game/src/sounds.rs` (`ROAR_*`, `WATER_FILL`, `WOODS_FILL`, `WIND_*`) are expected to want tuning by ear. Later stages (doc 29, wave 4): hit sounds by weapon and armour, weather, and per-land bed variants.

**Battle cries (built 2026-10-17):** regiments shout in their faction's voice when they charge (a moment before the blocks meet, and at contact if they haven't just done so), when they break, when they rally, and when their champion wins the duel between the lines. Each faction has four cries, 16 in all, 1.0 to 1.6 s each: the Ember Clans' are human war shouts and kiai, the Brood's insect and alien screams pitched up and clicking over a hiss, the Shard Choir's a men's chant pitched into a sung chord over real bells and struck glass, the Carrion Concord's undead moans and zombie voices pitched down over a rattle of bone. They are real recorded voices (CC0, plus one CC-BY warcry by spookymodem that the credits screen credits) found on OpenGameArt.org, kept in `assets-src/voices/`, and layered by `gc_sfx` with small offsets and detunes so one clip sounds like a whole regiment; the sources and licences are in [assets/audio/README.md](../assets/audio/README.md) and `assets-src/MANIFEST.md`. They are placed at the regiment like the other battle sounds (distance, panning, nothing from a regiment the player can't see), with a cooldown per regiment (10 s for a charge, 8 s for a rout or rally) and at most four cries at once. A regiment falling back, or braced spears that turned a charge back, give no charge cry, and nor does closing on an enemy the player can't see. The bank is now about 620 KB. Nobody has listened to them on real speakers yet; they were checked by their spectrum and levels only.

### Audio sourcing
The free-sound sites were not reachable when the first pass was made, so the prototype sounds are generated instead, which also means they carry no licence obligations. The Kenney CC0 packs are downloaded (`assets-src/kenney/`) and ready to audition. OpenGameArt.org is reachable now, and the battle cries are made from recordings found there (CC0, plus one CC-BY clip); each is in the table in `assets/audio/README.md`. Recorded CC0 or CC-BY sounds can replace any of the others later; a CC-BY one needs its author on the credits screen.

CC0 / Creative Commons effects libraries for prototyping, a couple of royalty-free music packs, then custom where it matters. Voice lines can be recorded by friends, which suits a personal game nicely.
